What does the research say?

How many sets does a beginner need?

Krieger (2010) pooled 55 effect sizes from 19 treatment groups across 8 studies and found that multiple sets per exercise produced a larger effect than a single set (difference = 0.10 +/- 0.04; CI: 0.02, 0.19; p = 0.016). Effect sizes rose with set count, 0.24 for 1 set, 0.34 for 2-3 sets and 0.44 for 4-6 sets, although 2-3 sets and 4-6 sets did not differ significantly from each other (p = 0.29). The paper's summary: multiple sets are associated with 40% greater hypertrophy-related effect sizes than 1 set, in both trained and untrained subjects.

Schoenfeld, Ogborn and Krieger (2017) looked at weekly volume per muscle across 34 treatment groups from 15 studies. Each additional weekly set was associated with an increase in effect size of 0.023, which the authors equated to a 0.37% larger gain, and the difference between higher and lower volumes was an effect size of 0.241, or a 3.9% difference in percentage gain. The three-band comparison (under 5, 5-9 and 10+ sets per muscle) showed a trend, P = 0.074, not a significant effect, while the continuous and higher-versus-lower analyses were significant. The authors concluded that volume and gains follow a graded dose-response relationship.

Strength matters too, because a beginner who gets stronger can load the leg press harder in the later weeks than in the first. Ralston and colleagues (2017) pooled nine studies and reported a mean effect size of 0.82 (95% CI 0.47-1.17) for low weekly sets against 1.01 (95% CI 0.70-1.32) for high weekly sets, and suggested that for novice and intermediate male trainees, low weekly sets produce smaller strength gains than medium or high weekly set training. The sample was male, so the finding is narrower than it sounds.

Iversen and colleagues (2021), in a narrative review written for people with limited training time, recommend a minimum of 4 weekly sets per muscle group in a 6 to 15 RM loading range,. The same review notes that untrained lifters may benefit from more weekly sets for the lower body than for the upper body, so treat 4 as the minimum, not the target. It also advises untrained individuals to rest 1 to 2 minutes between sets, and trained individuals 2 minutes or more. We build the week around those numbers below.

Why two sessions a week?

Schoenfeld, Ogborn and Krieger (2016) identified ten studies on training frequency and found that higher frequency carried a greater effect size than lower frequency (0.49 ± 0.08 vs. 0.30 ± 0.07). Their reading of the volume-equated evidence is that training a muscle twice a week promotes superior hypertrophy to once a week, that the major muscle groups should be trained at least twice a week, and that whether three times a week beats twice remains to be determined. The studies were not limited to beginners.

The Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans (2018) set a general-health floor of muscle-strengthening activity of moderate or greater intensity, involving all major muscle groups, on 2 or more days a week. Two leg days a week meet the twice-a-week frequency the hypertrophy data supports for the legs; they do not on their own cover all major muscle groups, so add upper-body work elsewhere in the week to meet that guideline.

Why an 8-week block? The papers above pool studies of different lengths, so the eight weeks are a planning choice, not a duration the studies prove. A fixed block gives a beginner leg hypertrophy program a start date, a finish date and enough sessions to compare your logged loads.

Do machines count, or do you need a barbell?

Haugen and colleagues (2023) pooled thirteen studies with 1016 participants (789 men, 219 women). Muscle hypertrophy was measured in only 5 of those studies, and in that subset free-weight and machine training showed no detectable difference (SMD: -0.055, CI: -0.397, 0.287, p = 0.751), a confidence interval wide enough to hold a real difference in either direction. Strength in free-weight tests did increase more with free-weight training (SMD: -0.210, CI: -0.391, -0.029, p = 0.023), which the authors read as strength being specific to the modality you train on. Their conclusion was that the choice between free weights and machines comes down to individual preferences and goals, and Iversen's review agrees.

A leg press, hack squat or leg curl machine fixes the path and lets a beginner spend attention on effort and rep quality instead of balance. A barbell squat teaches balance and transfers better to a squat strength test. On the current pooled data, a program that uses machines is not a lesser program for muscle size. What does the week look like?

Monday and Thursday leave recovery time after each session, and each muscle still gets trained twice in the week, the floor the frequency data supports. A beginner leg day built from the research covers these patterns:

  • One leg pressing pattern (squat, leg press or hack squat). Iversen's review asks for a minimum of one leg pressing exercise in any time-efficient program.
  • One hip hinge (Romanian deadlift, hip thrust or back extension) for the glutes and hamstrings.
  • One knee flexion movement (lying or seated leg curl) so the hamstrings get direct work.
  • One calf movement, standing or seated.

Work in the 6 to 15 RM range and rest 1 to 2 minutes between sets, the beginner rest interval from the Iversen review. With 2-3 sets per exercise and two sessions, a beginner's weekly set count per muscle clears the minimum of 4 weekly sets and, with 3 sets, sits inside the 5-9 band from the Schoenfeld volume analysis. That meets the minimum; add sets only as recovery allows. Krieger found no statistically significant difference between 2-3 and 4-6 sets per exercise, although effect sizes trended upward with set count, so starting at 2-3 sets and adding only as recovery allows is a reasonable choice.

How do you progress across the 8 weeks?

Use double progression. Pick a load you can lift for the bottom of the rep range with clean form. Each session, try to add a rep to each set. When every set reaches the top of the range, add load and drop back to the bottom. Keep the rest interval fixed. Log every set so you can see whether your loads and reps went up from week to week.

Add a set per exercise partway through the block only if you are recovering between sessions and last week's loads went up. The Schoenfeld dose-response work says each added set helps, by a 0.37% margin, and the authors read the overall pattern as graded. Stop sets short of failure on most work; Iversen's 15 to 40 repetition allowance applies only when sets are taken to volitional failure; this plan stays in the 6 to 15 RM range and stops short of failure.

If two dedicated leg days will not fit, the dumbbell-only full body workout two days a week keeps the same twice-a-week frequency per muscle with less equipment, and covers the upper body the activity guidelines ask for.

Who should not do this, and what should you check first?

Get clearance from a physician or physiotherapist before starting if any of the following applies: a heart condition or uncontrolled blood pressure, a recent surgery or injury to the hip, knee, ankle or lower back, pregnancy or recent delivery, a diagnosed condition that limits exercise tolerance, or any history of fainting during exertion. Sharp pain in a joint, as opposed to the burn of a working muscle, is a stop signal.

Checks before week one. Can you sit down to a chair and stand up with control and no pain? If not, start with a professional, not a program. Do you have the machines or free weights the sessions call for? The product page does not consolidate the equipment list, so read the lesson titles before you buy. Do you have a mirror, a phone video or a coach to check form?

Be clear about what the studies cover. Ralston's strength conclusion is about novice and intermediate male trainees. Haugen's pooled sample was mostly men, 789 of 1016, and only 5 of its 13 studies measured hypertrophy. The numbers are averages from mostly male samples; your own response will sit around them, not on them.

Frequently asked questions

How many sets per week do beginner legs need to grow?+

A practical floor is 4 working sets per muscle per week in a 6 to 15 rep range, from a 2021 review on time-efficient training, which also notes untrained lifters may need more sets for the legs. A 2017 meta-analysis concluded volume and gains follow a graded dose-response relationship; the three-band comparison was a trend (P = 0.074). Start at the minimum and add sets as recovery allows.

Is training legs twice a week enough for hypertrophy?+

Twice a week is the evidence-supported minimum. A 2016 meta-analysis of ten studies found higher training frequency had a larger effect size than lower frequency (0.49 versus 0.30) and concluded that, with volume equated, training a muscle twice a week promotes superior hypertrophy to once a week; whether three times beats two remains to be determined. The studies were not limited to beginners.

Are leg machines as good as squats for building muscle?+

For muscle size, a 2023 meta-analysis of thirteen studies (1016 participants in total; five of the studies measured muscle hypertrophy) found no detectable difference between free weights and machines, with a wide confidence interval. Free-weight training did produce larger gains on free-weight strength tests, so if you want a stronger barbell squat, squat with a barbell.

How long should a beginner rest between leg sets?+

A 2021 narrative review advises untrained lifters to rest 1 to 2 minutes between sets and trained lifters 2 minutes or more. Keep the interval the same from week to week, because resting longer makes a set easier and hides whether you got stronger. Set a timer rather than guessing, especially on leg press and squat sets.

Do two leg days meet the general activity guidelines?+

Not on their own. The 2018 federal guidelines recommend muscle-strengthening work for all major muscle groups on 2 or more days a week for general health, which leg days alone do not cover. Two leg days meet the twice-a-week frequency the hypertrophy data supports for the legs; add upper-body sessions elsewhere in the week to meet the guideline.

When should a beginner move on to the advanced leg program?+

After finishing the full 8-week beginner block, with a log showing loads went up across it, and once the pressing, hinge and leg curl patterns feel controlled under load. The advanced version lists the same price, the same 8 weeks and the same two sessions a week, so there is no saving in skipping ahead, and its curriculum uses harder single-leg exercises.
