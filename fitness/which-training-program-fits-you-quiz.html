By Purpose with Presence Fitness · Updated November 3, 2026 · free, runs in your browser

Which program should you buy?

The quiz below asks five questions and maps the answers to one program from our fitness program comparison, using only the facts the product pages state (length, sessions a week, equipment named, price as shown on November 3, 2026). It does not promise a result; it narrows the choice. Public-health guidance sets the floor that every option here clears: muscle-strengthening activity on 2 or more days a week, per the Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans.

Why constraints beat goals when choosing?

Most programs fail on schedule, not on design. Two sessions a week is enough to clear the public-health floor and, in the frequency meta-analysis, training a muscle twice a week outperformed once a week for growth (Schoenfeld et al. 2016), while a later analysis found no difference between frequencies once weekly volume was matched (Schoenfeld et al. 2019). So pick the number of days you will keep, then the equipment you own, then the goal. Our guides on the two-day dumbbell plan and the 15-minute plan go through the evidence for each.

Frequently asked questions

Does the quiz store my answers?+

No. It runs in your browser with no form submission, no cookie and no account. Nothing you select leaves the page. If you reload, the answers reset. The only link that leaves this site is the program link in the result, which is an affiliate link disclosed above it.

What if I own dumbbells and a gym membership?+

Answer with the equipment you will actually use on your busiest week. The two-day dumbbell program needs only dumbbells and a bench, so it survives travel and closed gyms. The gym-based programs name cables, machines and a pull-up bar in their exercise lists and are harder to run elsewhere.

Is two days a week really enough to build muscle?+

Two days a week meets the public-health floor for muscle-strengthening activity, and the frequency research found twice a week beat once a week for growth while more than twice made no clear difference once weekly volume was equal. The limiting factor is total weekly sets, which an eight-week two-day program can supply.

Why does the quiz send experienced lifters to the membership?+

Because the advanced programs, such as the advanced leg program and the hypertrophy total-body program, are included in the membership, and the quiz only links products we have read the pages for. The membership page lists thirty items; a lifter who will run two or more of them in a year pays less that way.

Can I use BFR without a gym?+

The BFR total-body program names a barbell, dumbbells, cables and machines across its sixteen workouts, so it assumes a gym. Cuffs alone do not make a program. If you own cuffs and dumbbells only, start with the dumbbell program and read our BFR beginner guide before adding cuffs to accessory work.
