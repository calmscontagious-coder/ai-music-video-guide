Holiday Gift Guide for Fitness: 10 Useful Ideas

Quick answer: This guide is for anyone gifting to a person who wants to train, from a first-timer to a regular lifter. The three best picks are a standing workout date, a set of resistance bands and a pair of adjustable dumbbells.

The CDC says adults need 150 minutes of moderate activity a week plus muscle-strengthening on two or more days (CDC adult activity guidelines), so the gifts here make that easier. Most cost little and come from any shop. Prices appear only where we read them on the seller's own page.

Ten gift ideas for fitness

1. A standing workout date

Free

Agree a weekly walk, run or session together and put it in both calendars. Exercise happens more reliably when someone is waiting.

Suits: Anyone who wants to move more and keeps skipping it. Check before buying: Pick a time you can keep, and a pace that suits the slower person.

2. A set of loop resistance bands

Low price

Light, cheap and easy to pack. Bands add resistance for warm-ups, glute work and travel workouts.

Suits: Beginners and people who travel. Check before buying: Several resistance levels in the set, and a fabric or latex choice if skin is sensitive.

3. A jump rope

A few dollars

A rope is a quick cardio tool for a small space.

Suits: Anyone with a bit of floor room and healthy joints. Check before buying: An adjustable length, and the surface they will use it on.

4. A foam roller or massage ball

A few dollars to low price

Many people use these after training. Choose a size that fits a bag.

Suits: Regular lifters and runners. Check before buying: Firmness: a softer roller is kinder for a beginner.

5. An insulated water bottle

Low price

A bottle they will carry to the gym or desk is used daily.

Suits: Anyone. Check before buying: Capacity, lid type and whether it fits their bag pocket or car cup holder.

7. Adjustable dumbbells

Mid to high price

One pair that replaces a rack. A good fit for a small home gym.

Suits: Someone who has decided to train at home. Check before buying: Weight range and step size, floor space, return policy and the weight they can lift now. See our adjustable dumbbell guide.

8. A gym day pass or class pack

Varies

A taste of a gym or a studio class with no long contract.

Suits: Someone curious about a place they have not tried. Check before buying: Expiry date, blackout days, and whether it needs a booking.

9. An exercise mat

Low to mid price

Floor work, stretching and yoga all go better on a mat that does not slide.

Suits: Anyone who trains at home. Check before buying: Thickness for knees and joints, and whether it rolls up small enough to store.

10. A workout log

A few dollars

A notebook to write sets, weights and how a session felt. It makes progress visible.

Suits: People who start strong and lose track. Check before buying: Ruled or gridded pages, and a size that fits a gym bag.

What not to buy

Skip weight-loss supplements, waist trainers and anything that promises to burn fat from one body part. Skip a bathroom scale unless they asked for one; it can feel like a comment. Skip blood-flow-restriction bands unless they asked: they carry real contraindications, covered in our BFR contraindications guide. Ask before buying for someone with a recent injury or a health condition.

This page is general information, not medical advice. Talk to a clinician before a new training program if you have a health condition.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good gift for someone starting to work out?

A workout date with you, a set of resistance bands, a jump rope or a beginner program. Choose something that lowers the effort of starting.

What can I give a gym-goer for under $20?

A jump rope, a foam roller or massage ball, a workout log or an insulated water bottle. Free: a weekly workout date.

How much exercise do adults need?

The CDC says adults need 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a week, or 75 minutes of vigorous activity, plus muscle-strengthening on two or more days a week.
