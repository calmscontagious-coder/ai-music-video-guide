Why does weight range matter for a muscle-building plan?

Muscle growth needs progression, so the top weight of a set decides how long it lasts you. Iversen and colleagues (2021), a narrative review, recommend a minimum of 4 weekly sets per muscle group using a 6 to 15 RM loading range, and say exercises can use machines or free weights based on goals, availability and personal preference.

Free weights are a sound choice for this. Haugen and colleagues (2023) pooled 13 studies with 1016 participants (hypertrophy in 5 of them). Strength in free-weight tests rose significantly more with free-weight training than with machines, while the direct comparison found no differences in strength, jump performance and muscle hypertrophy. We found no study comparing adjustable with fixed dumbbells, so we make no claim about that.

The ranges differ a lot. A Bowflex SelectTech 552 runs 5 to 52.5 lbs per dumbbell. The Bowflex 1090 runs 10 to 90 lbs. PowerBlock Elite USA 90 starts lifting at 5 to 50 lbs and grows to 90 lbs with 20 lb expansion kits. Ironmaster lists 5 to 75 lbs standard, with a 120 lb kit and a custom 165 lb add-on kit. A Core Home Fitness set stops at 50 lbs each.

What do increment size, mechanism and changeover time change?

Bowflex says its sets change in 2.5 lb or 5 lb increments, and the 1090 lists 5 lb steps. PowerBlock micro-adjusts by 2.5 or 5 pounds. Core adjusts in 5 lb increments.

The mechanism shapes how you switch, but only Ironmaster has a measured change time in our sources. The other speeds below are maker claims, not timings. Bowflex says you change weight with a quick twist of the dials. REP says you change weight in 10lb increments with the flip of a switch, with 2.5lb micro-adjustment plates for finer steps. PowerBlock uses a magnetic pin plus a dial lock lever. Ironmaster has you turn a locking screw, stack plates and lock it again; Garage-Gyms.com, in a review from 2020 that does not state its test duration in the text we read, says it still takes about 10 to 15 seconds per handle even when you get good at it.

How much drop tolerance, handle length and footprint do you need?

Drop tolerance matters if you train tired, and we would not call any of these sets safe to drop. Home Gym Advice says that like every twist-mechanism dumbbell the NÜOBELL is not built to be dropped, and that the people who report trouble are almost always the ones who twisted the handle while the dumbbell was half-lifted, or set it down hard off-square. Garage Gym Lab says it confirmed that with its own drop testing, and also says NÜOBELLs have more plastic components. Owner comments gathered by GYIBB say Bowflex 552 owners must never drop or slam them because of the plastic construction. Bowflex says its steel plates are coated in polypropylene and that premium, durable metal components attach the plates to the handle. REP's warranty excludes "Any damage to any part resulting from abnormal/abusive use (consistent drops)". Ironmaster describes chrome-plated steel handles and solid cast iron plates with no plastic or breakable parts; it makes no drop claim.

Handle length decides your pressing and curling range. GYIBB says Bowflex 552 owners find it frustrating that the dumbbells are as long at light weights as at heavy ones. LiftQube reports that NÜO lists a 32 mm handle diameter and a short 105 mm handle length, so a 10 lb NÜOBELL is the same length as an 80 lb one. The Core set starts at 9.3 in long at 5 lbs and grows with the load, and the Ironmaster measured 9.5 in at 25 lbs in the 2020 review, growing to 14.5 in at 75 lbs.

Footprint matters in an apartment. PowerBlock's compare table lists 16.25 in x 6 in x 6 in for the Elite USA 90. The Bowflex 552 is 16.9 in L x 8.3 in W x 9 in H per dumbbell, and the 1090 is 17.5 in L x 9.5 in W x 10 in H. Bowflex says a stand is optional but recommended.

Bowflex lists 2 years on parts for the 552 and 3 years for the 1090. PowerBlock lists a 5 year residential warranty, not valid for commercial use. Ironmaster and REP list lifetime warranties. Core lists 2 years on parts.

Which adjustable dumbbells suit which buyer?

Best for a first set at entry tier. The Core set (5 to 50 lbs, 5 lb steps, twist handle) and the Yes4All spin-lock sets are the entry-tier options by features. Our evidence is thin: one maker page for Core, one retailer page for Yes4All, no owner reports.

Best for long-term progression. Ironmaster lists the widest range in our table, from 5 to 75 lbs standard to a custom 165 lb add-on kit, and GYIBB reports owners keeping them 7 to 12+ years. PowerBlock Elite USA 90 and the Bowflex 1090 both reach 90 lbs; PowerBlock owners quoted by Horton Barbell and GummySearch report over a decade and over 15 years, and GYIBB quotes some Bowflex 552 owners at 10 to 17 years.

Best for changing weights often. Ironmaster is the only set with a measured time in our sources, 10 to 15 seconds per handle (Garage-Gyms, 2020). Bowflex and REP describe their dials and switch as quick, but we have no timing for them or the NÜOBELL twist handle, so we do not rank them. REP's trade-off is the 10lb step unless you add micro plates. Bowflex's trade-off is the durability reports above.

Best for a metal-built set. Ironmaster describes steel handles, cast iron plates and a lifetime warranty, and PowerBlock lists a 5 year residential warranty. Long-term reports exist for several brands, from 7 to 12+ years for Ironmaster and over a decade for PowerBlock to 8 to 17 years for some Bowflex 552 owners, and failures are reported too.

Best for a small space. PowerBlock Elite USA 90 is one of the more compact at 90 lb capacity, at 16.25 in x 6 in x 6 in. The NÜOBELL stays about 17 in long at every weight. The Core starts at 9.3 in at 5 lbs. Ironmaster is 9.5 in at 25 lbs and 14.5 in at 75 lbs.

How do these fit a 2-day dumbbell plan?

A dumbbell set is only half of the plan. Schoenfeld and colleagues (2016), a meta-analysis of ten studies, concluded that training a muscle group twice a week promotes superior hypertrophic outcomes to once a week, on a volume-equated basis. A later meta-analysis by Schoenfeld, Grgic and Krieger (2019), covering 25 studies, found no significant difference between higher and lower frequency on a volume-equated basis. The two reviews do not agree, and Iversen and colleagues say weekly volume is more important than training frequency. So a 2-day plan is reasonable if its weekly sets are adequate, and the set you buy has to cover 2 sessions a week for the length of that plan.

Frequently asked questions

Do adjustable dumbbells build muscle as well as other free weights?+

We found no study comparing adjustable with fixed dumbbells. A 2023 meta-analysis of 13 studies and 1016 participants (hypertrophy in 5) found no differences in muscle hypertrophy between free-weight and machine training. What you can lift, and how often you progress, matters more than the mechanism.

How much weight range do I need?+

Enough to keep your main lifts in a 6 to 15 RM range as you get stronger, per a 2021 narrative review. A set capped at 50 lbs each suits a true beginner. If you expect to outgrow it, look at sets reaching 90 lbs or more.

Can I drop adjustable dumbbells?+

None of them is safe to drop. Reviewers say NÜOBELL is not built to be dropped, and Bowflex 552 owners warn against slamming them. REP's warranty excludes abusive use such as consistent drops. Ironmaster describes steel handles and cast iron plates with no plastic, but makes no drop claim.

Is the Bowflex 552 recall still a concern?+

Owner comments mention a recall of older 552 units, and we have no details beyond that. The maker's FAQ says units currently available for purchase are not involved in any recalls. Check the model and date of any used set before you buy.

How many days a week should I train with them?+

A 2016 meta-analysis found twice a week beat once a week for hypertrophy on a volume-equated basis. A 2019 meta-analysis of 25 studies found no significant difference between frequencies on a volume-equated basis. The reviews do not agree; a 2021 review says weekly volume matters more. Two sessions with enough sets is reasonable.
