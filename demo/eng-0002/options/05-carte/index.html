    From La Maison de Marc Veyrat to West Green Street

    Romuald Guiot is a fourth-generation pastry chef. He trained at the three-Michelin-star La Maison de Marc Veyrat, opened La Mirabelle in New York, worked at Pitchoun and Porto’s, and reached the final of Food Network’s Spring Baking Championship, season 8. Everything in the case is plant-based, and the chef says guests are often surprised to learn it.

    The day’s leftovers go to Friends In Deed, the Pasadena charity the shop has supported since the Eaton Fire.

    Answers first

    Is everything plant-based?

    Yes. Every pastry, cake, jar and drink in the shop is 100% plant-based, croissants included, and the new store lists the nine major allergens plus coconut on every item.

    Can I order tonight and pick up tomorrow?

    Yes. Tap tomorrow in the pickup strip and choose a time.

    How far ahead do I order a custom cake?

    A week, which is the shop’s own rule. For a wedding, ask early: the chef answers with the dates he can take.

    Are there macaron classes in Pasadena?

    Yes: the Macaron Masterclass with Chef Romy, Tuesdays from 3:30 to 5:30 pm at 169 W Green St. A seat is $125 and you take home 20 to 30 macarons.

    What is La Cave?

    The shop’s vegan wine bar next door at 163 W Green St, open Wednesday to Sunday from 3 to 9 pm. It pours organic, biodynamic and vegan wines with small plates, Rebel Cheese boards and plated desserts.

    Where do I park?

    The city’s De Lacey garage at 45 S. De Lacey Ave charges $1 for the first two hours, then $2 an hour, per Old Pasadena’s parking page. Street meters in the core cost $1.25 an hour.

    Do you deliver?

    Through Uber Eats, DoorDash and Grubhub, which add their own fees. Pickup at the counter costs the menu price and nothing more.
