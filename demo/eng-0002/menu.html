How ordering works in the new store.

Three steps on a phone, one checkout, one pickup time. The bag on this preview already behaves this way, and live-store items check out for real today.

  1. Pick a day and a time.

    The bag opens on the next 14 days and the 15-minute slots that still have room, with the earliest chosen for you. A full slot closes itself, and anything that needs notice says when it can be ready instead of disappearing.

    Plan, sections 4.1 and 4.3; references research, finding 1 (Cedric Grolet’s day-first page); mobile ordering research, finding 41.

  2. Fill one bag.

    Éclairs for Saturday, the love-note jars, a gift that ships and a class seat share one cart and one checkout with Apple Pay, Google Pay or Shop Pay. One order carries one pickup time and takes the longest notice of anything in it. The price on the menu is the price at checkout; no online-ordering fee appears at the end.

    Plan, sections 4.1 and 4.3; operations research, finding 22 (California’s Honest Pricing Law).

  3. Say your name at the counter.

    Staff tap Ready for pickup and you get the message, with orders shelved by slot. In the Shop app the order shows its status and a QR code to scan, so the receipt is already on your phone.

    Shopify research, finding 28; loyalty and messaging addendum, finding 7.

  • One checkout for the counter and the web

    With Shopify POS at the counter, walk-in sales and web orders share one list of orders, customers and stock. Staff tap Ready for pickup, the customer is told, and a printed QR slip finds the order.

    The pickup app (Bird, $49.99 a month) sets prep time per item, caps each slot and closes full and closed days. The customer chooses in the bag, and a checkout validation refuses any order that skips the slot, Shop Pay included.

    Shopify research, finding 6; mobile ordering research, finding 36; plan, section 4.2.

  • Shop Pay and the Shop app

    Saved details for one-tap checkout, a Buy again button on past orders, and the pickup order in the Shop app with its status and a QR code once staff mark it ready.

  • Stock that matches the case

    Daily pastries count down as they sell, online and at the counter. At five left the menu says Only a few left; at zero it says Sold out today, so the site never sells a pastry the case no longer has.

    Sold online, spent at the counter, and kept in Apple Wallet with the balance and a QR code staff scan. California’s rule is built into the terms: cash back on balances under $15.

Questions about ordering.

The short answer first, then where to go next.

Can I order ahead for pickup at 169 W Green St?

Yes. Choose a day and a pickup time and the order is boxed for then, to collect at the counter. Today counter items order through the shop’s menu app and packaged goods through id-eclair.com, where pickup is usually ready in 24 hours; the new store puts both in one bag.

Is everything on the menu vegan?

Yes. The whole case is plant-based: the croissants are laminated with plant butter, the latte is made with plant milk, and the éclairs, macarons and cakes contain no animal products. More on the chef’s story page.

Which pastries are gluten-free?

The live store lists the cannelés, the assorted macarons, the four love-note jars, the truffles, the chocolate fingers and the cookie spoons as gluten-free. They come from a kitchen that also bakes with wheat, so ask at the counter if you need a strict choice.

Can I get iD Éclair delivered?

Yes, through Uber Eats, DoorDash and Grubhub today. The apps add their own fees, and Grubhub notes that prices may be lower in store; pickup on the new site carries the menu price with no fee added at the end.

What can be shipped?

Shelf-stable boxes: truffles, jams, biscuits, the chocolate bar and the gift baskets, which the new store plans to mail once the chef confirms. Fresh pastry stays pickup only, and the live store offers pickup today. See the goods that ship or the merchandise line.
