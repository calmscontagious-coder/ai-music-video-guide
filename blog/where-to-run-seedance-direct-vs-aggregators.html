Where to Run Seedance: Direct vs Aggregators, Sourced and Compared

By the AI Music Video GuideUpdated November 3, 20268 min read

Quick answer: Run Seedance on Dreamina, where ByteDance debuted version 2.5, on an API host like fal.ai if you can make API calls, or on a reseller if you want a browser tool. Prices differ by platform, and most published comparisons come from vendors, so check rates yourself.

What counts as an aggregator for Seedance?

In this article, an aggregator is any third-party site that sells Seedance access instead of sending you to Dreamina, where ByteDance debuted version 2.5. The notes we read name Higgsfield, fal.ai, Wireflow, Flora and Seedance2ai.online, and they differ in price, interface and how clearly they publish rates.

The label covers more than one kind of service. Some are API hosts. Higgsfield's guide calls fal.ai the cheapest way to run Seedance 2.0 for anyone who can make an API call (Higgsfield, read October 22, 2026), and Wireflow's pricing comparison calls fal.ai the largest Seedance host (Wireflow, read October 22, 2026). Others are browser tools. Seedance2ai.online describes itself as an independent Seedance 2.0 platform that runs in any browser: upload or describe, set parameters, generate (Yahoo Finance, February 26, 2026).

Then there is the first-party route. A PRNewswire release dated Aug. 3, 2026 says ByteDance announced the global launch of Seedance 2.5, debuting first on Dreamina on July 31, 2026 (VIR, August 3, 2026). That makes Dreamina the place where new versions appear first, at least for 2.5.

Why does the label matter? Because each host sets its own pricing and publishes it with different clarity. Wireflow says Flora publishes no first-party per-clip Seedance rate, and that because consumption depends on input duration as well as output, you cannot compute what a job will cost before you run it (Wireflow, read October 22, 2026). A flat per-second rate, by contrast, can be multiplied by clip length before you commit.

Can you skip aggregators and use Seedance directly?

Yes, according to one video. A speaker says you can connect directly to the Seedance API, which means "you don't even have to use aggregators" (YouTube, read October 22, 2026). The catch is skill: Higgsfield's comparison ties fal.ai's low price to knowing how to make an API call (Higgsfield, read October 22, 2026).

The source is a YouTube video titled Seedance 2.5 vs FREE Local AI Video model. The relevant stretch is a few lines of auto-generated transcript, so treat the wording with care (the captions render Seedance as seed dance and sit dance). The speaker also describes a chart comparing cost per clip and render time across three setups: local, server, and paying Seedance directly (YouTube, read October 22, 2026). The excerpt we have does not include the chart's numbers, so we can't report them.

Two things are not in our notes. The video's free local option is a separate model, per its title, and we found nothing showing Seedance itself running on a home machine. The notes also say nothing about what direct API access costs per second, so we won't guess.

What direct access asks of you

A direct connection likely asks for the same comfort with code that fal.ai does. It suits people who can script a request, not people who want to drag in a song and press a button. If that is you, our guide to AI video APIs covers the basics. If access itself is the obstacle, read how to access Seedance in the US.

How much does Seedance cost on different hosts?

Higgsfield's comparison puts fal.ai at $0.022 per second on Fast, about $0.22 for a 10-second clip, with no monthly subscription (Higgsfield, read October 22, 2026). Wireflow lists $0.52 per second at 1080p against fal.ai's $0.682, roughly 24 percent less (Wireflow, read October 22, 2026).

These two rates are not a like-for-like pair. The first is a Fast-tier rate for Seedance 2.0 and the second is a 1080p rate, and the notes do not say whether both apply to the same model version or tier, so we wouldn't subtract one from the other. Wireflow's comparison covers 8 platforms, but it is published by one of them (Wireflow, read October 22, 2026).

What do you get beyond price when you pick a host?

Features vary by version. Higgsfield says it runs Seedance 2.0 with up to 9 image references plus video clips and audio files in one call (Higgsfield, read October 22, 2026). Evolink says Seedance 2.5 supports up to 30 seconds in one pass and as many as 30 image references (Evolink, read October 22, 2026).

Seedance 2.5 or 2.0?

Evolink's migration guide says to choose 2.5 for longer, reference-heavy, edit-heavy work and to keep 2.0 for shorter jobs where an established baseline, predictable retries or lower production complexity matters more. It lists 2.5 as supporting up to 30 seconds in one pass, up to 30 image, 10 video and 10 audio references, reference-to-video control and timestamp-level editing (Evolink, read October 22, 2026). For a full-length song you would still be stitching clips, which our longer-clips guide covers.

What the 2.5 launch added on Dreamina

The launch release says creators can use Seedance 2.5 on Dreamina to generate up to 30 seconds of continuous video in standard mode. It frames the benefit as avoiding the stitching of several short clips whose timing, camera movement or style may not match (VIR, August 3, 2026). That is a vendor's framing, but the problem is real for music videos where a look has to hold across a chorus.

Audio and lip sync, the part musicians care about

Higgsfield says its Seedance 2.0 setup produces sound, dialogue and lip sync in 8+ languages alongside the visual rather than adding them in post (Higgsfield, read October 22, 2026). Whether another host exposes the same audio inputs is not something our notes settle. If you plan to feed in your own track, read our Seedance lip sync guide and what to do when reference audio fails before you pick.

One more gap: our notes do not say which hosts offer 2.5 and which still offer only 2.0, and the Higgsfield figures above refer to 2.0. Check the model name on the generate screen before you pay. Our versions explainer and Seedance 2.5 access guide track the differences.

Which Seedance route fits a musician making music videos?

If you want a hosted tool and no code, start with Dreamina or a reseller. If you can make API calls and want per-second billing with no subscription, an API host like fal.ai is the route the sources describe. Pick by workflow first, then check the rate.

The short answer to where to run Seedance depends on how you work:

  • No code, one place to start: Dreamina or a browser-based reseller. Seedance2ai.online's own release lists no watermark and commercial rights on all Seedance Pro tiers (Yahoo Finance, February 26, 2026), which is a claim to verify in its terms, not a guarantee.
  • Comfortable with code, paying per second: an API host such as fal.ai, at $0.022 per second on Fast with no monthly subscription (Higgsfield, read October 22, 2026).
  • Newest version first: Dreamina, where 2.5 debuted on July 31, 2026 (VIR, August 3, 2026).
  • Avoiding a Seedance bill altogether: the video's free local option is a different model. Our Wan 3.0 comparison looks at a free alternative.

How reliable are these comparisons?

Treat them as vendor material, ours included, since we earn a commission on one host. Higgsfield's guide describes its own platform, Wireflow's comparison finds Wireflow cheapest, and Seedance2ai.online issued its own press release (Yahoo Finance, February 26, 2026). Use them as dated claims, not independent tests.

Wireflow says its rates are the lowest published per-clip rates of the platforms it compared (Wireflow, read October 22, 2026). That may be true of the page's snapshot, but prices change, and a ranking written by a competitor is a starting point for your own check. Before you generate anything, confirm these on the host's own pricing page:

  • Which model version the rate covers. The Higgsfield figures refer to Seedance 2.0, and 2.5 is the newer release.
  • Whether the rate is per second, per clip or per credit. Wireflow publishes per tier and per second, while Flora publishes no first-party per-clip Seedance rate (Wireflow, read October 22, 2026).
  • The resolution. Wireflow's two figures above are quoted at 1080p.
  • Whether there is a subscription. fal.ai has none, per Higgsfield (Higgsfield, read October 22, 2026).
  • Watermark and commercial rights, which Seedance2ai.online claims for its Pro tiers but which you should read in the terms.
