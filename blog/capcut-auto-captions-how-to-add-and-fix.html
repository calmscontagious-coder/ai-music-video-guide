How do I add auto captions in CapCut?

The route is the same idea on every version. These steps are from CapCut's auto captions help page.

Phone app:

  1. Open the app and load your project.
  2. Tap Captions in the bottom menu.
  3. Choose the language spoken in the video, pick a caption template, and start generation.
  4. Wait for processing, then preview the captions.

Desktop app:

  1. Import your video and place it on the timeline.
  2. Go to Captions, then Auto Captions.
  3. Select the correct language and confirm the settings.
  4. Click Generate and wait for the captions to appear.

Web editor:

  1. Open the project in the web editor and select your clip.
  2. Click Captions, then Auto Captions.
  3. Choose the language and click Generate.

After that, treat the captions as a draft. CapCut's caption tool page says you can edit the text, add or delete lines, and change the font, colour, effects and motion. Read every line against the audio once. Check names, places and prices with extra care.

Are CapCut auto captions free?

CapCut's own pages give two answers. Its caption tool pages describe automatic captions as free of charge. Its help page on upgrade prompts names auto captions as a feature that includes Pro-only functionality, and its plan comparison lists auto captions among Pro's advanced AI tools.

What that means in practice: the export screen decides. If the captions you generated use something Pro-marked, CapCut asks for a subscription when you export. So test before you commit:

  1. Cut a short clip with speech in it.
  2. Generate captions and pick the style you want to use.
  3. Export it. If it saves without a prompt, that style works on your plan.

If you caption every video you post, a subscription removes the guesswork. You can see what CapCut Pro costs; Pro lists 1,200 credits a month for AI features and 1 TB of storage alongside the paid tools, and new subscribers in supported regions get a 7-day trial, which is long enough to caption a real batch of videos and judge the result.

How do I make bilingual captions?

CapCut can generate captions and translate them in one pass. From its bilingual subtitles page:

  • Phone app: tap Captions, then Auto Captions, choose the recognition language, then pick the language you want under Translate to.
  • Desktop app: click Captions, select Auto Captions, choose the target language, and click Generate.
  • Web editor: upload the video and select Captions in the left sidebar.

A translated caption is machine output. If the second language matters to your audience, ask a fluent speaker to read it before it goes out under your name.

Why are my auto captions not working?

CapCut lists five causes. It says the feature relies on speech recognition and cloud processing, so most failures trace back to the sound or the connection.

CauseWhat to do
Unclear audio or background noiseMake sure the speech is clear and audible
Unsupported or wrong language settingChoose the correct language for the video
Weak or unstable connectionCheck the connection and generate again
Muted or missing audio trackCheck the clip has sound and the volume is not too low
Old app version or a glitchUpdate the app, or refresh the browser page

For the web editor, CapCut recommends Chrome or Edge, and suggests refreshing the page if generation fails. On desktop, check the connection and restart the app if nothing happens after you click Generate. On a phone, update the app from the App Store or Google Play.

How should a musician caption a song?

CapCut says auto captions rely on speech recognition, so treat what it produces from a sung vocal as a draft. Paste your real lyric sheet next to the editor and check each line against it.

Timing is the part worth keeping. CapCut says generated captions are synced with playback, which saves placing each line by hand even when you retype the words. For a full lyric video, see our guide to making a lyric video, and for the release plan around it, CapCut for musicians.

How should a local business use captions?

Caption anything where a person talks: a walk-through of the shop, a quick answer to a common customer question, a short thank-you after a busy weekend. Captions carry the message for anyone watching with the sound off.

Keep each caption short, check the spelling of your business name, your street and your services, and put the caption block where the app's own buttons will not cover it. Our first-month video plan for a small business shows where captions fit.
