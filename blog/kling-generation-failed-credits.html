Kling Generation Failed: Do You Get Your Credits Back?

By the AI Music Video Guide team · Updated July 31, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer: On Kling's web app, a failed generation usually still costs you the credits. Kling's official credits policy says credits are non-refundable and can't be redeemed for cash, and there is no documented automatic refund when a generation errors out. The one clear exception is the API, where failed tasks reportedly don't deduct credits. If a system error ate your credits, email support@klingai.com with the task ID, your account email, and a screenshot of the error — restoration is case-by-case, not guaranteed. And be honest with yourself about what "failed" means: an error with no output is a support case; a video you simply don't like is not.

What actually happens to your credits when a Kling generation fails?

Start with the official line. Kling's published credits policy states that credits are non-refundable and cannot be exchanged for cash. It covers pricing, validity periods, and usage order — but as of July 2026 it does not spell out what happens when a generation fails mid-process. That silence matters: there is no policy sentence you can point to that promises your credits back.

Third-party analysis fills in the observed behavior. A detailed pricing breakdown from eesel (updated June 2026) puts it bluntly: failed consumer-side generations still consume credits, and there is no publicly documented auto-refund policy. The same analysis notes the flip side — failed API tasks do not deduct credits — which makes the API materially cheaper for anyone whose workflow involves experimental prompts with a lower success rate.

Then there are the community reports, which you should weigh as anecdote, not policy. In a May 2026 Adobe Community thread, a user running Kling 3.0 inside Adobe Firefly lost 750 credits across two generations that never produced a video. A community expert replied that credits deducted at generate time "should be restored" on failure — but that's Firefly's credit system talking, not Kling's, and the thread ends with no confirmed refund. It's a useful case study precisely because it shows the gap between "should be restored" and "was restored."

What counts as "failed" — and what doesn't?

Before you email support, sort your situation into one of three buckets, because they get treated very differently:

  • Hard failure. You got an explicit error message, or the job ended with no output ever delivered. This is the only bucket where a credit-restoration request has a real chance. One caution before you declare failure: a job still sitting in the queue isn't failed yet — community reporting on stuck Kling generations attributes long stalls to peak-time server load, with free-tier queue waits reportedly stretching from hours up to a couple of days at the worst times (Segmind). Slow is not failed; errored-out or never-delivered is. Once it genuinely fails, save the task ID and screenshot the error immediately.
  • Moderation rejection. Your prompt or reference image tripped a content filter. Guides to Kling's failure modes list policy-adjacent prompts — violence, political content, adult content — as a common cause of blocked generations (Dreamlux troubleshooting guide). Many AI video platforms also restrict prompts involving real people's likenesses, so treat celebrity or real-person prompts as high-risk even where the published rules don't spell it out. Platforms generally treat filter trips as your error, not theirs. Don't expect credits back, and don't keep re-rolling the same flagged prompt — repeated attempts at blocked content can escalate to account restrictions.
  • Disliked output. The generation completed and you hate it — wrong mood, mangled hands, drifting character. No platform refunds taste. This is the expensive bucket for musicians, because a music video needs 20+ usable shots and every re-roll of a technically-successful-but-unusable clip is full price. How do you request credits back from Kling?

There's no one-click "refund failed generation" button in the web app that we could verify as of July 2026. The route is manual support contact:

  1. Collect evidence first. Task ID, date and time, your account email, a screenshot of the error state, and a screenshot of your credit ledger showing the deduction.
  2. Email support@klingai.com. Per a 2026 walkthrough of Kling's refund process, use a clear subject line, include your transaction details, and expect a 5–14 business day individual review.
  3. Keep the ask narrow. You're asking for credits from specific failed tasks to be restored — not a subscription refund. Those are different requests with different odds: the same walkthrough notes that payments are "non-refundable once activated" with narrow exceptions (billing errors, duplicate charges), and that refund chances collapse once you've actively used credits.
  4. If you paid through an app store, subscription disputes go through Apple or Google, not Kling — another reason to keep credit-restoration and money-refund requests separate.

If you're weighing a full cancellation instead, we've covered that decision separately in our guide to cancelling AI video subscriptions — the mechanics differ by platform but the evidence-gathering step is universal.

How do you reduce your failure rate in the first place?

Support tickets are the last resort. Cheaper: fail less. Community troubleshooting guides for Kling converge on a few patterns — Dreamlux covers the prompt-side causes, and Segmind's breakdown of stuck generations covers the queue side. Treat these as general guidance rather than official documentation:

  • Overloaded prompts fail more. A prompt juggling three subjects, five camera moves, and a lighting change is a failure magnet on any model. One subject, one action, one camera instruction per generation is the reliable pattern — our library of camera movement prompts for AI music videos is built on exactly that principle.
  • Strip policy-adjacent language. Words that read as violent, political, or sexual — even metaphorically, which is a real hazard when you're prompting from lyrics — raise block rates. Rephrase mood in visual terms.
  • Don't hammer the queue. During peak load, jobs stall — Segmind's analysis of generations stuck at 99% points to server overload at busy hours, with free-tier waits reportedly running hours or longer, and recommends generating during off-peak times. Waiting resolves more stuck generations than immediate re-rolls, which just spend more credits into the same congestion.
  • Retry once, then change something. If the identical prompt fails twice, the third identical attempt is a donation. Simplify the prompt or swap the reference image before spending again.

Frequently asked questions

Does Kling automatically refund credits when a generation fails?+

No — not on the web app. Kling's official credits policy calls credits non-refundable and doesn't document any auto-refund for failed generations, and third-party reporting through mid-2026 consistently finds that failed consumer-side generations still consume credits. The API behaves differently: failed API tasks reportedly don't deduct credits. For web-app failures, your route is emailing support@klingai.com with the task ID and evidence; restoration is case-by-case.

What should I include when contacting Kling support about lost credits?+

The task ID of the failed generation, the date and time, your account email, a screenshot of the error state, and a screenshot of your credit balance showing the deduction. Keep the request narrow — ask for the specific failed tasks' credits to be restored rather than a general refund. Reviews are individual and reportedly take roughly 5–14 business days.

Does a video I don't like count as a failed generation?+

No. A failed generation means you got an error or no output was ever delivered — and a job that's merely sitting in a slow peak-time queue isn't failed yet either, since community reports document waits running hours or more before completion. If the video rendered but the result is unusable — wrong mood, warped faces, character drift — that's a completed generation and no platform refunds it. That distinction is where most credit money is actually lost in music-video work.

Do Kling credits expire?+

Yes. Per third-party pricing analysis current to June 2026, monthly subscription credits don't roll over — they expire at the end of each billing cycle. Separately purchased add-on credit packs last longer, reportedly two years from purchase. This makes failure losses sting more: credits eaten by errors were already on a monthly countdown.

Is the Kling API really cheaper if my prompts fail a lot?+

For failure-heavy workflows, reportedly yes: failed API tasks don't deduct credits, while failed web-app generations do. If you're comfortable with API workflows and your experimental prompts have a low success rate, that asymmetry is a real cost advantage. Verify against your own task logs, since this reflects observed behavior documented by third parties rather than a detailed official policy statement.

Should I switch platforms because of failed generations?+

Only if the math says so. Calculate your effective cost per usable clip — total credits spent divided by keepers. If failures and unusable takes push that well above sticker price on volume work, a platform with smaller per-generation stakes (like Seedance's 4–15 second segments, from $9.90/month as of July 2026) reduces what each retry costs. If your Kling failure rate is low and its longer extension workflow suits your videos, staying is the right call.
