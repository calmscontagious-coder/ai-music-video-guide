How to Cancel Your Seedance Subscription (and When You Actually Should)

Quick answer: To cancel a Seedance subscription, log in on the site that bills you, open your account or profile menu, go to the subscription/billing section (one Seedance-branded site calls it "Subscription Management"), and click cancel — or email the support address on that site's refund policy page. If you subscribed through an iOS app, cancel via iPhone Settings → your name → Subscriptions instead; the website can't do it for you. Cancelling stops the next renewal but usually keeps your access until the end of the paid period, and on at least one Seedance billing site your unused credits survive cancellation. Check the refund window first: you may be able to get money back instead of just stopping future charges.

Yes, we usually profit when people sign up for Seedance — and we're writing a cancel guide anyway, because the honest version of this decision ("cancel, keep your credits, come back when your next single is mastered") is what we'd tell a friend. Here's the full picture.

Step one: figure out who is actually billing you

This is the part most cancel guides skip. "Seedance" is the model name (built by ByteDance), but several differently-branded websites and iOS apps sell subscriptions to it — each with its own billing system and its own cancellation policy. Before you do anything, check your card statement or the receipt email from your original signup and note the exact domain or app name that charged you. Cancelling on the wrong site does nothing, and support for site A cannot touch a subscription billed by site B — Apple says the same about subscriptions billed outside the App Store.

If you're wondering whether the site that billed you is even legitimate, we dug into that separately in our "is Seedance legit" investigation.

How do you cancel on the website?

The exact menu labels vary by billing site, so treat this as the general path rather than a pixel-perfect walkthrough:

  1. Log in to the site that charges you.
  2. Open your account, profile, or avatar menu (usually top right).
  3. Look for Subscription, Billing, Manage subscription, or Subscription Management. One Seedance-branded site's refund policy names it exactly that: cancel "by navigating to the Subscription Management section within your account settings" or by emailing their support address.
  4. Click cancel and complete any confirmation screens. Screenshot the confirmation.

Two timing details worth knowing, as of July 2026:

  • Some sites want notice. At least one Seedance-branded service requires cancelling at least seven days before your renewal date to avoid being charged for the next cycle. Don't cancel the night before renewal and assume you're safe.
  • Cancellation stops renewal, not access. The seeddance.video refund policy states monthly subscriptions "will not auto-renew after cancellation" (source) — meaning you typically keep your plan until the end of the period you already paid for.

If you can't find any cancel button, email the support address listed on that site's refund policy or contact page and request cancellation in writing, including your account email. Keep the email as proof.

What if you subscribed through an iOS app?

App Store subscriptions are billed by Apple, and only Apple can cancel them. On your iPhone: open Settings, tap your name, tap Subscriptions, select the Seedance app, and tap Cancel Subscription — the subscription then runs out at the end of the current billing period (Apple Support). The same page covers doing it from a Mac or the web if you no longer have the device.

What happens to your remaining credits when you cancel?

Honest answer: it depends on which billing surface you're on, and one of the two policies we could verify doesn't address it at all.

  • One Seedance-branded site is explicit and generous: "even if you cancel your PRO membership, you'll still have access to all the credits you received while you were subscribed," and unused credits "roll over into the next month" and never expire.
  • The seeddance.video refund policy covers refund windows but is silent on what happens to leftover credits after cancellation (source).

Where a policy is silent, plan for the worst case: spend your remaining credits before the paid period ends, and download every video you've generated before you cancel. If you have credits to burn and a song sitting around, that's a free excuse to build a full video — our song-to-video walkthrough shows how far a leftover credit balance goes when you chain 15-second generations with Video Extension.

Can you get a refund instead of just cancelling?

Sometimes — but the windows are short and usage kills eligibility. On seeddance.video, as of July 2026: subscriptions are refundable within 14 days of purchase if credits have not been used, credit packages within 7 days if unused, and "credits that have been used to generate videos" are non-refundable, with refund requests handled by email (source). In plain terms: generate one clip and your refund case mostly evaporates. If you subscribed in the last two weeks and haven't rendered anything, ask for a refund first and cancel second. Other Seedance-branded sites publish different terms — at least one says it generally offers no refunds at all for completed purchases or active subscriptions — so read the refund policy on the exact domain that billed you.

When is cancelling the right call?

Cancel without guilt if any of these fit:

  • Your video is done and nothing's scheduled. Seedance is a project tool, not a utility bill. No release in the next 60 days means no reason to pay this month.
  • You're on Max or ProMax and barely touching your credits. Downgrade or cancel; re-upgrade for the next big project.
  • You've switched tools. If you need long continuous takes, Kling 3.0 extends to roughly three minutes versus Seedance's 15-second generations chained by extension — the tradeoffs are in our Kling vs Seedance comparison.
  • You only ever needed one video. One month was the correct purchase. Cancelling isn't a failure; it's the plan working.
  • You're just experimenting. The free tier's single watermarked daily credit is enough for prompt testing — but not for anything you'd publish, since it isn't commercially licensed. Details in our free credits guide.

Two cases where cancelling right now is the wrong move: you're mid-project (extension chains eat credits fast, and re-subscribing mid-edit is friction you don't need), or you're inside a refund window with unused credits — request the refund instead.

When should you re-subscribe — and how do you time it?

The efficient pattern: cancel immediately after you finish a project. Your access runs to the end of the paid period anyway, so there's nothing to gain by waiting — and everything to lose if you forget before renewal. Then re-subscribe the day your next single is mastered and you're ready to storyboard, not before; your monthly credits start counting down from the moment you're billed, so subscribing "to get ready" wastes days of the cycle. When that day comes, Seedance's plans start at $9.90/month as of July 2026, and it's worth checking which promo codes actually work before you check out. Frequently asked questions

Do I lose my credits if I cancel my Seedance subscription?+

It depends on which Seedance-branded site bills you. At least one states that you keep all credits earned while subscribed and that unused credits roll over and never expire. Others don't address it in their refund policies. The safe play: download all your videos and spend remaining credits before your paid period ends, and check the credits/FAQ page on the exact domain that charges you.

Can I get a refund after cancelling Seedance?+

Possibly, if you act fast and haven't used credits. As of July 2026, seeddance.video's policy allows subscription refunds within 14 days of purchase if credits are unused, and credit-package refunds within 7 days if unused. Credits already spent on video generations are non-refundable. Other Seedance-branded sites publish different terms — at least one states it generally does not offer refunds for completed purchases or active subscriptions at all — so read the refund policy on the site that billed you.

Does cancelling Seedance take effect immediately?+

No. Cancelling stops the next auto-renewal, but you typically keep access until the end of the billing period you already paid for. Note that at least one Seedance-branded site requires cancellation at least seven days before your renewal date to avoid being charged for the next cycle, so don't cut it close.

How do I cancel Seedance if I subscribed through the iOS app?+

App Store subscriptions are billed by Apple, so cancel through Apple: open Settings on your iPhone, tap your name, tap Subscriptions, select the Seedance app, and tap Cancel Subscription. The subscription ends at the close of the current billing period. The website's support team cannot cancel an Apple-billed subscription for you.

Can I still use Seedance after cancelling?+

Yes, on the free tier — but it's minimal: one credit per 24 hours, videos are watermarked, and free-tier output is not commercially licensed, so it's unusable for an actual music video release. It's fine for testing prompts and styles between projects, then re-subscribing when you have a song ready to shoot.

Is downgrading to the $9.90 Basic plan better than cancelling?+

Usually not. Parking on Basic costs $118.80 a year just to keep the account warm, and on billing sites where credits survive cancellation there's nothing to protect. For most musicians releasing a few videos a year, fully cancelling and re-subscribing only for project months is cheaper. Basic parking only makes sense if your billing site expires credits on cancel and you're certain you'll return soon.
