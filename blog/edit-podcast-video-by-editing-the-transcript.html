Edit a Musician's Podcast Video by Editing the Transcript

Most musicians who start a podcast already own the hard part: a microphone, a room and something to say. The part that eats weekends is the edit, and most of us are not video editors.

With a transcript editor the software transcribes the recording, you read it like a script, and the cuts you make in the words are made in the picture and sound underneath. Every figure below comes from a page we read on November 2, 2026, and where a page was silent we say so.

How does editing video by editing text work for a podcast?

You upload or record the episode, the software transcribes it, and the transcript becomes the timeline. Delete a rambling answer in the text and the matching video and audio are cut with it (Descript homepage, read November 2, 2026). Descript's plans list transcription in 25 languages with multitrack and 8+ speakers (Descript pricing page, read November 2, 2026).

Descript's own homepage puts it as "Edit your words to edit your video", and its FAQ says deleting a word deletes that moment of video. You read the conversation the way you would read lyrics, cut the false starts, tighten the gap where the guitar got retuned, and the picture follows. No scrubbing, no razor tool.

The recording can come from an upload, a recording made inside the app or a Rooms session, and all of those count against media time (Descript Help, media minutes and AI credits article, read November 2, 2026).

The honest limit is that this is a spoken-word tool. Descript's pricing page talks about transcription, speakers, AI speech, Studio Sound and stock voices. It says nothing about mixing a band or mastering a song. If your show has a music segment, finish that part elsewhere, bring the final mix in as a file, and keep it on a separate track so a transcript cut never slices through a chorus.

What the transcript cannot do: cutaways and visuals

When you edit video by editing text you remove words; it does not create the shots you cut to while someone is talking: the rehearsal room, the gear on the desk, a street outside the venue. For those you still need footage, shot on a phone or generated.

What do media minutes and AI credits count, and why do they run out?

Media minutes are charged on everything you upload or record, including Rooms sessions and screen recordings, and still images count as 1 second each. AI credits are spent by features such as Studio Sound and filler-word removal, each capped at 30 credits per file. Neither rolls over (Descript Help, media minutes and AI credits article, read November 2, 2026).

The help article defines media minutes as "the time you use when uploading or recording media". Treat the length of what you upload or record as what you spend, and plan the month from raw recording time. Rooms sessions are charged by session length rather than per participant, so a 1-hour session with 5 participants uses 60 media minutes (Descript Help, media minutes and AI credits article, read November 2, 2026).

AI credits are a second meter, spent by Underlord, Studio Sound, Remove filler words, Green Screen, Eye Contact, AI speech, Avatars and generated video. Chatting with Underlord uses a few credits per message, Underlord costs vary by request, and the Usage tab shows each run's cost (Descript Help, media minutes and AI credits article, read November 2, 2026).

Studio Sound and Remove Filler Words "have a max cost of 30 credits per file/tool application", which is the most either of those two tools costs per file, so it is the ceiling to budget against (Descript Help, media minutes and AI credits article, read November 2, 2026).

Why the two meters surprise people

  • No rollover on either. The article says "Unused Media minutes don't roll over month to month" and the same for AI credits. Allowances reset when the subscription renews (Descript Help, media minutes and AI credits article, read November 2, 2026).
  • Plan from raw length. The meter counts time uploaded or recorded, so extra takes add to it.
  • Top-ups are one-time purchases shared across the Drive and bought from the Usage tab. Their prices are on a separate Descript Help article about top-ups, so we do not quote them here (Descript Help, media minutes and AI credits article, read November 2, 2026).

Reading the table as a podcaster

Media time is the number to match to your show: Hobbyist allows 10 hours of media time a month and Creator allows 30. Add up the hours you record in a month, including retakes and Rooms sessions, and compare that to the plan. Free's 720p export carries a watermark, so we would not deliver an episode from it (Descript pricing page, read November 2, 2026).

Voice cloning is the other line to read carefully. The Free plan lists custom voice clones as "Limited", Hobbyist lists them as included, and the page uses the terms "AI Speech" and "custom voice clones" rather than a product name. It does not give a numeric clone allowance on any plan, so we cannot tell you how many voices you get or how much AI speech you can generate before the credits run out (Descript pricing page, read November 2, 2026).

How does billing work if you upgrade mid-month or run two shows?

Plans are drive-specific, so each drive carries its own plan and its own bill. Payments are taken upfront at the start of the billing cycle. An upgrade takes effect immediately and is prorated, while a downgrade waits until the current cycle ends (Descript Help, billing and plans overview, read November 2, 2026).

The drive rule matters if you keep projects separate. A band podcast and a solo studio vlog in two drives means two plans, each billed separately (Descript Help, billing and plans overview, read November 2, 2026). Creator lists a team of up to 3, billed separately, and Business a team of up to 5; Hobbyist lists 1 person (Descript pricing page, read November 2, 2026).

What export settings does a video podcast feed need?

For a video podcast on Spotify, the recommended file is an MP4 at 1080p or higher, H.264 or H.265, 16:9, with AAC-LC stereo audio at 128 Kbps or higher. Episodes under 4 hours are recommended and up to 12 hours are compatible (Spotify for Creators video specs, read November 2, 2026).

This is where the plan choice becomes a delivery question. The Hobbyist local export is 1080p without a watermark (Descript pricing page, read November 2, 2026), and the Spotify spec asks for "1080p or higher" while accepting "180p or higher" with a note that it encourages the highest resolution available (Spotify for Creators video specs, read November 2, 2026).

A Free plan export is 720p, which sits under the recommendation and carries a watermark. We would not publish a watermarked episode on a show we were trying to grow.

More of the spec

  • Container and codec: MP4 (MOV also compatible; "We discourage FLV and AVI"), H.264 or H.265 (HEVC). Frame rate "24, 25, 30, 50, or 60 FPS".
  • Audio: AAC-LC, PCM or FLAC at "128 Kbps or higher", stereo; mono is upmixed, 5.1 downmixed.
  • Size and length: "Less than 10 GB" recommended, "Up to 60 GB" compatible; "Less than 4 hours" recommended, "Up to 12 hours" compatible. One video track and one audio track of the same duration.

The items above are from the Spotify for Creators video specs page, which shows only "2026 Spotify AB" as a date.

One caution on length: the Descript pricing page caps web link exports at 1 hour on Free and Hobbyist and 3 hours on Creator, and lists no length cap on local export, so for a long episode plan on a local file rather than a shared link (Descript pricing page, read November 2, 2026). Can you fix a flubbed line with a voice clone, and must you disclose it?

Descript's pricing page (read November 2, 2026) lists "AI Speech" and custom voice clones, so a flubbed word may be replaceable with AI speech instead of re-recording. YouTube's rules exempt "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs" from disclosure; the exemption names only your voice (YouTube Help, disclosing altered or synthetic content, read November 2, 2026).

The YouTube page draws the line at realistic content. Disclosure is required for content that "Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do", "Alters footage of a real event or place", "Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur" or "Creates music that's the main focus of the video". It is not required for non-realistic AI content or for edits that are "primarily aesthetic" (YouTube Help, disclosing altered or synthetic content, read November 2, 2026).

When it is needed, you select "Yes" under "AI use" in YouTube Studio. A label may appear in the video player for AI music or photorealistic content, or in the expanded description for other AI audio or animated content, and YouTube may apply labels automatically. The page says disclosure alone doesn't affect audience reach or monetization eligibility (YouTube Help, disclosing altered or synthetic content, read November 2, 2026).

Frequently asked questions

What counts as media minutes when I upload a podcast recording?

Media minutes are the time you use when uploading or recording media. That includes video, audio, Rooms sessions, and screen, webcam or microphone recordings, and still images count as 1 second each. Because the meter counts time uploaded or recorded, plan from the length of the raw recording.

Do unused media minutes or AI credits roll over to the next month?

No. The help article states that unused media minutes don't roll over month to month, and the same applies to unused AI credits. Both allowances reset when the subscription renews at the start of each billing cycle. If you need more in a given month, top-ups are one-time purchases shared across the drive, bought from the Usage tab.

Can the free plan export an episode for a video podcast feed?

It can export, but at 720p with a watermark, and only 60 minutes of media time a month. Spotify's video spec recommends 1080p or higher and accepts lower resolutions, so the file would be accepted, but it sits under the recommendation and carries a watermark. The first plan with 1080p export and no watermark is the Hobbyist tier.

Do I have to tell YouTube if I clone my own voice to fix a line?

Not for your own voice. YouTube's disclosure page lists cloning one's own voice to create voiceovers or dubs among the uses that need no disclosure. You do need to disclose realistic content that makes a real person appear to say something they didn't, or AI music that is the main focus of the video, by selecting Yes under AI use in YouTube Studio.

Can a transcript editor mix the music segments of my show?

The pricing and help pages we read describe transcription, speaker handling, AI speech, Studio Sound and stock voices. They say nothing about mixing or mastering music, so we would not plan on it. Finish the music elsewhere, bring the final mix in as a file, and keep it on a separate track so a transcript cut never slices through a song.

What happens to my bill if I upgrade in the middle of the month?

Upgrades take effect immediately and are prorated. Downgrades wait until the current billing cycle ends. Switching from monthly to annual applies immediately, while annual to monthly waits for the next cycle. Payments are processed upfront at the beginning of each cycle, and plans are drive-specific, so a second drive for a second show is billed separately.
