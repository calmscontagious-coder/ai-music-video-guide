Seedance Refunds and Failed Generations: What You Get Back

By the AI Music Video Guide team · Updated October 21, 2026 · 12 min read

This post contains affiliate links — if you sign up through them we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you (disclosure). Researched and edited for accuracy with AI assistance.

Quick answer: On seeddance.video, a failed generation gets its credits back automatically, according to its Seedance 2.0 and 2.5 generator pages. Its refund policy covers money only: 14 days for an unused subscription, 7 days for an unused pack, nothing once credits are used. Pay-as-you-go APIs such as fal.ai and BytePlus bill only successful outputs.

The Seedance refund question sounds simple. It is not, because the model is one thing and the company charging your card is another. This guide reads the policy pages as they stood on October 21, 2026, and tells you what each one gives back.

One disclosure first. seeddance.video, the site our links go to, says in its terms and on its refund page that it is an independent platform, not ByteDance and not the official Seedance product. Our investigation into which Seedance sites are official covers what that means for you.

Two ground rules. A render that completes and looks wrong is not a failure to any vendor we checked. And Seedance-branded sites change their policies without notice, so we date every quote.

Do credits come back when a Seedance generation fails?

Yes on seeddance.video, as long as the job ends in an error rather than a finished clip. Its Seedance 2.5 page says "Generation Failed? Credits are refunded automatically", and its Seedance 2.0 page says "Failed renders auto-refund". The official BytePlus API skips the refund step entirely, because it never bills a failed job.

The caveat is where the promise lives. It sits on the generator pages, not in the formal policy. The seeddance.video refund policy, read October 21, 2026, covers money rather than credit returns: it lists "credits that have been used to generate videos" as non-refundable and says nothing about failed renders. The terms of service, last updated January 2025, add that the site does "not guarantee specific video or image quality". So screenshot that generator page line with your balance before a long session.

If a deduction does not come back, the money-back fallback is the pricing page FAQ. Under "Can I get a refund?" it says refunds are issued for "billing mistakes, verified platform failures that prevented delivery, or accidental duplicate charges", and that approved refunds return to the original payment method within 5 to 10 business days. Write in with the order number, the render time, and screenshots of the error and your balance.

At the source, ByteDance's own API is the cleanest. The BytePlus ModelArk pricing page, last updated October 19, 2026, states: "You are only charged for successfully generated videos. No fee is charged if generation fails due to reasons such as content moderation."

What do you get back when you cancel a Seedance subscription?

On seeddance.video, cancelling stops the next renewal and keeps your credits usable until the current billing period ends. Money comes back only inside a window: 14 days from purchase for a subscription with no credits used, 7 days for an unused credit pack. Used credits, promotional bonus credits and anything bought more than 6 months ago are not refundable.

The wording matters because the annual plans issue every credit up front. Basic at $9.90 a month on the annual toggle, the pricing page's default view, hands you 1,800 credits on day one. If you generate ten clips in the first week and cancel in the second, you have used credits from a plan you now want refunded. The policy says refunds are "available for unused subscription time" and makes "credits have not been used" the condition. In practice an annual plan is refundable only if you have rendered nothing.

The gentler half of the policy gets less attention. Monthly subscriptions "will not auto-renew after cancellation", and the pricing FAQ says unused credits "stay live until the end of the current billing period". Unused monthly credits also roll forward indefinitely while the subscription is active, so a quiet month is not a wasted month. For the click-by-click path, including iOS purchases that the website cannot cancel, see our guide to cancelling a Seedance subscription.

One more line people miss: "purchases made more than 6 months ago" are non-refundable regardless of use. A pack bought in March and never opened keeps its credits for roughly ten years, but the money is not coming back.

How do you claim a refund or missing credits?

First check whether the credits came back on their own, since seeddance.video promises an automatic return for failed generations. If they did not, email support at the site that billed you with the order ID, the render time and a screenshot, inside the refund window. App Store purchases go to Apple, not to the website.

  1. Check your balance and My Creations first. A render that shows as failed sometimes completes later, and Runway says credits from a generation error reappear within a few minutes. Give any platform that long before you write in.
  2. Capture evidence. Screenshot the error, note the time, the model and settings, and your credit balance before and after.
  3. Find the billing domain. Check your card statement or receipt email. Support for one Seedance site cannot touch another site's charges, and the same model name is sold by several companies.
  4. Write one short email. A Seedance refund request on seeddance.video uses the subject "Refund Request - [Your Account Email]" and includes your account email, order ID and the reason. Ask for a specific remedy: credits restored or the charge reversed. The policy promises a review within 5 business days; card refunds then take 5 to 10 business days and PayPal 1 to 3.
  5. Diary the dates. The 14-day and 7-day windows run from the purchase date, not from the day the render failed.
  6. iOS purchases go through Apple. Sign in at reportaproblem.apple.com, choose "Request a refund", pick the reason and the item, then wait 24 to 48 hours for an update. The website that runs the app cannot process an App Store charge.
  7. Know your statutory rights. Across the EU, a service bought online carries a 14-day cooling-off period that starts the day the contract is agreed, and you do not have to give a reason, as the European Commission's Your Europe guide to returns explains. In Ireland that right sits in the Consumer Rights Act 2022, and the CCPC's guide to selling digital content or services sets the limits: you pay a reasonable cost for anything used before cancelling, and the right is gone once a service is fully provided and you agreed to lose it. Elsewhere, a card dispute is the usual backstop once support has had its 5 business days.

How do other AI video tools treat failed generations?

Most published policies now return credits for a true technical failure and keep them for a completed render you dislike. Kling, Runway, Hailuo and seeddance.video promise returns for failures in writing, while Dreamina and CapCut only say purchased credits cannot be refunded. The pay-as-you-go APIs, fal.ai, Replicate and BytePlus, simply do not bill a failed job.

Which pay-as-you-go route wastes the least on failed renders?

On failed renders they now tie: fal.ai, Replicate and BytePlus skip the bill, and seeddance.video promises the credits back. The gap is the price per clip. A 5-second Seedance 2.5 clip at 720p costs about $2.37 on fal.ai, against 115 credits on seeddance.video, which is roughly $5.75 to $13.80 from a one-time pack.

The arithmetic, all from pages read on October 21, 2026. The fal.ai Seedance 2.5 page lists roughly $0.2205 per second at 480p and $0.4730 per second at 720p. That puts 5 seconds at about $1.10 at 480p and about $2.37 at 720p. The fal.ai FAQ says server errors (HTTP 500+) are never charged, with one caveat: an invalid-input error (HTTP 422) can still cost money if a GPU spent time on it before the error was caught. Replicate's billing page says that for all models a failed run is not charged, but a run you cancel on an official model may still be billed.

On seeddance.video, the Seedance 2.5 generator charges 12 credits per second at 480p, 23 at 720p and 48 at 1080p. The form's default of 60 credits is a 5-second clip at 480p, and the same clip at 720p is 115 credits. One-time packs run from 250 credits for $29.90 ($0.120 per credit) to 3,000 credits for $149.90 ($0.050 per credit). Compared like with like, fal.ai charges about $1.10 at 480p against $3.00 to $7.20 from a pack, and about $2.37 at 720p against $5.75 to $13.80. seeddance.video is the more expensive route per clip, and we would rather say so here than have you find it on your statement.

Annual plans narrow the gap without closing it. Basic works out at $0.066 per credit, which puts a 5-second 480p Seedance 2.5 clip near $3.96, and Pro Max at $0.040 per credit brings the same 480p clip to $2.40. At 720p those become about $7.59 and $4.60. The pack cards say "up to 50 videos" for 500 credits; that figure assumes the cheapest engine on the site, not Seedance 2.5, where 500 credits buys about 8 clips at 480p or 4 at 720p.

We left Seedance 2.0 out of the price comparison because its own page contradicts itself. The generator form showed 40 credits for a default 5-second clip, while the page copy says "A 5-second 480p take costs 100 credits" at 20 credits per second. Trust the number next to the Generate button.

So the ranking, now that a failed render costs nothing on all four routes, comes down to price per clip and moderation handling. fal.ai is the cheapest per clip of the routes we priced, if you can live with a developer console. BytePlus gives you the official rate for those comfortable with token math, and it is the clearest on moderation, billing nothing even when a filter blocks the job. seeddance.video costs the most per clip and is silent on moderation blocks. It earns its place on convenience: a browser, references that mix images, video and audio in one prompt, music and voice tools in the same wallet, and packs that last about ten years.

One trap applies everywhere. The CometAPI pricing guide, updated September 3, 2026, warns that a polling timeout is not proof that generation failed, and resubmitting a job that is still running can create a second billable task. Our pay-as-you-go comparison covers the non-API options.

What counts as a failed generation, and what is just a bad one?

A failure ends with an error and no file, while a bad result is a finished file you do not want. Every policy we read refunds the first and keeps the second, and Runway's help page says it plainly: credits power the generation process itself, not the quality of the output. Moderation refusals fall in between and vary by vendor.

The moderation gap is the one that catches music video makers, because album art, lyrics and performer likeness can trip filters. BytePlus charges nothing for a moderation failure. Hailuo refunds credits when content does not pass review. The seedancev2ai.com refund policy, dated April 2026, returns credits after a "platform-side technical error" but says renders rejected by content moderation are not refunded. seeddance.video promises refunds for failed generations without saying whether a moderation block counts. If your concept sits near a filter, test it on a platform that refunds moderation failures before you spend a pack on it.

Five habits cut the waste on every platform. Test at 5 seconds and 480p before you commit to 30 seconds and 720p; on per-second pricing a 5-second test costs a sixth of a 30-second render at the same resolution. Change one variable per retry, or three failures teach you nothing. Never resubmit while a job is pending. Screenshot your balance before a session so you can prove a deduction. And if several deductions fail to come back, send support one email listing every affected job with its time and order ID.

Frequently asked questions

Does Seedance refund credits for a failed video?+

On seeddance.video, yes. Its Seedance 2.0 and 2.5 generator pages, read October 21, 2026, promise that credits for a failed generation are refunded automatically. The formal refund policy does not mention failures, so screenshot the generator page line. If a deduction does not return, the pricing FAQ offers money back for verified platform failures by email. ByteDance's BytePlus API never charges for failures.

Can I get my money back if I cancel Seedance after a week?+

On seeddance.video, only if you have not used any credits. The refund policy allows subscription refunds within 14 days of purchase when credits are unused, and credit pack refunds within 7 days when unused. After that, cancelling stops the next renewal and your remaining credits stay usable until the billing period ends, but no money is returned.

Why did my credits disappear after a render that never finished?+

Credits are usually deducted when a job starts, so a failed job leaves a gap until the platform reverses it. Runway says returned credits reappear within a few minutes, and seeddance.video promises the return happens automatically. On a site with no written rule, such as Dreamina, there is nothing to point to. Check your creations list, then send support the order ID, timestamp and a screenshot.

Does the official Seedance API charge for failed generations?+

No. The BytePlus ModelArk pricing page, updated October 19, 2026, says you are only charged for successfully generated videos and that no fee applies when generation fails, including failures caused by content moderation. Resellers of the same model on fal.ai and Replicate also skip the bill on server-side failures, though fal.ai may charge for invalid-input errors that consumed GPU time.

Is a video I do not like a failed generation?+

No vendor we read treats it that way. Runway's help page says credits power the generation process, not the quality of the output, and a finished video that ignores your prompt is consumed. Every failure clause we read, from Kling to Hailuo to seeddance.video, covers jobs that end in an error. Reduce the risk by testing at 5 seconds and 480p and changing one variable per retry.

Which Seedance site do refunds come from?+

The one that charged your card, which is not always the one you think. Several companies sell the Seedance model under similar names, each with its own refund rules and support address. Check the domain on your receipt before you write in. If you paid through an iOS app, the request goes to Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com, not to the website.
