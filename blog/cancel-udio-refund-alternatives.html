Udio Locked Your Songs: How to Cancel, Get a Refund, and Where to Go Next

By the AI Music Video GuideUpdated August 29, 20266 min read

Quick answer: To cancel Udio, log in on the web, open udio.com/account, and click Cancel subscription — it takes effect at the end of your current billing cycle, and there is no prorated refund for unused time. Downloads remain disabled after the Universal Music Group settlement: the 48-hour rescue window closed on November 5, 2025, so any song you didn't export is now streamable on Udio only. Refunds are case-by-case through support@udio.com, and most departing users rebuild on Suno for pure generation or on Seedance when they want music and video under one login.

What did the UMG settlement actually change?

The settlement, announced October 29, 2025, ended Universal Music Group's copyright lawsuit and turned Udio into a stream-only service: downloading of audio, video, and stems was switched off within a day. A one-time 48-hour rescue window ran November 3–5, 2025, and a fully licensed relaunch is planned for 2026 — with no export option announced.

The financial terms were not disclosed, so be wary of anyone quoting figures. The change users felt was the export shutdown, which converted a tool people used for release-ready WAVs into a walled garden. According to Billboard's reporting, the backlash was strong enough that the company opened the 48-hour download window starting November 3, 2025, so users could rescue existing work before exports closed again.

As partial compensation, the company granted every user 1,000 non-expiring bonus credits and raised monthly allowances — Standard moved from 1,200 to 2,400 credits and Pro from 4,800 to 6,000, according to the official help-center notice. Warner's settlement followed in mid-November, with Sony Music's suit still pending at that time (Source: Chat GPT Is Eating the World, Nov 19, 2025). None of that restores exports — which is why cancellation threads keep filling up.

How do you cancel a Udio subscription?

Log in at udio.com in a browser, open udio.com/account, click Cancel subscription, and confirm. Cancellation takes effect at the end of your current billing cycle, you keep full access until then, and your account and on-platform library stay intact. If Apple bills you, cancel in your iPhone's Subscriptions settings instead.

If you subscribed on the web (most users), the flow is short:

  1. Log in at udio.com in a browser — not the mobile app.
  2. Go to udio.com/account.
  3. Click Cancel subscription and confirm.
  4. Screenshot the confirmation screen and keep the confirmation email. You'll want proof if you later dispute a charge.

Per the help center, cancellation is effective at the end of your monthly or yearly cycle, and you keep full access until then. Two gotchas worth knowing:

  • App-store billing: if your receipts come from Apple rather than Udio (you subscribed inside the iOS app), the subscription is managed by Apple. Cancel it in Settings → your name → Subscriptions on your iPhone — canceling on the website will not stop an Apple-billed renewal, and Apple-billed refunds go through reportaproblem.apple.com, not Udio.
  • Canceling is not deleting. Account deletion is a separate, permanent action. Since your un-exported songs now exist only on the platform's servers, do not delete your account if you ever want to stream those tracks or benefit from any future export policy.

Will Udio refund your subscription?

Usually not automatically: Udio's cancellation policy is end-of-cycle access with no proration, and its public help documentation makes no refund promises. User reports describe refund requests — including from annual subscribers who paid shortly before the export lockdown — being declined or handled case-by-case (Source: Trustpilot reviews of udio.com).

That said, a specific, polite request beats an angry one. Email support@udio.com or use the messenger on help.udio.com with something like:

"I subscribed on [date] primarily for the download/export feature, which was removed on October 30, 2025. Because a core paid feature changed after purchase, I'm requesting a prorated refund for the unused portion of my [annual/monthly] plan. My account email is [email]."

Escalation order: (1) Udio support with the wording above; (2) for Apple-billed plans, Apple's refund request flow; (3) a card dispute as a genuine last resort — card issuers do sometimes side with customers when a paid feature was removed mid-term, but a chargeback will very likely end your Udio account, taking your streamable library with it. Weigh that before you file.

Your existing songs: what you still own

Good news for anyone who exported in time. Songs created before the UMG agreement and downloaded during the window are owned by their creators under Udio's original terms, including commercial-use rights — a point the company confirmed publicly when it opened the 48-hour window, per Digital Music News. Practical steps:

  • Back up your pre-lockdown files twice (local drive plus cloud). There is no re-download if you lose them.
  • Keep provenance records: creation dates, subscription receipts, and screenshots of your Udio library. Distributors increasingly ask about AI provenance, and proof that tracks predate the settlement is worth having.
  • Check platform rules before releasing: acceptance of AI music varies by store and streaming service — see our breakdown of which platforms accept AI music.

Songs you did not download are not gone — they stream on your Udio account — but there is currently no way to export them, and none has been announced for the 2026 relaunch.

Rebuilding your workflow: a 20-minute checklist

  1. Cancel at udio.com/account (or in Apple's Subscriptions if app-billed) and save the confirmation.
  2. Send the refund request email — worst case is a "no."
  3. Back up every pre-lockdown Udio file to two locations, with dates and receipts.
  4. Do not delete your Udio account; your un-exported songs live there.
  5. Pick your new home: Suno for generation only, or Seedance if you want songs and release videos on one subscription.
  6. Re-create your best prompts and styles from memory or saved notes — prompt libraries don't transfer between platforms.
  7. Before releasing anything, refresh on the basics in our guide to making an AI song and check your distributor's AI policy.

The frustrating part of the lockdown is that it punished the users who treated the platform most seriously. The fix is structural: from now on, export early, back up everything, and favor platforms whose paid tiers put ownership and files in your hands.

Frequently asked questions

Can I still download my old Udio songs?+

No. Udio disabled all downloads on October 30, 2025 after its UMG settlement, and the one-time 48-hour rescue window ran November 3–5, 2025. Songs you didn't export during or before that window remain streamable in your Udio account but cannot currently be exported, and no export option has been announced for the 2026 relaunch.

Will Udio refund my subscription?+

There is no automatic or prorated refund — cancellation simply stops renewal at the end of your billing cycle. Refunds are handled case-by-case: email support@udio.com citing that the download feature was removed after you paid. Annual subscribers who paid shortly before the lockdown have the strongest case. If you subscribed through Apple's App Store, request the refund through Apple instead.

Can I legally sell or stream songs I downloaded from Udio before the lockdown?+

Yes. Songs created before the UMG agreement and downloaded in time are owned by their creators under Udio's original terms, including commercial-use rights — Udio confirmed this when it opened the 48-hour window. Keep your receipts and creation dates as provenance, and check each distributor's AI-music policy before releasing.

Does canceling my Udio subscription delete my songs?+

No. Canceling only stops future billing; your account and on-platform library remain. Deleting your account is a separate, permanent action — avoid it, because your un-exported songs exist only on Udio's servers and deletion would remove your only access to them.

What is the best Udio alternative right now?+

For pure song generation with downloads, Suno is the closest replacement (free tier of 50 credits/day; commercial rights from $8/month). If you also make release videos, Seedance through the reseller we link bundles a Suno-engine Music Maker with multi-engine video generation on one credit pool, with commercial licensing and watermark-free exports on paid plans from $9.90/month on annual billing, or $19.90 billed monthly.
