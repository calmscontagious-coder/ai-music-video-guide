How to Edit AI Clips Into a Music Video With Beat-Synced Cuts

By the AI Music Video Guide teamUpdated November 4, 202610 min read

Quick answer: Sort clips by song section, lay them above the finished track, and let a beat detector place the cuts. CapCut's Auto Cut has a Beat Sync option with a sensitivity control (CapCut help, article dated February 3, 2026). Then export an MP4 at 1080p or higher, which Spotify's video podcast specs page recommends (read November 2, 2026).

Once the generator is done you have a folder of short clips, one finished song, and no edit. This guide walks through that stage: how we'd organise the clips, how CapCut's Auto Cut lines the cuts up with the beat on desktop and on a phone, which export settings to pick, what the one published Spotify video spec asks for, and when YouTube requires an AI disclosure.

Every figure comes from a page we read on November 2, 2026; dated sources show their publication date. Where the pages were vague we say so instead of guessing.

How should you organise AI clips before you edit?

Name every clip by the song section it belongs to, keep one visual style across the whole set, and generate more clips than the edit needs. A beat-synced cut only looks intentional when the shot that lands on the downbeat was built for that moment, so the sorting work pays for itself on the timeline.

We'd start in a file browser, not an editor. Rename clips with the section first (intro, verse, chorus, bridge, outro) and a short description after, so the chorus shots sit together when you sort by name. A storyboard helps here, and we cover one approach in our guide to storyboarding an AI music video.

Overshoot on quantity. Beat-synced editing eats footage, because every cut needs a fresh shot and an automatic tool will happily place many cuts per bar on a fast song. Generators also produce clips with warped hands, drifting faces or a style that doesn't match the rest, and you'll bin those. Our piece on making AI videos longer than a single clip covers chaining short generations.

Hold one visual style. Pick a palette, a lens feel and a lighting mood, write them into every prompt, and throw out clips that break them even if they look good alone. One orange, sunlit shot in a cold blue sequence reads as a mistake no matter how well it's timed.

How does Auto Cut sync the cuts to the beat?

Auto Cut is CapCut's automatic trimming tool, and Beat Sync is the option built for music. On desktop you reach it from the AI button in the top toolbar, choose Beat Sync, set the sensitivity to cut on every beat or only strong beats, and apply (CapCut help, articles dated February 3, 2026).

CapCut's help centre describes Auto Cut as "an AI-powered editing feature in CapCut that automatically trims, segments, and syncs your raw footage". It detects cut points from voice, beats or on-screen text and applies matching transitions.

For a music video the trigger you want is the beat, and the first help page says the sensitivity can be set to "cut on every beat" or only strong beats. We'd start with strong beats on anything faster than a ballad; every-beat mode on a dense track produces a strobe of cuts that burns through your folder and tires the viewer.

The desktop path

  • Open a project with your clips on the timeline, in section order, with the finished song on the audio track underneath.
  • Click the "AI" or "Smart Edit" button in the top toolbar, or go to File, AI Features, Auto Cut. A second help article gives the route as Edit, AI Tools, Auto Cut, and the first article warns that the menu path may vary by version, so look in whichever menu your build shows.
  • Choose Beat Sync. The other two options, Speech Pause Detection and Script-Based Editing (also called AI Script Mode), are for speech and script-driven edits.
  • Adjust sensitivity, the minimum or range of clip duration, and the transition style if your version offers them.
  • Confirm with "Apply" or "Generate", then reorder, delete or add effects on the timeline.

The mobile path

  • Open a project, then tap the Templates icon at the top right, next to Export.
  • Find "AI Creation" or "Smart Tools" and select Auto Cut. The help page says it may appear as "Beat Cut", "Smart Cut" or "Rhythm Edit".
  • Choose music beat as the trigger (the alternatives are voice and text-to-video), apply, and preview.

Two honest caveats from the help pages. The notes we read state no maximum clip count or duration limit for Auto Cut, and mention no Pro-only restriction, which doesn't prove there isn't one on your build. Separately, the AI text-command feature is rolling out gradually and requires an updated desktop app, so update before you assume a missing menu item is gone for good. For a deeper look at the technique itself, including when to override the automatic cuts, read our guide to beat-synced AI video editing.

What to fix after the automatic pass

Treat the Auto Cut result as a draft timeline, not a finished edit. CapCut's own page on music video workflows says beat-aware tools analyse a track for rhythm, BPM, mood and emotional movement and produce a draft timeline aligned to musical moments, and its advice is to use templates for speed and then adjust fonts, colours, crops and timing (CapCut, page dated June 5, 2026).

We'd check three kinds of cut by hand: the one into the first chorus, any cut that puts two near-identical shots side by side, and any cut that lands mid-motion, where the tool chose the beat but not the frame. If you edit AI clips into a music video regularly, this manual pass is the part worth slowing down for.

How do you export the finished music video at the right settings?

Click Export at the top right, then set resolution, frame rate and bitrate. Desktop offers 2K (2560x1440) or 4K (3840x2160) and frame rates up to 60fps depending on source (CapCut help, article dated January 14, 2026). The free-versus-Pro caveat on that page applies only to CapCut Online, the browser version, not to desktop or mobile.

The export panel is where a lot of AI music videos quietly lose quality. The help page says frame rate goes up to 60fps depending on the source, so match the export to your clips' frame rate, or re-generate the odd one out. The same page says mobile offers 720P, 1080P, 2K and 4K "if supported", and that the 4K option only appears when the source clips are higher than 1080p.

Free or Pro for the export?

For CapCut Online in the browser, the export help page says free accounts "may face watermarks or bitrate limits on 4K exports" and that "CapCut Pro subscribers get unrestricted 4K". It states no free-versus-Pro rule for desktop or mobile exports, and it does not say what the free tier does at 1080p. We'd read "may" literally: run a short test export on the free tier at your target resolution and check for a watermark before paying for anything.

For reference only, because CapCut's pricing page says its displayed pricing "is for reference only", Pro is listed at $19.99 a month or $179.99 a year with 1200 monthly AI credits, 1 TB of storage and a 7-day free trial that may vary by region (CapCut pricing page, read November 2, 2026). Ultra is listed at $79 a month or $900 a year with 8645 monthly AI credits.

One gap to flag: the pricing page itself does not state export resolution or watermark rules for Free versus Pro, and the only resolution figures on it (720p and 480p) describe AI generation limits, not exports.

If 1080p is your delivery target, keep the master file and only reach for 4K when a client or platform asks for it. No page we read says what the free tier does at 1080p on desktop or mobile, so the test export is the only honest answer.

Which published video spec should you export to?

The one published Spotify video spec is written for video podcast episodes, so we use it as a safe export target, not as a music video delivery spec. It recommends MP4, H.264 or H.265, 1080p or higher, 16:9, 24, 25, 30, 50 or 60 FPS, and AAC-LC stereo at 128 Kbps or higher (Spotify for Creators, read November 2, 2026).

To be clear about the audience: that page is written for video podcasts, and we found no music-video spec in the pages we read. We still use it because it is specific and published. The full recommended and compatible values from Spotify's video specs page (read November 2, 2026) are below.

Two details on that page matter more than they look. The file must carry one video track and one audio track of the same duration, so trim any silent tail on the video or any stray audio past the last frame before you export. And the page discourages FLV and AVI, so if an older tool hands you one of those, re-wrap it as MP4 rather than uploading as is.

CapCut's export help page does not name a container or codec, so check the export dialog for MP4 and H.264 before relying on any spec.

Platforms other than Spotify have their own rules on aspect ratio, AI labelling and music rights. We keep those in one place in our guide to AI music video rules across TikTok, Spotify and Instagram.

Do you have to disclose AI music on a video platform?

On YouTube, yes, when the music is the main focus. YouTube's help page lists content that "Creates music that's the main focus of the video" among the realistic AI uses that must be disclosed, by selecting "Yes" under "AI use" in YouTube Studio (YouTube Help, read November 2, 2026). The page says disclosure alone doesn't affect reach or monetisation eligibility.

This is the rule most AI musicians miss, because they think of the disclosure as being about deepfakes. YouTube's list of content that needs a label covers four cases: making a real person appear to say or do something they didn't, altering footage of a real event or place, generating a realistic scene that didn't occur, and creating music that is the main focus of the video. A music video built on an AI-generated song likely falls under the last case regardless of how the visuals were made.

The visuals are judged separately. The same page says creators don't need to disclose non-realistic content made with AI, nor minor edits that are "primarily aesthetic" such as beauty filters and colour adjustments, nor "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs". So a stylised, clearly animated set of AI clips may not need a label on its own, but the AI song underneath still does.

What the label looks like depends on the content. According to YouTube's page on disclosing altered or synthetic content (read November 2, 2026), for AI music or photorealistic content the label may appear in the video player, while for non-music AI audio or animated content it may appear in the expanded description. YouTube may also apply labels automatically. We'd disclose every time rather than wait for an automatic label.

Frequently asked questions

Does the beat-sync feature cut on every single beat?+

Only if you ask it to. The first help page for the editor we describe says the sensitivity can be set to cut on every beat or only on strong beats. On fast songs, every-beat mode produces a flicker of cuts and uses up your clip folder quickly, so we start with strong beats and add cuts by hand where the song needs them.

Why can't I see a 4K option when exporting on my phone?+

A few things can hide it. The editor's export help page, dated January 14, 2026, says mobile offers 720P, 1080P, 2K and 4K only if supported on your device, and that the 4K option appears only when the source clips are higher than 1080p, so a folder of 1080p generations will not show it. Export at the highest resolution your clips actually have rather than upscaling in the editor.

What export settings does Spotify's published video spec ask for?+

Spotify for Creators publishes one video spec, written for video podcast episodes, and we use it as a safe export target. Read November 2, 2026, it recommends an MP4 file with H.264 or H.265 video, 1080p or higher, 16:9, a frame rate of 24, 25, 30, 50 or 60 FPS, and AAC-LC stereo audio at 128 Kbps or higher.

Do I need a paid editing plan to finish a beat-synced music video?+

We could not confirm that from the pages we read. The export help page says free accounts may face watermarks or bitrate limits on 4K exports in the browser version, and that paid subscribers get unrestricted 4K there. It does not say what the free tier does at 1080p, and the pricing page states no export or watermark rules. Run a short test export on the free tier first.

Do I have to label an AI music video on YouTube?+

Yes when the music itself is AI-made and is the main focus of the video. YouTube's disclosure page, read November 2, 2026, lists that case among the content that must be disclosed by selecting Yes under AI use in YouTube Studio. Non-realistic visuals and minor aesthetic edits don't need a label, but the AI song underneath still does.

How many clips should I generate for one song?+

More than the edit needs, without a fixed number. Beat-synced cutting consumes a fresh shot per cut, and some generated clips will be unusable because of warped hands, drifting faces or a style that doesn't match. Keep spare clips for every section so no shot repeats within the same minute, and name each clip by its section before you start.
