Which plan lets you put an AI-generated track on streaming services?

On ElevenLabs, Exhibit A of the Music Terms marks streaming Prohibited on Free and Starter and Permitted on Creator ($22 a month) and Pro ($99 a month) (ElevenLabs Music Terms and music page, read November 13, 2026). Suno's pricing page gives Pro and Premier commercial use rights and Free no commercial rights (Suno pricing page, read November 13, 2026).

The ElevenLabs music page sums up each tier briefly: Free is 'Personal use only', Starter is 'Commercial, no streaming', and Creator and Pro are 'Commercial, not enterprise' (ElevenLabs music page, read November 13, 2026). Exhibit A of the Music Terms holds the detail, and the music page FAQ sends you there.

On Free, Exhibit A marks streaming 'Prohibited', downloads 'Not permitted', API 'No' and attribution 'Required (denote Eleven Music when distributing)'. Starter keeps streaming 'Prohibited' but allows 30 minutes of downloads, API access and no attribution. Creator and Pro both mark streaming 'Permitted'; Creator lists 62 minutes of monthly generation and 250 download minutes, Pro lists 304 minutes of monthly generation, 500 download minutes and high-quality downloads (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026).

Exhibit A lists Free, Starter, Creator and Pro as for individual use only; Scale is for individuals or organizations with fewer than 10 employees, and Business for fewer than 50 (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026).

No streaming service is named in the Music Terms, so 'Permitted' is a blanket statement, not a list of distributors. The Free row's media-rights line reads 'All online and offline commercial use permitted, except film, TV, radio, and Studio Games' (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026). That line sits beside Prohibited streaming and no downloads on Free, and the music page labels Free 'Personal use only', so we do not read Free as a release license.

Suno's pricing page puts 'No commercial rights' on Free, which gives 50 credits per day and the v6-mini model, and 'Commercial use rights' on Pro and Premier (Suno pricing page, read November 13, 2026). The terms add a condition the pricing page does not: you may commercially exploit Output only 'provided you have obtained a permitted download of that Output' (Suno Terms of Service, read November 13, 2026).

The pricing page lists 20 song downloads per month on Pro and 60 on Premier (Suno pricing page, read November 13, 2026). Those download rights 'are perpetual' and survive 'the expiry, cancellation, downgrade or suspension of your subscription' (Suno Terms of Service, read November 13, 2026). Our guide to the Suno plan you need for commercial use covers that side, and which platforms accept AI music covers the distributors.

Who owns the track once it is generated?

ElevenLabs says you retain all rights in your Input and Output (ElevenLabs Music Terms section 2.2, read November 13, 2026). Suno assigns its rights in Output to Pro and Premier subscribers but keeps ownership of songs made on the Free tier, and warns that no copyright may vest in any Output (Suno Terms of Service, read November 13, 2026).

Section 3.1 of the Music Terms adds that 'you are responsible for reviewing Output for clearance before using, publishing, or distributing it' (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026). The terms were last updated on 09 October 2026; material reductions of rights take effect 30 days after notice.

Section 1.5 covers plan changes: an upgrade puts all Output in the account under the upgraded plan, while a self-serve user who downgrades or terminates keeps existing Output under the plan in effect when it was created, with new Output under the then-current plan (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026).

Suno splits ownership by tier and timing. The help center's test is 'was I subscribed when I made the song?' On Pro or Premier at creation, 'you are considered the owner of the song'; on Free, 'we retain ownership of the songs you generate' (Suno Help Center, read November 13, 2026). The page does not say whether upgrading later changes older songs.

Two cautions follow. 'Suno makes no representation or warranty to you that any copyright will vest in any Output', and 'We reserve the right to append a fingerprint, watermark, or metadata indicating the applicable service tier' (Suno Terms of Service, read November 13, 2026). Remixes are a joint work owned jointly and equally by you and the Remixer, and nothing in that paragraph permits commercial use of a Remix.

Neither vendor promises copyright. Suno's help center says the material may not be eligible for copyright protection, because US copyright law protects material created by a human (Suno Help Center, read November 13, 2026). For the video side of the same question, see who owns an AI music video.

What can't you prompt, and what can't you release?

ElevenLabs bars prompts naming any artist, song, album, publisher or label, or using a substantial portion of a song's lyrics, and bars Output that mimics a recording artist's voice misleadingly. Film, TV, radio and Studio Games are excluded on self-serve plans. Suno bars Voice Models of another person and impersonation (both vendors' terms, read November 13, 2026).

Section 2.1 of the ElevenLabs Music Terms lists the prohibited prompts in full, down to 'a substantial or distinctive portion of any song's lyrics'. Section 3.3 bars Output that 'replicates or mimics the voice, likeness, or identifiable characteristics of any recording artist' where misleading, and section 4.2 prohibits music libraries unless authorized in writing (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026).

Suno's terms name no artists. The Voice Model section has you 'expressly agree not to create, or attempt to create, a Voice Model of another person', and User Conduct item 4 bars output presented as 'created by or otherwise associated with an individual' (Suno Terms of Service, read November 13, 2026).

Two vendor pages disagree on film and TV

The docs say 'Eleven Music is cleared for nearly all commercial uses, from film and television to podcasts and social media videos' (ElevenLabs docs, read November 13, 2026). The music page FAQ says self-serve commercial use is permitted 'except for film, TV, and Studio Games', and Exhibit A of the Music Terms adds radio to that list (ElevenLabs music page and Music Terms, read November 13, 2026).

Both pages point to the Music Terms as governing, so the Music Terms are the page to check before any film, TV or radio use. That is our reading, not legal advice; confirm with the vendor before you rely on it. Enterprise Music is the only row that lists 'All online and offline commercial use permitted' with no exclusion (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026). Our piece on releasing AI music legally covers the wider industry rules.

Do you have to say the track was made with AI?

Sometimes, and the platform decides. ElevenLabs section 4.4 makes you responsible for each streaming platform's disclosure policy on AI-generated content, and Free-tier output must carry attribution (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026). YouTube requires disclosure when AI 'Creates music that's the main focus of the video' and may add a label (YouTube Help, read November 13, 2026).

Attribution is tier-specific. Exhibit A marks it 'Required (denote Eleven Music when distributing)' on Free and 'No Attribution Required' on Starter, and section 4.8(a) asks for wording 'substantially similar to "Created in collaboration with ElevenLabs"' where required (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026). Free marks streaming Prohibited and downloads Not permitted, so we would not plan a release on that tier.

Suno's terms put no disclosure duty on you in the sections we read, but the fingerprint, watermark or metadata clause means a tier marker may travel with the file whether you disclose or not (Suno Terms of Service, read November 13, 2026).

What the video platform asks for

YouTube lists four triggers for a disclosure: content that 'Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do', 'Alters footage of a real event or place', 'Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur', or 'Creates music that's the main focus of the video' (YouTube Help, read November 13, 2026). An AI-generated track under a music video is the fourth one.

You set it during upload in YouTube Studio under 'AI use' in the Attributes section. If you select 'Yes', YouTube adds a label, which may sit on the player for music (YouTube Help, read November 13, 2026). Non-realistic content and minor edits such as caption creation need no disclosure.

What does each plan cost, and how much music do you get for it?

ElevenLabs lists Free at $0 with 10k credits, Starter at $6 with 30k, Creator at $22 with 121k and Pro at $99 with 600k (ElevenLabs pricing page, read November 13, 2026). Suno shows Pro at $8 with 2,500 credits and Premier at $24 with 10,000, both figures tied to yearly checkout (Suno pricing page, read November 13, 2026).

The Music Terms translate the music tiers into minutes: 62 minutes of monthly generation on Creator and 304 on Pro, with 250 download minutes on Creator and 500 on Pro (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026). The pricing page prices Eleven Music at about 900 credits per minute (ElevenLabs pricing page, read November 13, 2026). Those are two separate limits, a music-minutes cap in the terms and a credit pool on the pricing page.

The music page lists extra minutes at $0.33 on Free, $0.18 on Starter, $0.16 on Creator and $0.15 on Pro (ElevenLabs music page, read November 13, 2026). Check the page you buy from.

'Unused credits roll over for up to two months, up to 2x your monthly quota' (ElevenLabs pricing page, read November 13, 2026). Each track runs from 3 seconds to 5 minutes and downloads as MP3 or WAV (ElevenLabs docs, read November 13, 2026). The current ElevenLabs plans through our link are the place to confirm this before paying.

Suno's credits work differently: 'Credits included in subscriptions do not carry over from day to day or month to month', while 'Purchased top up credits do not expire, but require an active subscription to use' (Suno pricing page, read November 13, 2026). Songs per credit are not stated.

Frequently asked questions

Which plan do I need to stream a track made with an AI music generator?

On the voice tool we link, streaming is marked Prohibited on the Free and Starter tiers and Permitted from the Creator tier at $22 a month and the Pro tier at $99, according to the vendor's music terms read November 13, 2026. The other generator we compared switches commercial rights on at its first paid tier, with a permitted download required before you distribute.

Do I own a song made with an AI music generator?

It depends on the vendor and the tier. One vendor's terms say you retain all rights in your input and output. The other assigns its rights to paid subscribers but keeps ownership of songs made on its free tier. Both vendors' pages stop short of promising copyright, and one notes that US copyright law protects material created by a human.

Can I keep my commercial rights if I cancel the subscription?

On the generator that assigns ownership, yes for tracks made on a paid plan and covered by a permitted download: the terms call those download rights perpetual and say they survive expiry, cancellation, downgrade or suspension. The voice tool's music terms say in section 1.5 that a self-serve user who downgrades or terminates keeps existing output under the plan in effect when it was created.

Can I use a real artist's name in the prompt?

Not on the voice tool we link. Its music terms prohibit prompts that include any artist's or songwriter's real or stage name, any song, album, publisher or record label name, or a substantial portion of any song's lyrics, and they bar output that mimics a recording artist's voice in a misleading way. The other generator bars creating a voice model of another person.

Do I need to label an AI-generated track in a video upload?

On the video platform whose help page we read, yes when the AI music is the main focus of the video. That is one of four listed triggers; you answer under 'AI use' in the Attributes section during upload, and a label may appear on the player for music. Non-realistic content, caption creation and cloning your own voice for a voiceover need no disclosure.

Can a track made on these tools go into a film or TV production?

Not on the self-serve plans of the voice tool we link: its music page excludes film, TV and Studio Games, its terms add radio, and only its Enterprise Music tier lists no exclusion. One of its docs pages says the opposite, and the terms govern. The other generator's pages we read do not address film or TV placement at all.
