Turn a Udio Song Into a Music Video (After the Download Lock)

By the AI Music Video Guide team · Updated October 21, 2026 · 12 min read

This post contains affiliate links — if you sign up through them we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you (disclosure). Researched and edited for accuracy with AI assistance.

Quick answer: If you exported the track before Udio locked downloads in October 2025, or during the November 3 to 5 window, upload it to Seedance and render the song whole or in 15 second clips. If you never exported it, nothing legitimate gets the audio out: Udio's terms forbid downloading and recording. Rebuild it on Suno.

We make videos for songs from both generators. When Udio's download button went away, so did the handoff from music tool to video tool. This guide covers what still works for a Udio music video and what it costs. Every price, date and policy below comes from a page we opened on October 21, 2026, or carries the date of its report.

What did the Universal settlement change about Udio downloads?

On October 29, 2025, Udio settled Universal Music Group's copyright lawsuit and switched off downloads of audio, video and stems the same day. A 48-hour grace window opened on November 3, 2025 and closed on November 5. As of October 21, 2026, Udio's help center still lists downloads as disabled, with no return date.

Udio's CEO wrote on the company blog that "downloads from the platform will be unavailable" starting that day (Udio blog, October 29, 2025). Universal's release described a walled garden with fingerprinting and filtering (UMG, October 29, 2025).

The dates that matter, in order:

  • October 29, 2025: Universal settlement. Downloads of audio, video and stems disabled; subscribers get a one-time 1,000 non-expiring credits (Udio Help Center, checked October 21, 2026).
  • November 3 to 5, 2025: a 48-hour window, starting Monday, November 3, to pull previously made tracks, then the walls go back up (Billboard, October 2025).
  • November 12, 2025: Terms of Service revised. Section 1.2 now says you may not download copies of any Output (Udio Terms of Service).
  • November 2025 to April 2026: Warner, Merlin, Kobalt and Believe sign licensing deals with Udio. Sony keeps suing (Music Business Worldwide, May 2026).
  • July 14, 2026: Udio picks BuyDRM to encrypt streaming across Widevine, FairPlay and PlayReady (Music Business Worldwide, July 2026).

The transition product is still for sale. Udio's pricing page on October 21, 2026 lists Standard at $10 a month for 2,400 credits and Pro at $30 for 6,000, plus a free tier of 10 credits a day. None of the plans lists downloads.

Can you still get a Udio song out of the app in 2026?

No. The download control is gone for audio, video and stems, and the Terms of Service revised on November 12, 2025 say you may not download, reproduce or publish Output on any personal device or on platforms such as YouTube, TikTok and Spotify. The one outbound path Udio offers is a udio.com share link that anyone can play without an account.

A share link is a listening page, not a file. No video tool can read audio from it, and YouTube will not take it as an upload.

The ownership picture is messier. Udio's help center says the company claims no ownership over what you generate and that you own the output, with a "Created with Udio" credit required for songs made on a free account (Udio Help Center, checked October 21, 2026). The current terms say the opposite on both counts. Section 6.1 says Udio and its licensors own all Output and that you assign any rights you hold to Udio, Section 6.3 limits use to personal, non-commercial purposes, and Section 6.4 still describes how to credit Udio when Output is used publicly.

Files that left the app earlier are a different case. When Udio announced the November window, it said songs downloaded during it would stay under the prior terms, which granted users ownership and commercial use (Billboard, October 2025). The live terms do not repeat that promise. We are not lawyers, so read the current terms yourself before you publish.

Is recording the stream or a "Udio downloader" a way around it?

Not one we would use. Section 5.2 of Udio's terms bans circumventing the service's content protections and bans scrapers or any manual process that extracts content, so a screen recording or a paste-the-link downloader breaks the agreement attached to your account. The platform Udio is building also adds stream encryption and an inaudible watermark, so a ripped file can be identified later.

Downloader sites have a practical problem too: Udio no longer serves the file, so they fail or capture a lossy stream. Udio's planned fingerprinting lets distributors check uploads against its creations and block matches (Music Business Worldwide, May 2026), and a banned account loses the library you built.

If you already have the file, how do you make the video with Seedance?

Seedance offers two routes. Music to Video turns the whole exported file into one video the length of the song, while the Seedance 2.0 route has you shoot one clip per section over @audio1 and stitch the clips. The first is faster; the second lets you choose every shot.

The one-render route: Music to Video

The site's Music to Video tool takes one MP3, WAV or M4A between 5 seconds and 10 minutes long and returns a single video that runs the exact length of the song. It bills by the second of audio, 2 credits a second at 480p and 4 at 720p, so a 3 minute song costs about 360 credits at 480p or 720 at 720p (seeddance.video Music to Video page, checked October 21, 2026). The page shows the total before you generate and refunds failed runs.

You can add up to 3 reference photos to keep one performer on screen, a scene description, and lyrics lip sync. Long WAV files can pass the 50MB upload cap, so upload a 320 kbps MP3 for songs past about 4 minutes. The trade is control: you describe the scene once and the tool decides the shots and cuts. If you want a particular image on the second chorus, use the clip route.

The shot-by-shot route: Seedance 2.0 clips

Upload pieces of the file as @audio1, keep the same @image1 performer in every clip, and stitch the clips over the full original track. Six steps, with the credit math.

  1. Check the file. A WAV from the old Udio export is ideal; a 320 kbps MP3 is fine. Cut it into 10 to 15 second pieces named by section (intro, verse 1, hook). Seedance 2.0 accepts up to 3 WAV or MP3 files per render, 2 to 15 seconds each and 15 seconds in total, alongside 9 images and 3 video clips (Seedance 2.0 page, checked October 21, 2026).
  2. Storyboard against the song, not against a mood. A 3 minute track is 12 or 13 fifteen-second pieces. Plan 15 to 20 renders so you can throw away the ones that drift, and decide where the picture changes before you spend a credit.
  3. Lock the performer. One reference image as @image1 in every render keeps the same character on screen. A back-of-head or silhouette reference works if you don't want a face in the video.
  4. Write the prompt with jobs, not adjectives. Start with: @audio1 is the finished soundtrack, follow it exactly, do not generate new music. Then describe the shot and leave the music itself out. Seedance treats uploaded audio as a style reference by default and will compose its own track or re-sing your vocal; the fixes are in our guide to Seedance ignoring reference audio.
  5. Price one test clip, then budget. The plan page we checked on October 21, 2026 lists Basic at $9.90 a month billed annually for 150 credits and Pro at $19.90 for 350, rated at up to 15 and up to 35 video renders a month (Seedance plans). Those counts assume about 10 credits a render. The Seedance 2.0 page lists 20 credits a second at 480p and 40 at 720p, so a 10 second 720p clip costs 400 credits, and the generator showed 40 credits at its default settings when we checked. At the per-second rates or the default, 15 to 20 renders cost more than one month's 350 credits. Annual plans issue the whole year up front, 4,200 credits on Pro and 1,800 on Basic, so render one test clip and read the credit counter before you storyboard 20.
  6. Stitch over the original. Mute the generated audio on every clip, lay your full Udio file underneath in CapCut or DaVinci Resolve, and cut on the beat. Export 16:9 for YouTube and 9:16 for Shorts and TikTok. If the song came from a free Udio account, put "Created with Udio" in the title or description.

Two honest limits on this route. Seedance lip sync holds for a line or two and then drifts, so singing close-ups work best under 10 seconds. And Seedance 2.0 caps clips at 15 seconds, so a full song means 15 to 20 renders. Seedance 2.5 on the same site makes clips up to 30 seconds, at 12 credits a second at 480p and 23 at 720p (Seedance 2.5 page, checked October 21, 2026), which halves the stitching. Music to Video skips the stitching, at the cost of shot-by-shot control. For the per-clip arithmetic, the Seedance music video tutorial walks through a 3 minute build at 480p and 720p.

One more thing before you pay anyone: the subscription platform we link to is a third-party aggregator that runs Seedance alongside other engines. ByteDance's own consumer app is Dreamina, and its official API is BytePlus ModelArk, where Seedance 2.0 listed at $0.15 a second at 720p as of September 2026 (FrameSurfer, September 2026). The aggregator asks less of you: no code, one credit pool, a browser.

What if you never exported the Udio track?

Rebuild it on a generator that lets files leave. Suno Pro is $8 a month billed yearly for 2,500 credits and 20 song downloads a month as of October 21, 2026, and a downloaded WAV goes through the same Seedance routes above. The Udio original stays where it is, as a share link you can play for people.

What carries over: lyrics you wrote yourself, the style prompt, the tempo and key, the section structure, and a rough sense of the vocal. What doesn't: the recording. Lyrics that Udio generated for you are Output under its terms, so write fresh ones. Expect several generations before a Suno take sits close to what you had, and keep the Udio link open to compare.

Suno's download allowance is the constraint to plan around. Pro gives 20 downloads a month and Premier, at $24 a month billed yearly for 10,000 credits, gives 60 (Suno pricing and Suno Help Center, both checked October 21, 2026). Downloading the same song twice does not spend a second download, so audition inside Suno and export only the keeper.

If the song matters commercially, re-record it in a DAW or with a vocalist, and no AI platform's terms touch the master. If you are leaving Udio over this, our guide to cancelling Udio and asking for a refund covers the account side.

Should you wait for Starstruck instead?

No. Starstruck is Udio's coming fan app for covers and remixes of opted-in label artists; every creation stays inside it and the rights holder, not the fan, owns the result. As of October 21, 2026 we found no launch date on Udio's own site, and its help center still describes the transition product.

According to a Water & Music report on a private Udio and Kobalt webinar held April 30, 2026, Starstruck will offer four modes, Cover, Reimagine, Remix and Create, each starting from a specific artist and song. Encrypted streams, an inaudible watermark on every output, and fingerprinting guard the garden (Music Business Worldwide, May 2026).

You want your Udio music video on YouTube. Starstruck is built so nothing made inside it reaches YouTube, and around other people's catalogs, not your originals.

Frequently asked questions

Can I still download my old Udio songs in 2026?+

No. Udio disabled downloads of audio, video and stems on October 29, 2025, reopened them for 48 hours from November 3 to 5, 2025, and has not restored them since. On October 21, 2026 the help center still lists downloads as disabled with no return date, and the pricing page does not list downloads under any plan.

Is it against the rules to screen-record a Udio song for a video?+

Udio's Terms of Service, revised November 12, 2025, say you may not download, reproduce or publish Output on any device or platform, and Section 5.2 bans circumventing content protections or extracting content by any process. That reads as a yes. We are not lawyers, so read the terms yourself, but we do not record streams and do not teach it.

How much does a Udio music video cost if I rebuild the track?+

Start from about $28 a month on annual billing: Suno Pro at $8 for 2,500 credits and 20 downloads, plus Seedance Pro at $19.90 for 350 credits. Music to Video bills 4 credits a second at 720p, about 720 credits for a 3 minute song. Clip costs vary with length and resolution, so price one test clip first. Prices checked October 21, 2026.

Will Seedance keep my exact Udio audio in the video?+

Only if you tell it to. By default Seedance treats an uploaded file as a style reference and may compose new music or re-sing the vocal. Tag the file as @audio1, state that it is the finished soundtrack to follow exactly, and remove every musical adjective from the prompt. Then mute the clip audio in your editor and lay the original track underneath.

Will Udio's Starstruck app let me export a music video?+

No. Starstruck is designed so creations never leave the app: streams are encrypted, every output carries an inaudible watermark, and fingerprints let distributors block uploads. It is a fan app for covers and remixes of opted-in label artists, and the rights holder owns the result. As of October 21, 2026 Udio's own site lists no launch date.

Can I at least share the Udio version somewhere?+

Yes, by link. Every Udio song has a udio.com share link anyone can play without an account. On ownership, Udio's help center says you own what you generate, but Section 6.1 of the current Terms says Udio and its licensors own all Output. The link is a listening page, not a file, so it cannot go to YouTube or a video generator.
