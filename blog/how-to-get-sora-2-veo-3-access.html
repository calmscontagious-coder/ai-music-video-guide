How to Get Veo 3.1 Access: Flow, Gemini, AI Studio and Vids (2026)

By the AI Music Video GuideUpdated October 21, 202612 min read

Quick answer: Anyone with a Google account can use Veo 3.1 today. Google Flow gives every account 50 free credits a day, enough for Lite or Fast clips; Quality clips need a Google AI plan. Google Vids adds 6 free clips a month. The Gemini app has moved to Gemini Omni. Developers pay per second in AI Studio and the Gemini API.

This page used to cover Sora 2 as well. We've narrowed it to Veo 3.1, because Sora 2 access no longer exists. If you came for Sora, our Sora 2 alternatives guide covers what replaced it.

Every price and limit below was checked against Google's own pages on October 21, 2026. Veo 3.1 access has changed twice this year, once when Vids opened it to free accounts and once when the Gemini app dropped it. Where two Google pages disagree, we say so.

Can you still get Sora 2 access?

No. OpenAI says the Sora web and app experiences were discontinued on April 26, 2026, and the Sora API on September 24, 2026. Existing users can still export their videos from the Sora sunset page.

Where can you use Veo 3.1 right now?

Four places: Google Flow (the consumer studio), Google Vids (the video editor inside Workspace), Google AI Studio and the Gemini API (developer routes), and Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform (the renamed Vertex AI) for enterprise, where Veo 3.1 is generally available while the Gemini API models are still in preview. The Gemini chat app no longer offers Veo 3.1 as a selectable model; it runs Gemini Omni instead.

Is Veo 3.1 free in Google Flow?

Partly: Google's Flow help page says every account, subscribed or not, receives 50 Flow credits a day. A Veo 3.1 Lite clip costs 10 credits and a Fast clip costs 20, so a free account can make five Lite clips or two Fast clips daily. Veo 3.1 Quality costs 100 credits, which a 50-credit day never reaches, and daily credits don't roll over.

Two more catches apply to free accounts. First, Google says non-subscribers may not be able to generate video at all during peak hours, roughly 2 PM to 5 PM UTC. Second, 1080p upscaling is "not available" without a subscription, so free Veo 3.1 clips stay at their native output size.

The refresh timing is also unusual. Your next 50 credits arrive the day after your first generation, not at midnight.

Flow's own Veo 3.1 tiers break down like this (per generation, October 21, 2026):

  • Veo 3.1 Lite: 4, 6 or 8 seconds, plus Extend. 10 credits, or 5 for Ultra subscribers.
  • Veo 3.1 Fast: 4, 6 or 8 seconds, plus Extend. 20 credits, or 10 for Ultra subscribers.
  • Veo 3.1 Quality: 8 seconds, plus Extend. 100 credits for everyone, no Ultra discount.
  • 1080p upscaling: 0 credits for Plus, Pro and Ultra; unavailable otherwise.
  • 4K upscaling: 50 credits, Ultra only.

One request can produce two generations, and Flow charges per generation. Check the settings icon in the prompt box before a Quality render; Google says the costs can change at any time.

Which Google AI plan gives you the most Veo 3.1 for the money?

For most creators, Google AI Pro at $19.99 a month. It adds 1,000 Flow credits a month on top of the daily 50, which buys 10 Quality clips, 50 Fast clips or 100 Lite clips. Ultra only pays off if you render Lite and Fast in volume, because Ultra halves those two prices and is the only tier with 4K upscaling.

Does the Gemini app still run Veo 3.1?

No. Google's own Gemini video page says Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash "will now replace the previous Google Gemini Veo 3.1 model" in the Gemini app, and the Gemini Apps help page describes video generation as an Omni feature. Omni launched on May 19, 2026, for all Plus, Pro and Ultra subscribers through the Gemini app and Flow.

Inside the Gemini app you get Omni's strengths: 10-second clips, native audio, up to 5 reference photos, scene extension, video-to-video edits and multi-turn editing in one chat. You don't get a model picker. To choose Veo 3.1 Quality for a shot, open Flow and pick it there.

Omni is also cheaper than Veo 3.1 Fast and Quality in Flow credits. A 720p Omni Flash clip costs 7 credits at 4 seconds, 10 at 6 seconds, 12 at 8 seconds and 15 at 10 seconds. A 360p draft costs 4 to 7. Editing an uploaded or generated video costs 40. For blocking out a music video, we now draft in Omni at 360p and spend Veo 3.1 Quality credits only on the shots we keep.

Video generation in the Gemini app requires a Google AI plan on a personal account, or a qualifying Workspace license at work, and it's limited to users 18 and over. Uploading your own video for edits isn't available in the EEA, Switzerland, the UK and some US states. Every video the Gemini app makes carries Google's SynthID watermark.

How do you use Veo 3.1 in Google AI Studio or the Gemini API?

With a paid-tier API key. The Gemini API pricing page lists all three Veo 3.1 variants as "Not available" on the free tier, so a Google AI Pro subscription doesn't cover it. You attach billing to a Google Cloud project, then call veo-3.1-generate-preview, veo-3.1-fast-generate-preview or veo-3.1-lite-generate-preview, or pick the same models in AI Studio's video playground.

Per-second prices on the pricing page, dated October 1, 2026:

  • Veo 3.1 Standard with audio: $0.40 per second at 720p or 1080p; $0.60 per second at 4K.
  • Veo 3.1 Fast with audio: $0.10 per second at 720p; $0.12 at 1080p; $0.30 at 4K.
  • Veo 3.1 Lite with audio: $0.05 per second at 720p; $0.08 at 1080p; no 4K output.

So an 8-second Standard clip at 1080p costs $3.20, a Fast clip at 720p costs $0.80 and a Lite clip at 720p costs $0.40. The same 8 seconds at 4K Standard is $4.80. For a three-minute song cut from 8-second shots, that's roughly 23 clips before retakes, so Standard at 1080p runs about $74 if every take lands. It won't. Budget two to three takes per shot.

The API docs set the technical limits. Durations are 4, 6 or 8 seconds. Anything at 1080p or 4K must be 8 seconds. Video extension works at 720p only. Aspect ratios are 16:9 and 9:16. Google's changelog shows Veo 3.1 and 3.1 Fast entered public preview on October 15, 2025, Lite arrived March 31, 2026, and the older Veo 2 and Veo 3.0 model IDs shut down on June 30, 2026.

Google's video overview page now tells developers to treat Gemini Omni Flash as the default and reach for Veo 3.1 "for specific capabilities like scene extension, last-frame control, or integration with legacy pipelines." Omni Flash on the API works out to about $0.10 per second of 720p video.

How many free Veo 3.1 clips does Google Vids give you?

Fewer than the launch post promised: Google's April 2, 2026 announcement said every personal account gets 10 Veo 3.1 generations a month at no cost. The Vids help page we read on October 21, 2026 now lists personal accounts at 50 credits a month and 6 video clips a month, shared with AI avatars. The limit resets at 12:00 AM Pacific on the first of each month and doesn't roll over.

Paid tiers scale by seconds rather than clips. Google AI Pro accounts get 500 video-clip seconds a month, Ultra 5x gets 2,500 and Ultra 20x gets 10,000. Workspace Business Starter gets 200 seconds; Standard and Plus get 500.

Vids is the simplest Veo 3.1 door for people who've never touched a video tool. You type a prompt or drop in a photo, Vids returns an 8-second clip, and the editor handles voice-over, captions and export to YouTube. It is not where we'd build a music video; Flow's shot controls aren't here.

Is Veo 3.1 available in your country?

Probably: Google's Flow availability page lists about 150 countries and territories where the desktop app and mobile app work, for users 18 and over, with prompts supported in 37 languages. Google AI Pro and Ultra are sold in more than 150 countries. The gaps are feature-level rather than country-level.

Three restrictions to know. Editing uploaded videos isn't supported in the EEA, Switzerland or the UK. Generating videos of prominent people isn't supported anywhere. Features involving identifiable minors vary by region. Google also recommends English prompts for best results.

If your country is missing from the list, we don't recommend a VPN. Google checks location in use, not just at signup: the Gemini help page notes that travelling into an unsupported country switches Ultra features off until you return. An aggregator is the honest workaround.

When is Seedance the better route to Veo?

When you want Veo 3.1 next to Seedance 2, Kling and Wan without three subscriptions, or when Google doesn't serve your country. Seedance's engine menu lists Google Veo 3, Veo 3.1 and Veo 3.1 Lite, and its Veo 3.1 panel offers Fast and Quality modes at 8 seconds. It is the right tool for comparing engines on one prompt, not for squeezing the most Veo out of a dollar.

Here is what Seedance's own pricing page showed on October 21, 2026. Every account can claim 1 free credit every 24 hours, and a Veo 3.1 Fast clip costs 10 credits, so the free tier is a sample, not a test budget. The Basic plan is $19.90 a month for 200 credits, which the site rates at up to 20 video renders. Billed yearly it is $9.90 a month for 150 credits a month, with all 1,800 issued on day one. Exports are watermark-free MP4, and the site states that all renders include a commercial license.

Credits burn faster when you hop between engines, and third-party model availability on any aggregator can change without notice. If your project is "I need the real Veo 3.1 Quality, 1080p, ten shots a month," Google AI Pro at $19.99 is the cheaper and more direct answer.

What does Veo 3.1 do badly?

Length, people and price: every Veo 3.1 clip tops out at 8 seconds, the higher resolutions force that length, and scene extension is 720p only. The API restricts person generation on image-to-video and reference-image jobs to adults. Quality renders cost 100 Flow credits or $3.20 per 8 seconds at 1080p through the API, which adds up fast on a full song.

Google's own direction is the other weakness. The Gemini app has already replaced Veo with Omni, the API docs call Omni the default, and Flow prices Omni clips at a fraction of Veo Quality. Veo 3.1 is still Google's "leading video generation model" on the DeepMind Veo page, and Google's own API guidance still sends you to Veo for scene extension and last-frame control. Learn it for those two features, and expect the surrounding product to keep shifting toward Omni.

Frequently asked questions

Is Veo 3.1 free to use?+

Partly. Google Flow gives every account 50 credits a day, which covers five Veo 3.1 Lite clips or two Fast clips, but never a 100-credit Quality clip. Google Vids gives personal accounts 6 clips a month. The Gemini API has no free tier for Veo 3.1. Free Flow users also lose video generation during peak hours, roughly 2 PM to 5 PM UTC.

Do I need Google AI Pro to use Veo 3.1 Quality in Flow?+

You need some paid Google AI plan, because Quality costs 100 credits and the free allowance is 50 a day with no rollover. Google AI Plus adds 200 credits a month, enough for two Quality clips. Pro at $19.99 adds 1,000 credits, or 10 Quality clips, and turns on free 1080p upscaling. Ultra adds 10,000 or 25,000 credits and 4K upscaling.

Why can't I pick Veo 3.1 in the Gemini app anymore?+

Because Google replaced it. The Gemini video page says Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash now replaces the previous Veo 3.1 model in the app, and the help page describes video generation as an Omni feature. Omni makes 10-second clips with native audio and conversational edits. If you want Veo 3.1 specifically, open Google Flow and choose it there.

How much does an 8-second Veo 3.1 clip cost through the API?+

On the Gemini API pricing page dated October 1, 2026: Standard with audio is $0.40 a second at 720p or 1080p, so $3.20 per 8-second clip, and $0.60 a second at 4K, so $4.80. Fast is $0.80 per 8 seconds at 720p. Lite is $0.40 per 8 seconds at 720p. You need a paid-tier key; the free tier lists Veo 3.1 as not available.

Does Veo 3.1 put a watermark on videos?+

Videos made in the Gemini app carry SynthID, Google's imperceptible watermark, according to Google's video generation page, and you can ask Gemini to check a file for it. There is no visible logo stamped on the frame. Treat the invisible mark as permanent; it's meant to survive editing, and platforms are building detectors for it.

Can I use Veo without a Google account?+

Through an aggregator, yes. Seedance lists Google Veo 3, Veo 3.1 and Veo 3.1 Lite in its engine menu alongside Seedance 2, Kling and Wan. The free tier is 1 credit a day, and a Veo 3.1 Fast clip costs 10 credits, so plan on the Basic plan at $19.90 a month for 200 credits. You still need a Seedance account.
