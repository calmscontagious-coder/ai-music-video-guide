Holiday Gift Guide for Musicians: 10 Useful Ideas

By the AI Music Video GuideUpdated November 11, 20264 min read

Quick answer: This guide is for anyone shopping for a musician, from a beginner to a home producer. The three best picks are earplugs made for music, a pair of closed-back headphones, and a lesson or a rehearsal-room hour.

Most picks cost little, some cost nothing, and all can come from any shop. Nothing on this page earns the studio a commission. Prices appear only where we read them on the maker's page, and they may have changed.

Ten gift ideas for musicians

1. A night of honest listening

Free

Sit through their newest demo, write down three things that worked and one question. Musicians rarely get a careful listener.

Suits: Anyone sharing songs with few people to hear them. Check before buying: Ask which version they want feedback on, and keep the notes kind and specific.

2. Strings, picks and a capo

A few dollars

The consumables of a guitarist, bassist or ukulele player. A fresh set of strings is a small gift they will use.

Suits: Players of fretted instruments. Check before buying: The instrument, string gauge and scale length. Look at the packet on their instrument or ask.

3. A clip-on tuner

A few dollars

A clip-on tuner reads the instrument's vibration, so it works in a noisy room.

Suits: Beginners and gigging players. Check before buying: That it fits the headstock or the instrument body, and that it runs on a battery type they can buy.

4. Manuscript paper, a notebook and good pencils

A few dollars

Somewhere to write riffs, lyrics, chord charts and set lists that is not a phone.

Suits: Songwriters, arrangers and students. Check before buying: Staff paper or plain lined, depending on how they write.

5. Earplugs made for music

Low price

CDC NIOSH says noise is considered hazardous at 85 dBA or higher, and that work-related hearing loss can nearly always be prevented. Earplugs sold for musicians cut volume without muffling the sound as much as foam ones.

Suits: Drummers, band members, sound engineers, and anyone who goes to loud rehearsals. Check before buying: The listed attenuation, the fit, and whether the pack includes a case. This is a hearing-health gift, so pick a named music or concert plug from a maker that publishes its numbers. Source: CDC NIOSH on occupational hearing loss.

6. Closed-back studio headphones

Audio-Technica ATH-M40x: about $109 when checked (Audio-Technica, checked 2026-11-11)

The maker lists it as a closed-back dynamic model with 40 mm drivers, a detachable cable and swivelling earcups. Closed-back designs keep sound from leaking into a microphone while tracking.

Suits: Home recorders and anyone mixing in a shared room. Check before buying: Whether they already own studio headphones, the cable end they need, and the return window. The price may have changed since we checked.

7. A USB audio interface

Mid price

An interface lets a laptop record a microphone or an instrument cleanly. It is the usual first step from a phone demo to a proper recording.

Suits: Songwriters who want better sound at home. Check before buying: How many inputs they need, whether it supplies phantom power for condenser microphones, and the USB port type on their computer. Pick a known maker with driver support for their system.

8. A microphone stand, a pop filter and a cable

Low to mid price

The small pieces that decide whether a microphone is usable at all. A desk or boom stand and a pop filter fix most home-recording annoyances.

Suits: Anyone who has a microphone and holds it in one hand. Check before buying: The microphone's connector (XLR or USB) and the thread size on the stand.

9. A lesson with a teacher

Varies

One lesson, or a short block, with a teacher on their instrument. It works for beginners and for players who want help with one skill.

Suits: A player who has said what they want to learn. Check before buying: Whether the teacher accepts a gift voucher, how long it stays valid, and the lesson length.

10. An hour in a rehearsal room

Varies by room

Rehearsal rooms charge by the hour. A booked hour gives a solo player or a band space to play loud.

Suits: Bands, drummers and players who live in apartments. Check before buying: The room's amenities (a drum kit or amps) and the cancellation terms.

What not to buy

Skip an instrument unless they asked for that exact one: fit, feel and sound are personal. Skip a plugin or software bundle you cannot check against their setup. Skip a novelty item with no named maker, such as an unbranded microphone bundle, since there is nothing to check and no one to return it to.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good gift for a musician?

Earplugs made for music, closed-back headphones, or a lesson or rehearsal-room hour. Choose by what the person does most: playing loud, recording at home, or learning.

What can I give a musician for under $15?

A set of strings, a clip-on tuner, manuscript paper or a notebook. A free option is a night of careful listening to their newest demo.

Are earplugs a good gift for a musician?

Yes, if they play or attend loud rehearsals. CDC NIOSH says noise is considered hazardous at 85 dBA or higher. Choose plugs sold for music and check the fit.

Should I buy an instrument as a gift?

Only if they named the exact model. Pick accessories or lessons otherwise.
