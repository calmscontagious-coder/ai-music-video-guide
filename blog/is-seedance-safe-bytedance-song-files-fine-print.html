Is Seedance Safe? ByteDance, Your Song Files, and the Fine Print

By the AI Music Video Guide team · Updated October 21, 2026 · 12 min read

This post contains affiliate links — if you sign up through them we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you (disclosure). Researched and edited for accuracy with AI assistance.

Quick answer: Seedance is safe to use: a real ByteDance model, payments through card processors, no breach we could find. The risk is the fine print. Dreamina's terms take a perpetual, irrevocable license over everything you upload, for any purpose, which leaves room for training. Upload finished songs and stylized faces. Keep stems, demos and other people's faces off it.

Is Seedance safe for your song files and your face? We read Dreamina's terms (January 22, 2026) and privacy policy (April 30, 2026), ByteDance's enterprise rules on BytePlus, and Kling's and Runway's terms and privacy policies. Here is what they say and the upload rules we follow.

Who runs Seedance, and where does your data go?

Dreamina, the consumer home of Seedance, is operated by Bytedance Pte. Ltd., a Singapore company, according to its privacy policy dated April 30, 2026. The same policy says the information it collects is stored on servers in the United States, Singapore and Malaysia, and may be processed by staff and affiliates outside your country.

In January 2026 the US version of TikTok moved under a joint venture controlled by American investors, as Politico reported on October 9, 2026. Dreamina is not part of that arrangement. It is a ByteDance product, and its policy says your information can be shared across the wider corporate group for storage, research, moderation and support.

The policy also allows sharing with law enforcement when legally required and in a sale or merger. Your files sit in three jurisdictions, and the policy does not promise otherwise. US users are pointed to a separate Dreamina US Privacy Policy, which we could not verify; read it from inside your account.

What happens to a song or face you upload, according to the privacy policy?

The file is kept as User Content with its metadata, including when, where and by whom it was created. Extracted facial and body features are deleted once your video is generated and are not shared with third parties. Model training is a listed purpose, though the legal-basis table it publishes for Europe does not tie training to your uploads.

Most musicians skip the metadata line. A WAV can carry your name, software and a timestamp; a phone photo carries GPS coordinates. Strip it first with a free tool such as ExifTool.

The face line is better than we expected: extracted face or body attributes are deleted as soon as the requested service is provided. Dreamina's delete-after-generation promise is the most specific of the three privacy policies we read. Runway's (effective May 11, 2026) comes closest, keeping face scans only as long as the requested service needs and no more than three years after your last interaction, but it lists vendors and affiliates among recipients of biometric data. Kling's (April 21, 2026) says Character Face Mode uploads facial feature points, and on-device effects store no face data.

On training, the uses section lists machine learning models as a purpose, but the legal-basis table for users in Europe maps that purpose to account, technical, usage and location information, not User Content. The terms are the real issue: they license your uploads for any purpose, so we treat anything we upload as potential training data. No opt-out toggle is described; users in the EEA and UK can object to processing based on legitimate interests, which is the basis the policy gives for training.

Retention is vague. The Dreamina homepage, checked October 21, 2026, says uploads and generated content are automatically removed after a defined period, but never defines it. To remove a file, delete it from your library, then send a deletion request from your account email, a right the policy grants.

What rights do you give away in the Dreamina terms of service?

You keep ownership of your inputs and outputs. In exchange you grant Dreamina, its affiliates and successors, and other users of the platform a perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, transferable, sub-licensable, royalty-free license to use those assets for any purpose, and the license survives you closing your account. You also waive moral rights where the law allows.

Use is defined broadly: reproduce, modify, adapt, distribute, make derivative works and otherwise exploit. Sharing with other users is not unique to Dreamina. Kling's terms (April 21, 2026, section 4.8) let other users recreate and derive from content you publish, and Runway's terms (September 15, 2026, section 4.3) say other users may see, use and modify content you make available in public areas. The difference is scope. Dreamina's grant reaches other users for every asset you upload or generate, published or not, and for any purpose.

Kling's license is narrower in time and place, not in use. Clause 4.7.3(f) lets Kling train its AI models on your Input, and clause 4.7.2 lets it use or license your content to third parties for promotion and research. Clause 4.7.4 is the real advantage: you can revoke the authorization by emailing Kling support. Runway's section 4.4 takes an irrevocable license and states that inputs and outputs may train its models.

Section 7 of the Dreamina terms says you agree not to use the Services or any content therein for any commercial purposes, and the pricing page FAQ (checked October 21, 2026) says commercial eligibility depends on the applicable terms and the models involved. If you plan to monetize the video, confirm your plan's commercial terms in writing first. The company can also disclose your identity to anyone claiming your upload infringes their intellectual property or privacy. If you upload a song you do not fully own, that clause is how the rights-holder finds you.

Is it safe to upload your own face?

Legally yes, your face is yours; practically, Seedance will often refuse it. Since ByteDance announced face filters and C2PA watermarking on March 30, 2026, the model restricts video made from images that contain recognizable real faces, and we found no verified-likeness option in the consumer Dreamina app as of October 21, 2026. Verified routes exist elsewhere, with limits.

BytePlus's August 6, 2026 announcement of Seedance 2.5 spells out the enterprise path: real-person verification and likeness authorization in the ModelArk console, a library of more than 10,000 virtual humans, and no availability in the United States. For individuals, ByteDance's Seedance 2.0 Real Human route uses a one-time liveness check of less than 1 minute through ComfyUI partner nodes, for users on what the ComfyUI docs call a permitted network environment.

We skip the filter-bypass hacks that circulate on video sites. They breach most platforms' terms, and the output lands in the same perpetual license with your face attached. Our rule: a stylized avatar built from your features, or no face at all. Our guide to making a music video without showing your face covers seven routes.

If a real face appears, it should be yours or belong to someone who signed for it, and a photography release does not cover AI animation. Elser AI's August 3, 2026 guidance lists what a usable release now covers: source images, AI generation and editing, motion transfer, voice synthesis, scene types, channels, territory, duration, storage and revocation. About thirty-five US states recognize a right of publicity, per Devlin Law Firm's February 27, 2026 analysis. The person whose face you borrow has a claim against you, not against ByteDance.

Is it safe to upload your song files?

If you wrote and recorded the song, uploading the finished master is the lowest-risk thing you can do on Seedance. You own it, the license you grant is non-exclusive, and a released track is already public. Do not upload stems, unreleased demos, or any recording that contains a sample, a co-writer's share, or a label's master you do not control.

A perpetual license over a released, registered song costs you little. Your unreleased hook, dry vocal stem or multitrack session is a different thing: those are the assets a sounds-like dispute or a leaked training set would hurt.

Songs you did not write carry another risk. Elser AI's guidance is blunt: a commercial track used to guide timing can implicate reproduction and derivative-use rights, and asking for something that feels like a known track can produce output that resembles protected music. Use your own composition, licensed production music, a click track, or audio a client has authorized in writing, and remove any unlicensed guide track from the final export.

Expect a quality limit too. Seedance 2.0 treats reference audio as a guide, not a bed, and often generates its own music. If your track was replaced, our fix guide for Seedance reference audio not working covers the audio tagging and the lip-sync pass.

What should you upload to Seedance, and what should you keep off it?

Upload finished, released work you own, plus original art and avatars. Keep unreleased audio, multitracks, other people's faces, client files without paperwork, and anything pulled from a film or another artist's video off the platform.

Does the Hollywood dispute put you at risk?

Not directly. The cease-and-desist letters and the Senate pressure in February and March 2026 targeted ByteDance's training data and the model's ability to produce studio characters. No individual creator has been named, and as of August 7, 2026 there was no filed US lawsuit and no settlement.

The sequence, per Morphed's timeline and Devlin's analysis: Seedance 2.0 launched on February 12, 2026 with clips of up to 15 seconds. Six rights-holders and the Motion Picture Association sent letters within eight days, and two senators asked for a shutdown on March 16, 2026. ByteDance announced C2PA Content Credentials and content filters on March 30, and Seedance 2.5 launched publicly on July 31, 2026 with those safeguards on and clips of up to 30 seconds. On August 18, 2026 the MPA and ByteDance signed a voluntary memorandum of understanding covering Dreamina and CapCut, as Tech Jacks Solutions reported, without disclosing its technical requirements.

What this means for you is mostly labeling. Seedance 2.5 output ships with visible watermarks and C2PA provenance metadata, so platforms that read Content Credentials can flag the clip as AI-generated. Do not present it as filmed footage, and do not strip the credential. The only other way to inherit the studios' problem is to prompt for their characters, which the filters refuse.

Is your money safe on Dreamina?

Your card is handled by payment providers, and we found no breach reports. The complaints are about billing, not security: credits lost on failed generations without refunds, and cancellation loops. Dreamina's Trustpilot page showed 27 reviews and a 1.6 out of 5 score when we checked on October 21, 2026.

Twenty-seven reviews is a small, self-selected sample, but the pattern matches what the terms allow. Accounts can be suspended with or without notice, credits are consumed per attempt, and the current offer is a first-month discount that converts to a recurring monthly membership (90 percent off, $1.50, when we checked on October 21, 2026).

Our money rules: pay monthly, never annual, until you have shipped three videos you are happy with, and put the renewal date in your calendar the day you subscribe. Download every clip the day you make it, because the retention period is undefined. If you need out, our Seedance cancellation guide covers the web and iOS paths and leftover credits.

So is Seedance safe enough for us to keep using it?

Yes, with a short checklist. It is our current pick for 30-second, audio-synced clips, and Dreamina's delete-after-generation promise is the most specific of the three privacy policies we read. We just never hand it anything we would be uncomfortable seeing in a stranger's video.

  1. Release or register the song first, then upload the finished master with metadata stripped.
  2. Build a stylized avatar instead of uploading a real face. If a real person appears, get a release that names AI generation.
  3. Audit every reference slot, not just the prompt. If you cannot name who owns a file, it does not go in.
  4. Planning to monetize? Get your plan's commercial terms in writing before you publish, because section 7 bars commercial use unless separate terms allow it.
  5. Download each clip the day it renders. Delete the upload from your library when the project is done.
  6. Keep the C2PA credential and the watermark. Label the video as AI on every platform that asks.
  7. Re-read the terms whenever the last-updated date changes.

If the perpetual license to other users is a deal-breaker, Kling is the honest alternative: its grant is limited in time and territory and revocable by email, though it still covers training. If everything must stay on your own hardware, no cloud model passes that test, Seedance included.

Frequently asked questions

Does Seedance train on the videos I make?+

Possibly. The Dreamina privacy policy (April 30, 2026) lists model training as a purpose, though its European legal-basis table ties training to account, usage, technical and location data rather than your uploads. The terms still license everything you upload for any purpose. No opt-out toggle is described; users in the EEA and UK can object to processing based on legitimate interests. Upload only finished work.

Can I delete my song or face from Dreamina after generating?+

You can delete assets from your library, and the privacy policy gives you the right to request access to or deletion of your data by contacting the company from your account email. Deletion does not revoke the license you granted under the terms, which is written to survive termination. Delete anyway. It still reduces what sits on the servers and what could leak.

Is Seedance safer than Kling or Runway for uploads?+

It depends on which risk you weigh. Dreamina's license reaches other users for every asset, published or not, and cannot be revoked. Runway's terms (September 15, 2026) say outright that your inputs train its models. Kling's license (April 21, 2026) also covers training but is limited in time and territory and revocable by email. Dreamina's delete-after-generation promise is the most specific of the three privacy policies we read.

Will Seedance accept a photo of my own face?+

Often not. Since the safeguards announced on March 30, 2026, the model restricts video made from images that contain recognizable real faces, and we found no verified-likeness option in the consumer Dreamina app as of October 21, 2026. BytePlus offers verified likeness to enterprise clients outside the United States, and ComfyUI partner nodes offer a liveness-checked route where permitted. Is uploading a song I did not write to Seedance illegal?+

It can infringe. Using a commercial recording as a timing or style reference may implicate reproduction and derivative rights, and Dreamina's terms let the company disclose your identity to a rights-holder who complains. Use your own composition, licensed production music or a click track. If the song is yours but has co-writers or a label, get written permission before you upload it.

Does the MPA agreement with ByteDance change anything for musicians?+

Not yet in any visible way. The August 18, 2026 memorandum covers Dreamina and CapCut, but neither party published the technical measures it requires. Watch the last-updated dates on the Dreamina terms and privacy policy. When the agreement produces real rules, they will show up there first, most likely as new restrictions on what you can upload as a reference.
