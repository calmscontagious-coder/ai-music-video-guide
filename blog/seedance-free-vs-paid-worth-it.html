What do you actually get on the Seedance free plan?

As of July 2026, the free tier gives you exactly one generation credit, refreshing every 24 hours. Every free render carries a watermark, and the output is not licensed for commercial use. Independent pricing teardowns also report queue waits of 15–30 minutes at peak times for free users.

Call it what it is: a window onto the interface, not a production tier. Used that way, it's genuinely valuable. A week of daily credits adds up to 7, still short of the 48 the shortest Seedance 2.5 clip costs, so use that week to write prompts for the camera moves, lighting words, and pacing that suit your song, and your first paid month will burn far fewer credits on retries. Our free credits guide walks through that exact drill.

Billed monthly: Basic $19.90 for 200 credits a month, Pro $39.90 for 500, Max $69.90 for 1,050 and Pro Max $99.90 for 2,100. Billed annually, the per-month rates are Basic $9.90, Pro $19.90, Max $34.90 and Pro Max $59.90, with the full year of credits (1,800, 4,200, 9,000 and 18,000) issued upfront. Confirm the current numbers on the official pricing page before you buy. Ignore lookalike sites advertising $30 Standard tiers or $12 Mini plans; some even claim the free plan is watermark-free with commercial rights included (a claim made on seedance.tv, which we deliberately do not link to), which is flatly wrong on both counts.

How many credits does a full music video actually take?

More than any plan's marketing implies, because a 3–4 minute video is assembled from short segments and every second is billed, including takes you throw away. Seedance 2.0 generates up to 15 seconds per clip, and its Video Extension feature chains 4–15 second segments into longer sequences. Seedance 2.5, launched July 31, 2026, renders 4 to 30 seconds per clip in a single pass (Source: ByteDance Seed, July 31, 2026; Flick, July 2026), and on seeddance.video any whole number of seconds in that range (seeddance.video Seedance 2.5 page, read October 25, 2026). A 3.5-minute video is 210 seconds: roughly fourteen 15-second segments on 2.0, or seven 30-second ones on 2.5. On 2.5 the bill is 12 credits a second at 480p, 23 at 720p and 48 at 1080p, so 210 seconds at 480p is 2,520 credits before retries.

Then come the multipliers. Retries burn real money, because a take you reject costs the same as a keeper. Failed generations are a separate case: seeddance.video's Seedance 2.0 and 2.5 pages both say failed renders are refunded automatically (seeddance.video Seedance 2.5 page, read October 25, 2026), which contradicts the "every failed generation costs full credits" line in Cutout.pro's pricing teardown. Cutout.pro also reports that enabling audio generation adds another 50–100%. For musicians, that audio toggle is waste: you already have a finished track, and Seedance accepts excerpts of it as reference audio (2 to 15 seconds per file on 2.0, per Morphic's July 2026 guide; up to 30 seconds combined on 2.5), so you can time your cuts to it in the edit without paying for generated audio.

The credit-efficient workflow: test prompts at 4–5 seconds and 720p, keep audio off, and render finals-only at 1080p. Before choosing a plan, run your song length and retry appetite through our credit calculator, then read how to make AI videos longer than 10 seconds and our character consistency guide — face drift between segments is where most retry budgets die.

One worked example for a full song: a 4–5 minute video typically needs 16–30 keeper clips, and real projects budget a 30–40% retry allowance on top — call it 21–42 generations end to end. In credits, a 4-minute video is 240 seconds, or 2,880 credits at 480p on Seedance 2.5 before retries. No monthly plan covers that in one billing month (monthly Pro Max issues 2,100), while annual Pro's 4,200 upfront credits cover one such video plus a 30 to 40 percent retry allowance.

Which Seedance plan should you get?

For one full music video, the per-second math matters more than the plan name: a month of monthly Pro ($39.90, 500 credits) renders about 41 seconds of Seedance 2.5 at 480p, so a single month only covers a short loop or teaser. Ranked by use case:

  1. Basic ($19.90 billed monthly, or $9.90/mo billed annually): the smallest pool. 200 credits a month is about 16 seconds of Seedance 2.5 at 480p; the annual 1,800 credits cover one short lyric video with little room for retries.
  2. Pro ($39.90 billed monthly, or $19.90/mo billed annually): annual Pro's 4,200 upfront credits are the smallest subscription pool that covers one full 3–4 minute video at 480p with retries. Run your song length and retry appetite through our credit calculator before you buy.
  3. Max ($69.90 billed monthly, or $34.90/mo billed annually): heavy iteration: multiple visual concepts, aggressive retakes, or a perfectionist streak.
  4. Pro Max ($99.90 billed monthly, or $59.90/mo billed annually): several videos a month or paid client work. Overkill for a single release.

One structural catch: the official pricing FAQ says unused subscription credits roll forward only while the subscription stays active (seeddance.video/pricing, read October 25, 2026), which contradicts the expire-each-cycle claim in Cutout.pro's teardown. Cancel and they stay usable only to the end of the billing period, so render before you cancel. Refunds cover subscriptions within 14 days if no credits were used and unused credit packs within 7 days; used credits are not refundable (refund policy, read October 25, 2026). When is the free tier enough — and when should you not upgrade?

An honest pricing guide has to say when to keep your money. Don't upgrade if:

  • Your song isn't finished. Seedance uses excerpts of your track as reference audio; generating visuals for a demo you'll re-record wastes every credit.
  • You're still storyboarding. The free daily credit (1, against 48 for the shortest clip) won't cover concept tests, so storyboard on paper and pay only when you're ready to produce.
  • You only need private feedback clips. Watermarked renders are fine for showing a manager or bandmate a direction.
  • You release one single a year and a static visualizer serves you. A $0 looping visual can be the right call — that isn't a failure.
  • You own a GPU with 8GB+ of VRAM and like tinkering. Open-source models like WAN, HunyuanVideo, and LTX-Video run locally for free — WAN and LTX-Video reportedly run on 8GB cards, while HunyuanVideo wants 16–24GB for best results. The trade: output quality trails the frontier tools, there's no music-track reference input, and setup is measured in evenings. If a weekend of ComfyUI debugging sounds worse than $19.90, that's your answer.

Subscription fatigue is real on an indie budget already split across plugins and distribution — and across the AI-tools category broadly, commercial rights tend to sit behind paid tiers. So reframe it: this is a per-project cost, not another standing monthly bill.

Can't you just remove the watermark from free renders?

You can find tools that scrub the pixels; none of them adds the missing commercial license. A free-tier render stays unlicensed for commercial use no matter how clean it looks. Stripping the mark carries its own risk, too: it can violate the platform's terms, model-provider rules, and AI-disclosure laws, and because watermarks generally qualify as copyright-management information, knowingly removing them can trigger statutory damages under U.S. copyright law (17 U.S.C. §1202) — plus demonetization or strike risk on monetized channels. One month of Basic ($19.90 billed monthly) is cheaper than one copyright strike: see our YouTube monetization guide and the clean-license options in our no-watermark roundup.

Should you pay for Kling or Veo instead?

It depends on what you value most. Seedance accepts excerpts of your track as reference audio, which makes timing your cuts to the song far easier in the edit. Kling 3.0 extends a clip to roughly 3 minutes of continuous runtime, Kling 4.0 renders 3 to 30 seconds per clip (Source: Kling, September 30, 2026), and Veo 3.1 generates 4–8 second clips extendable to about 148 seconds. If unbroken runtime matters more to you than working against your own track, Kling is the honest alternative — our head-to-head comparison and pricing comparison across generators cover that math.

The honest verdict

The free tier is a look around, not a studio: 1 credit a day won't render a clip, so learn your song's visual language on paper and keep anything unlicensed out of a commercial release. When the song is finished and the storyboard is set, buy the credit pool that covers your song's seconds at your resolution (for a full video at 480p that is annual Pro or a large one-time pack, not one month of a monthly plan), start on the official site, render the whole project, and cancel before your renewal date. That's the entire strategy, and it costs a fraction of what a filmed indie video would.

Frequently asked questions

Is Seedance free to use?+

Barely. The free tier gives 1 credit every 24 hours (pricing page, read October 25, 2026), and the shortest Seedance 2.5 render, 4 seconds at 480p, costs 48 credits, so it lets you explore the interface but not render. Watermark-free renders and a commercial license require paid credits: Basic is $19.90 billed monthly or $9.90 a month billed annually, and one-time packs start at $29.90.

Does the Seedance free plan allow commercial use?+

No. Free-tier renders are watermarked and not licensed for commercial use, so you cannot legally use them in a monetized music video, ad, or release campaign. A commercial license comes only with paid credits ($9.90 to $59.90 a month on annual billing, or $19.90 to $99.90 billed monthly, as of October 25, 2026). Removing the watermark with a third-party tool does not add the missing license and can violate platform terms, AI-disclosure laws, and U.S. copyright-management-information rules.

Is Seedance Pro worth it for a music video?+

On annual billing, yes for most independent musicians: annual Pro ($19.90 a month, 4,200 credits upfront) covers one full 3 to 4 minute video at 480p on Seedance 2.5 with a retry allowance. Billed monthly, Pro is $39.90 for 500 credits, about 41 seconds of 2.5 at 480p, which won't cover a full song in one month. Basic ($19.90 monthly, 200 credits) suits tests and short loops; Max ($69.90 monthly or $34.90 a month annually) only makes sense if you iterate heavily across multiple visual concepts.

How many credits does a Seedance music video cost?+

It depends on song length, resolution and retries, because credits are billed per second. On the reseller we link, Seedance 2.5 costs 12 credits a second at 480p, 23 at 720p and 48 at 1080p, so a 3.5-minute (210-second) video is about 2,520 credits at 480p before retries. Seedance 2.5 renders 4 to 30 seconds per clip, so that is at least seven segments; Seedance 2.0 tops out at 15 seconds. The platform says failed renders are refunded automatically; takes you reject are not. Drafting at 480p and rendering finals at a higher resolution keeps totals down. Run your own numbers through a credit calculator before picking a plan.

Do Seedance credits roll over to the next month?+

Yes, while the subscription stays active. The official pricing FAQ (read October 25, 2026) says unused monthly credits roll forward indefinitely as long as you stay subscribed, annual plans issue the full year of credits upfront, and one-time packs stay valid for roughly ten years. Some third-party teardowns say monthly credits expire each cycle; the platform's own page says otherwise. If you cancel, credits stay usable until the end of the current billing period, so render first.

What is the cheapest way to use Seedance for a music video?+

Plan the shots on paper first, since the free daily credit (1) won't render a clip. Then buy the smallest pool that covers your song's seconds: a 3-minute video at 480p on Seedance 2.5 is about 2,160 credits before retries, which annual Basic (1,800 upfront) can't cover and annual Pro (4,200) covers with room for retries; a one-time Pro Max pack is 3,000 credits for $149.90. Draft short and at 480p, render finals at a higher resolution, and cancel before renewal once the video is done.
