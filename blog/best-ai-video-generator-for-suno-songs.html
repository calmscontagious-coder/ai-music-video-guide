Quick answer: In October 2026 the best AI video generator for Suno songs is Seedance 2.5 on Dreamina. It accepts your track as a reference and renders 30 seconds a pass; no other model here does both. Kling 4.0 lists higher resolution but syncs singing only through a separate Lip Sync tool. Suno v6 landed on September 9, 2026, and Suno said it would retire its older models as v6 rolls out. We make our own songs in Suno and cut videos for them, so this guide to where the song goes next is written from the workflow, not the brochure.

One honest framing first. None of the five model makers below makes a three-minute video in one pass. Each produces clips, and the song gets assembled in an editor afterward. The difference is how much of the sync work the generator does for you. One reseller's whole-song tool gets its own section.

What does "works with Suno" mean for a video tool?

A tool works with Suno when it can take the exported song as an input, hold a shot long enough to cover a verse or a hook, and stay out of the way of your audio. Price per 30 seconds matters after those three tests, not before them.

Suno gives you audio. v6 can start a song from text, audio, images or video, but it outputs a WAV or MP3. So the first test is whether the video tool has a button that accepts that file. If yes, the model can time motion and mouths to the real track. If no, you sync in an editor.

The second test is length per pass. A chorus often runs 15 to 25 seconds, so a model that stops at 8 seconds forces three generations per chorus and two seams.

The third test is the generator's own audio. Veo 3.1, Kling 4.0 and Seedance 2.5 all generate sound, and for a Suno song that sound is noise you mute unless the model takes your track instead.

Why is Seedance 2.5 our pick for most Suno songs?

Because it is the one generator built to listen. ByteDance's July 31, 2026 launch notes list up to 30 images, 10 video clips and 10 audio clips as references in a single pass, 30-second clips, multi-round extension and timestamp-level edits. For a sung track, that is the feature list you want.

The practical loop looks like this. Export the song from Suno. Cut the section you want, a hook or a verse, to 30 seconds or under. Open Seedance 2.5 in Dreamina, add the clip as an audio reference, add a character image, and describe the shot. Dreamina's own example prompt pairs an @Audio 1 reference with an instruction for every subject to lip-sync while singing, so ask for the sync in words. Results vary from take to take; if your track is not changing the output, read why Seedance ignores a reference audio file. Dreamina's model page also says 2.5 removes random subtitles and unwanted background music.

Where it falls down. Native resolution is 480p or 720p on the BytePlus API product page, with durations of 4 to 30 seconds. Dreamina advertises 4K, and its own Seedance 2.5 pricing guide lists HD Upscale as a separate post-generation step, so treat 4K as an upscale and budget credits for it. ByteDance also says the physics of complex motion and scenes with several interacting subjects still need work. Keep choreography to one body per clip.

Then there is the price churn. A 90 percent first-month offer ended October 9, 2026. On October 21 Dreamina's pricing page showed 98 percent off: $0.29 for the first month of Basic paid by card or PayPal, $15 by other methods, renewing at $15 a month with 1,575 credits. It says the offer ends October 30, and dates plan discounts October 10 to 23. The same page lists Advanced billed yearly at Seedance 2.5 from $0.08 a second, so from $2.40 for 30 seconds. After the first month, Basic credits cost more per second than Advanced; we worked the per-clip numbers in our Kling 4.0 versus Seedance 2.5 comparison.

When is Kling 4.0 the better choice for a Suno song?

When the picture matters more than the sync. Kling 4.0, public since October 15, 2026, renders up to 30 seconds a pass with two-channel stereo, but its release notes list reference audio as voice only, so a Suno song cannot drive the generation. You cut to the song in an editor, or run the clip through Kling's Lip Sync tool afterward.

Kling's 4.0 release notes list 720p, 1080p and 4K output, 3 to 30 seconds per generation, up to 10 keyframes and up to 15 reference assets. The same notes mark 10-bit HDR at 1080p and 4K as coming soon, and the October 16, 2026 Kuaishou release says 4.0 will also support 4K and 1080p, so check the resolution menu before you plan around 4K. Kling says extension up to two minutes is coming soon.

The singing problem has a workaround. Kling's Lip Sync page says you can upload a singing track and apply it to a supported character video, trimming the audio if it runs longer than the clip. It is a paid step; credit use depends on video length and shows before you confirm.

Credits: Kling's July 28, 2026 credit guide prices 4K on the 3.0 series at 30 credits a second, and bought credits run $1 for 66 under the credits policy, so 30 seconds of 4K is 900 credits, about $13.64 if you buy them. Kling has not published a 4.0 rate in the pages we read; the live generation panel shows it. Kling's July 3, 2026 guide shows Standard at $6.99 for the first month with 660 credits, not enough for one 30-second 4K clip; Pro is $25.99 with 3,000.

Can Google Flow Music or Veo 3.1 make a video for a Suno song?

Flow Music can, Veo 3.1 alone cannot. Google's help page for Flow Music's Music videos tab lists an Add then Audio option for using your own music, and Gemini Omni Flash directs those videos. Veo 3.1 takes text and images only and always generates its own soundtrack.

Flow still runs Veo 3.1. Google's May 19, 2026 blog post brought Gemini Omni Flash, a video model you direct in turns, to Flow and Flow Music, and says music video creation with it is available to all Google AI subscribers. The Flow Music help page lists the steps, including Add then Audio for your music.

Clip length and cost sit in Google's Flow credits table: Omni Flash makes clips of 4, 6, 8 or 10 seconds, and a 10-second clip at 720p costs 15 credits, so the 50 free daily credits cover three 10-second clips. The Omni Flash API extends a clip 3 to 10 seconds at a time and outputs 720p by default, with 1080p as an upscale, so plan for short pieces and seams. Google's October 1, 2026 API pricing page puts Omni Flash at about $0.10 a second of 720p and Veo 3.1 Standard at $0.40.

Veo 3.1 itself is a clip tool. The developer docs, updated September 5, 2026, list 4, 6 or 8 second clips (8 only at 1080p or 4K), audio always on, and 7-second extensions up to 148 seconds at 720p. You mute the footage and cut it to the track by hand.

Subscriptions add monthly Flow credits: 1,000 with Google AI Pro, $19.99 a month on Google's plans page, 10,000 with Ultra $100 and 25,000 with Ultra $200.

Where does Runway fit for Suno videos?

Runway is the one-subscription answer. Its plans include Gen-4.5 and hosted Kling 3.0 on every paid tier, hosted Seedance 2.5 on Pro and Max, and no watermarks from Standard up. It does not list your own track as a generation input.

Runway's pricing page, checked October 21, 2026: Standard is $15 a month or $12 billed annually for 625 credits, which Runway states as 52 seconds of Gen-4.5. Pro is $35 or $28 annually for 2,250 credits; Max is $95 or $76 annually for 9,500 credits with one month of rollover. Pick Runway if you want several models under one bill and already edit there; skip it if the point is the audio reference.

What about a tool that takes the whole song?

Seeddance, a separate site that hosts several video models under one credit system, has a Music to Video tool that takes your entire track and returns one performance video the same length. It is the only option in this guide that skips clip-by-clip assembly, but the model behind it is not named and the lip-sync quality is the vendor's claim.

Seeddance's Music to Video tool accepts an MP3, WAV or M4A file from 5 seconds to 10 minutes, up to 50MB, and returns a lip-synced performance video that runs the length of the track. It bills 4 credits a second at 720p and 2 at 480p, and refunds failed renders. Basic is $9.90 a month billed annually for 150 credits a month, which is $0.066 a credit and about 37 seconds of 720p. The page credits vocal analysis for the lip sync; we have not measured it.

Seeddance is not Dreamina. It also hosts Seedance 2.5, at 23 credits a second at 720p, which is 690 credits or about $45 for 30 seconds at the Basic credit rate, far above the $2.40 starting price on Dreamina. Use Seeddance for a whole-song performance video and Dreamina for Seedance 2.5 clips. Figures from Seeddance's pricing page and tool pages, checked October 21, 2026.

Which tool should you pick for your Suno song?

The right AI video generator for Suno songs depends on what the song needs on screen. A sung performance with a face on camera points to Seedance 2.5, and cinematic footage with no singing points to Kling 4.0 cut in an editor. No budget points to Flow Music's daily credits.

  • A sung hook with a face on camera: Seedance 2.5 on Dreamina with the track as an audio reference. If the picture matters more, Kling 4.0 for the shot, then Kling Lip Sync for the vocal.
  • A full-length performance video from one upload: Seeddance's Music to Video tool, if you accept an unnamed model and test the lip sync on a short clip first.
  • A visualizer or abstract loop you will cut yourself: Kling 4.0 Flash or Veo 3.1 Fast footage, muted, assembled in your editor.
  • Zero budget: Flow Music with the 50 daily Flow credits and your song added through Add then Audio. Expect short pieces.
  • One bill for several models: Runway Pro, which hosts Seedance 2.5 and Kling 3.0 next to Gen-4.5.

Start with one section, not the whole song. Export a 30-second hook from Suno, run it through Seedance 2.5 with the audio reference on, and judge the sync on your own track before you commit to any monthly plan.

Frequently asked questions
