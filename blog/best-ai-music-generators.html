Best AI Music Generators 2026: Suno v6, ACE-Step, MiniMax and More

By the AI Music Video Guide team · Updated October 21, 2026 · 12 min read

Quick answer: Suno is still the best AI music generator for songs you can release, now on its v6 models, at $10 a month for Pro with 20 downloads. Udio still cannot export a file. ElevenLabs Music allows Spotify release from the $22 Creator plan. For free, local generation under commercial-use licenses, try ACE-Step 1.5 or MiniMax Music 3.

Most of this field changed between August and October 2026. Suno capped downloads on September 3, then replaced every model with the v6 family on September 9. Udio is a year into a download freeze with no relaunch date. ElevenLabs rewrote its music terms so paid plans can release to streaming. MiniMax Music 3 joined ACE-Step 1.5 as an open-weight model that runs on a home GPU for nothing.

We use these tools to make songs that become music videos, so we score them the way a releasing artist would: a song you cannot export is a demo.

How did we rank the best AI music generators?

We ranked on three questions before sound: can you download the audio, can you release it commercially, and what does each kept song cost. Every price and term below comes from the vendor's own pages, read on October 21, 2026.

That order matters more this year. Suno meters downloads, Udio has none, ElevenLabs bills music by the minute, and the open models need your own hardware. Monthly fees alone hide all of that.

Where a vendor's pages disagree, we say so. Where we rely on a secondary source, we name it.

What changed with Suno v6, v6-wild and v6-mini?

On September 9, 2026, Suno replaced every earlier model with three new ones built with Warner Music Group, BMG and Believe: v6 for precision, v6-wild for variety, and v6-mini as the free model. The older v4.5, v5 and v5.5 models are retired, and since September 3 downloads are capped at 20 a month on Pro and 60 on Premier.

Per Suno's v6 announcement (September 9, 2026), v6-mini is the faster model and the only one on Free. Suno's help center says a generation on any of the three can run up to 8 minutes, with Extend for more. Headline features: plain-language edits to one section or lyric, mashups, and songs started from a photo, video or voice memo.

The pricing page as of October 21, 2026: Free is $0 with 50 credits a day, v6-mini only, no monthly downloads and no commercial rights; accounts opened before September 3 keep up to 7 lifetime trial downloads for personal use. Pro is $10 a month ($8 billed yearly) for 2,500 credits, v6 and v6-wild, 20 song downloads a month and commercial use. Premier is $30 a month ($24 yearly) for 10,000 credits, 60 downloads, Suno Studio and three stem-separation types. Credits do not roll over. Suno's downloads policy post (August 10, 2026) confirms the caps apply to songs made before September 3, that Studio exports on Premier are uncapped, and that extra downloads can be bought. Suno publishes no price for those extras; Undetectr's October 4, 2026 comparison reports an in-app figure of $2.99 each.

Which Suno v6 model should you use?

Draft on v6-mini, finish on v6, and switch to v6-wild when v6 sounds flat. That is the pattern in the launch-week threads collected by Undetectr's v6 review (updated September 25, 2026). The consensus: v6 beats v5.5 on fidelity but loses character against v4.5 and v5, and the gap is widest in metal, drum and bass and other genres a licensed catalog covers thinly. Pop, rock and folk users were broadly happy. Long songs reportedly weaken in the back half.

Two more things to know before you build on v6. On September 18, 2026, Sony Music and Universal Music Group filed a new 45-page suit in Massachusetts arguing v6 is still infringing because it was trained in part on outputs of the older models; Variety reported the complaint names 60,202 recordings. And Undetectr found a full C2PA content-credentials manifest inside a v6 WAV export, so the file carries its own provenance. For which distributors accept that, see our guide to which platforms accept AI music.

Is Udio still worth paying for in October 2026?

Not if you want a file. Udio disabled downloads of audio, video and stems on October 29, 2025 after its Universal Music settlement, and apart from a 48-hour rescue window that opened November 3, 2025, downloads have stayed off. You can subscribe, generate and share on udio.com, but you cannot release anywhere else.

Udio's help center confirms the partnership date of October 29, 2025 and the line that downloading remains disabled. The pricing page on October 21, 2026 lists Free at 10 credits a day, Standard at $10 a month ($8 yearly) and Pro at $30 ($24 yearly). Undetectr's October 4 matrix notes Udio is still on its v1.5 Allegro model. No public export terms for the coming UMG platform exist. Our advice from July stands: wait.

Can you release ElevenLabs Music songs to Spotify now?

Yes, from the Creator plan at $22 a month. ElevenLabs' Music Terms, updated October 9, 2026, permit music streaming distribution on Creator, Pro, Scale and Business, and prohibit it on Free and Starter. Film, TV, radio and large studio games need an Enterprise agreement on every self-serve plan.

Our earlier version of this guide said ElevenLabs banned streaming release outright. That rule belongs to the Music Marketplace, where you license other users' songs. The Music Terms and their Exhibit A rights table govern songs you generate yourself. That table also sets monthly limits: Starter (listed at $6) gets 17 minutes of generation and no streaming; Creator ($22) gets 62 minutes of generation, 250 minutes of downloads and streaming permitted; Pro ($99) gets 304 minutes. The same table marks high-quality downloads (lossless WAV or 192 kbps MP3) as not included on Free, Starter or Creator, so check the export format before you send a Creator file to a distributor. The pricing page on October 21, 2026 shows the same plan prices and says Eleven Music draws about 900 credits a minute from the same pool as speech, so voice work shrinks your music budget.

The model is Music v2.5, the default since the September 2026 announcement, which also says the separate ElevenMusic app gives Free users five lossless downloads a day with attribution. Read the terms inside the app you use. ElevenLabs bans naming any artist, songwriter, song, album, publisher or label in a prompt. The upside remains licensed provenance for online video and ad work (film, TV and radio still need Enterprise Music); the downside is that a 4-minute song costs 3,600 credits.

Which open-source AI music models can replace Suno?

None fully, but two are close enough to matter. ACE-Step 1.5 is MIT-licensed, runs in under 4 GB of VRAM and makes full songs with vocals in 50+ languages. MiniMax Music 3 shipped open weights on August 13, 2026 and generates five-minute songs with a commercial license for companies under $20 million in revenue.

ACE-Step 1.5: the permissive pick

The ACE-Step 1.5 repository describes a hybrid language-model-plus-diffusion design that generates a full song in under 2 seconds on an A100 and under 10 seconds on an RTX 3090, with durations from 10 seconds to 10 minutes, LoRA training from about eight songs, covers and repainting. The XL variants with a 4B decoder arrived April 2, 2026 and need 12 GB of VRAM or more. The team's own quality claim is "between Suno v4.5 and Suno v5". A hosted version at acemusic.ai is free as of October 21, 2026. The honest weakness: IT-JIM's May 21, 2026 listening comparison of eight open models found a persistent metallic shimmer on ACE-Step vocals and instruments, and concluded there is still no open-source Suno. It also called ACE-Step the clear choice for fine-tuning and tooling. The MIT license covers the code and weights, not the training data; the README's own disclaimer warns of unintentional copyright infringement and asks you to verify originality.

MiniMax Music 3: open weights from the Hailuo company

MiniMax released Music 3 as open weights on Hugging Face on August 13, 2026, per Kie.ai's release coverage (published August 14, 2026): lyrics plus a structured caption in, a complete song up to five minutes at 32 kHz stereo out. ComfyUI's official workflow notes the Community License allows commercial use below $20 million in yearly revenue. Two cautions. MiniMax's own platform pricing page says that from August 20, 2026 its paid music API is closed to new users and the free music endpoints are discontinued, so the hosted route is the consumer MiniMax Audio app or third parties; fal.ai still sells the older Music 2.0 at $0.03 a generation with no post-generation editing. And the 8B model wants a serious GPU.

Stable Audio 3.0: licensed data, instrumental only

Stability AI's Stable Audio 3.0 announcement covers four models trained on fully licensed data: Small SFX, Small and Medium are open weights, Large runs through the API and the stableaudio.com app. Medium and Large generate past six minutes, the family supports inpainting, and LoRA training is documented for the open Small and Medium weights. Outputs are yours to commercialize under the Community License, with an Enterprise license above $1 million in revenue. The app pricing on October 21, 2026 is Solo $12 (660 credits) up to Studio $199. No vocals, which keeps it in the sound-design and score lane. TechCrunch reported on October 2, 2026 that Sony, Warner and Universal joined a $76 million round in late August and licensed catalogs for training, so this is the open camp the labels chose.

Which instrumental tools are still safe for background music?

AIVA for compositions you want to own, Soundraw for cheap video beds, Mubert for volume. All three are instrumental-only, so none of them suits a song with a singer.

AIVA (prices read October 21, 2026): Free keeps copyright with AIVA and allows 3 downloads a month; Standard at €11 a month billed yearly allows monetization only on YouTube, Twitch, TikTok and Instagram with 15 downloads; Pro at €33 a month billed yearly states the copyright is owned by you, with full monetization, 300 downloads and tracks up to 5:30 in all formats.

Soundraw lists Creator at $5.99, Artist Starter $10.49 (10 downloads), Artist Pro $12.59 (20 downloads, WAV and stems) and Artist Unlimited $17.49; Undetectr's October 4 check records those as the yearly rates, with monthly billing roughly double. Read the license before releasing: Creator is background use only, Artist plans allow Spotify distribution only if you modify the beat first, and Content ID registration is never allowed.

Mubert runs Ambassador (free, 25 generations, 5 downloads, non-commercial), Creator at $14 a month ($11.69 yearly, still non-commercial) and Pro at $39 ($32.49 yearly) with commercial use. Tracks run up to 25 minutes. Per Undetectr's October 4, 2026 reading of Mubert's terms, no plan allows streaming-service release or Content ID.

Who owns an AI-generated song in the US?

You own the human parts. The US Copyright Office's Part 2 report, published January 29, 2025 and still the operative guidance on copyright.gov, holds that purely AI-generated material lacks human authorship and that prompts alone do not make you the author. Lyrics you wrote, melodies you composed and your arrangement choices can be registered; the generated audio is disclaimed.

That is why a vendor's "commercial rights" and a copyright are different things. Suno's paid plans, ElevenLabs' terms and AIVA Pro all grant you rights in your output, and none of those grants can create protection US law does not recognize. Write your own lyrics; that is the moat.

Frequently asked questions

Is Suno or Udio better in October 2026?+

Suno, because you can leave with the file. Udio disabled all downloads on October 29, 2025 after settling with Universal Music, and apart from a 48-hour window in November 2025 they have stayed off, so its songs stay on udio.com. Suno's paid tiers still allow downloads, capped at 20 a month on Pro and 60 on Premier, with commercial rights on every downloaded song.

What is the difference between Suno v6, v6-wild and v6-mini?+

All three launched September 9, 2026. v6 is the flagship for Pro and Premier, built for precise, consistent results from a clear prompt. v6-wild, also paid-only, is less predictable and meant for exploration. v6-mini is the faster model every account gets, including Free. Each generates up to 8 minutes per run, and every earlier Suno model has been retired.

How many songs can you download from Suno each month?+

Since September 3, 2026, Pro includes 20 song downloads a month and Premier 60, with extra downloads sold separately; Suno publishes no price, and a reported in-app figure is $2.99 each. Free accounts get no monthly downloads. Re-downloading a song or pulling its stems does not use another allowance, and Premier exports from Suno Studio are uncapped.

Can you put ElevenLabs Music songs on Spotify?+

Yes, from the Creator plan at $22 a month. ElevenLabs' Music Terms, updated October 9, 2026, permit streaming distribution on Creator, Pro, Scale and Business and prohibit it on Free and Starter. Film, TV, radio and large studio games need an Enterprise agreement. The separate Music Marketplace still bans streaming for licensed tracks, which is a different product.

Is there a free, open-source alternative to Suno?+

Two worth trying. ACE-Step 1.5 is MIT-licensed, runs in under 4 GB of VRAM, makes songs with vocals up to 10 minutes and has a free hosted version at acemusic.ai. MiniMax Music 3 shipped open weights on August 13, 2026, generates five-minute songs and allows commercial use under $20 million in revenue. Neither matches Suno's vocal polish yet.

Do you own the copyright to AI-generated music?+

Not the AI-generated audio itself. The US Copyright Office's January 2025 report holds that purely AI-generated material lacks human authorship and that prompts alone do not make you the author. You can register the parts you wrote, such as lyrics, melody and arrangement, and disclaim the rest. A vendor's commercial-use grant is a license, not a copyright.
