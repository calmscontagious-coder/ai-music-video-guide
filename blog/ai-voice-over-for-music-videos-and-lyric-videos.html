What does an AI voice-over add to a music video or lyric video?

A spoken layer the song itself doesn't carry: a channel intro, a narrated bridge that sets up the lyric, a trailer voice for a teaser, or a short "why we wrote this" track at the top of a lyric video. The pages we read cover narration and video projects; they don't describe the voices singing, so this is speech only.

If you still need the video itself, our guides to making a lyric video with AI and turning a song into a music video cover that side.

How do you write a script that an AI voice reads well?

Write it as spoken lines, not as lyrics, and control delivery with audio tags inside the text: the text to speech page lists [softly], [whispers], [hesitant], [excited] and [laughs warmly] as examples. Punctuation shapes pacing, and a pronunciation dictionary or IPA fixes how the voice says a band name (ElevenLabs text to speech page, read November 13, 2026).

Step 1: draft the lines in plain prose

Say the lines out loud first. A trailer line that reads well on a page often runs long when spoken, and a lyric-video intro only has to carry one idea: who you are, what the song is, why it exists.

Then check the length against the model's cap per request. The docs give Eleven v4 a 10,000 character limit, Eleven v3 5,000 characters, Multilingual v2 10,000 characters and Flash v2.5 40,000 characters (ElevenLabs models docs, read November 13, 2026). The product page gives Eleven v4 5,000 characters in its model section and 10,000 characters in its FAQ, so the two pages disagree (ElevenLabs text to speech page, read November 13, 2026). A one-minute intro sits well under either number.

Step 2: add audio tags, punctuation and pronunciations

Audio tags are square-bracket cues written into the script; the product page's examples include [softly], [laughs warmly], [sighs contentedly], [laughs], [whispers], [hesitant] and [excited] (ElevenLabs text to speech page, read November 13, 2026). A "story behind the song" track might open [softly] and shift to [excited] at the chorus. The pages do not say which models read the tags as cues, so test one short tagged line on Multilingual v2 or Flash before relying on them there.

Two voice settings sit next to the tags, stability and similarity, and punctuation shapes pacing, so a comma is the cheapest way to add a breath before the beat drops (ElevenLabs text to speech page, read November 13, 2026). For a band name, the page points to IPA or pronunciation dictionaries; set those before the full script, not after a three-minute pass comes out wrong.

Should you pick a library voice or design your own?

Pick from the library when a narration or ad-read voice will do: it filters by use case (Narration, Ads, Characters, Conversational, Social Media). Design one from a text prompt when you want a voice no other channel uses; Voice Design returns three options per prompt (ElevenLabs voice library page and Voice Design guide, read November 13, 2026).

Step 3: filter the library by use case

The library's size is stated inconsistently: "10,000+ studio quality voices" on the voice library page, "17,000+ voices" on a button and "17,500+ voices" in the FAQ of the text to speech page (ElevenLabs voice library and text to speech pages, read November 13, 2026). The filtering is the useful part. Accent filters include British, Australian and Indian English plus Mexican and Castilian Spanish, under "90+ languages and accents", and the accent comes from the voice you chose (ElevenLabs voice library and text to speech pages, read November 13, 2026).

The library page doesn't define a quality label ("Studio-Quality Conversational Voices" is a collection name, not a tier), and it doesn't state commercial-use terms or plan requirements for library voices (ElevenLabs voice library page, read November 13, 2026).

Step 4: or design a voice from a prompt

Voice Design "lets you create a unique voice from a text prompt". The path is Voices, then My Voices, Add a new voice, Voice Design. The prompt can name age, gender, accent, tone, pacing, emotion and audio quality, and "When you hit generate, we'll generate three voice options for you" (ElevenLabs Voice Design guide, read November 13, 2026).

The only charge is the credits for your preview text, charged once even though three samples are made, and saving a result uses one voice slot (ElevenLabs Voice Design guide, read November 13, 2026). Free has 3 custom voice slots, Starter 10, Creator 30 and Pro 160 (ElevenLabs pricing page, read November 13, 2026). Designed voices work with Eleven v4 and earlier models, though they may be less performative on v4 (ElevenLabs Voice Design guide, read November 13, 2026).

A third route is your own voice: the pricing page lists Instant Voice Cloning from Starter and a professional clone from Creator, and the docs guide says that even with their consent you cannot clone someone else's voice (ElevenLabs pricing page and voice cloning docs guide, read November 13, 2026).

Which model should you choose for a music video voice-over?

Eleven v4 for an emotive read, Multilingual v2 for a long stable one, Flash v2.5 for fast cheap drafts. The docs' selection guide names eleven_v4 or eleven_multilingual_v2 for video narration, and lists eleven_turbo_v2_5 as deprecated, "outclassed by Flash models", so use eleven_flash_v2_5 instead (ElevenLabs models docs, read November 13, 2026).

How do you generate the audio and place it over the video?

Generate in the text to speech editor, download the MP3 and drop it into the editor that holds your cut. Or open a Studio Video project, upload the footage to its Library, drag the clip to the timeline, align the narration and export as video (ElevenLabs text to speech page and Studio guide, read November 13, 2026).

Step 6: generate, listen, download

Downloads default to MP3; more formats come from the History tab or the API (ElevenLabs text to speech page, read November 13, 2026).

Text to speech costs 1 credit per character on the V2 Multilingual models and the pricing page equates 10k credits with about 10 minutes of speech (ElevenLabs pricing page, read November 13, 2026). By our arithmetic at that rate, a 60-second spoken intro is about 1,000 credits, and a 3-minute narrated intro-plus-outro for a lyric video is about 3,000 credits. Those are our estimates for a Multilingual v2 pass, not vendor figures: the page gives no credit rate for Eleven v4, and a regenerated line is charged again.

Step 7: or build it in Studio over the footage

Studio's "Video project (New)" opens a video editor with Library, Script and Captions panels and a Studio Agent. To narrate over footage you already have, the guide says "Add a video track to pair narration with existing footage or B-roll": select Upload in the Library, drag the clip to the timeline and align the narration against it (ElevenLabs Studio guide, read November 13, 2026).

A single-chapter project exports as MP3 or WAV, and "If the project contains a video track or captions, you can also export it as video"; export quality depends on your plan (ElevenLabs Studio guide, read November 13, 2026). The Studio docs list a maximum of 500 projects on Free and 750 on Starter, while the pricing page lists 3 and 20; the pages do not say why they differ, so plan around the pricing page numbers (ElevenLabs Studio guide and pricing page, read November 13, 2026).

Do you need a YouTube AI label for an AI voice-over?

Not for your own cloned voice: YouTube lists "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs" among edits needing no disclosure. It asks for disclosure of realistic AI content that "Creates music that's the main focus of the video" or makes a real person appear to say something they didn't (YouTube Help disclosure page, read November 13, 2026).

The other two triggers are altered footage of a real event or place and a realistic scene that didn't occur; non-realistic content and minor edits such as beauty filters and caption creation need no disclosure (YouTube Help disclosure page, read November 13, 2026).

That leaves a gap the page does not address by name: a library or designed voice reading your script. It is neither your own cloned voice nor a real person made to say something they didn't, and the page does not say either way. The four triggers are the test, and if the song under it is AI-generated and the main focus, the music rule applies whatever the voice. For the ad-revenue side, see monetizing AI music videos on YouTube.

The switch lives in YouTube Studio at upload, in the Attributes section under "AI use", where you tap Yes or No. If you select Yes, YouTube adds a label, on the player or in the expanded description depending on the content, and it may also apply labels automatically (YouTube Help disclosure page, read November 13, 2026).

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a free AI voice-over in a monetized music video?

Not under the free plan of the voice tool we link. Its product page says commercial use is available on any paid plan, while the free plan covers personal, non-commercial use and requires attribution. The cheapest paid plan is $6 a month with 30k credits, and paid plans include commercial rights for YouTube videos with no extra royalties.

How many credits does a 60-second spoken intro use?

By our arithmetic, about 1,000 credits. The pricing page charges 1 credit per character on the V2 Multilingual models and equates 10k credits with about 10 minutes of speech, so a minute is roughly 1,000 credits. That is our estimate from the page's rate for the V2 Multilingual models, not a vendor figure; the page gives no credit rate for the newest model, and every regenerated line costs again.

Which model is best for an emotional story-behind-the-song narration?

The docs point to the newest model for emotive long-form narration and to Multilingual v2 as the most stable on long generations. Draft on the fast, lower-cost Flash model to check timing against your cut, then run the final pass on the emotive model with audio tags such as [softly] or [excited] written into the script.

Do I have to tell YouTube the voice-over is AI?

Not if it is a clone of your own voice; YouTube lists cloning your own voice for voice overs or dubs among edits that need no disclosure. A label is required when AI music is the main focus of the video or when a real person is made to appear to say something they didn't. You answer under AI use in the Attributes section at upload.

Can the AI voice pronounce my band name correctly?

Usually only after you tell it how. The product page points to IPA or pronunciation dictionaries for brand names, so set the pronunciation before generating the full script. Punctuation shapes pacing as well, so a comma before the name gives it a beat. Generate one short test line first rather than paying credits for a full three-minute pass.

Can I put the narration straight over the video without a separate editor?

Yes, in the Studio video project of the tool we link. You upload existing footage to its Library, drag the clip to the timeline and align the narration against it, then export as video if the project has a video track or captions. Single-chapter audio projects export as MP3 or WAV, and export quality depends on your plan.
