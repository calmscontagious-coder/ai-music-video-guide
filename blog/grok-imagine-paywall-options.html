Grok Imagine Isn't Free Anymore: Your Real Options After the March 2026 Paywall

By the AI Music Video Guide teamUpdated October 25, 20267 min read

Quick answer: No — Grok Imagine is no longer free in any meaningful way. xAI removed free image and video generation on March 19, 2026; free accounts now get at most a small credit trickle that won't cover a single video. Real access starts at $10/month with SuperGrok Lite, and there's a second route most displaced free users don't know about: Grok Imagine now runs as an engine inside Seedance, where one credit pool — $19.90/month, or $9.90/month billed annually — also buys Sora 2, Veo 3, and Seedance 2.0 generations.

What changed in March 2026

Grok Imagine spent most of 2025 as the most generous free AI video tool on the market, and that generosity ended in two steps. In January 2026, xAI tightened free generation after a wave of viral deepfake incidents drew regulator attention in the EU and UK. Then came the real cutoff: according to Fello AI's Grok pricing tracker, image and video generation through Grok Imagine was removed from the free tier entirely on March 19, 2026.

What's left for free accounts is murky, because xAI has never published an official number. Some trackers report zero standard generations for free users; X Imagine AI's breakdown of the credit system reports a goodwill trickle of roughly 5 credits per day resetting at midnight UTC, while other trackers describe the allowances as weekly usage pools. The cadence is disputed; what every source agrees on is that unused credits don't bank. Either way, the practical reality is the same: a trickle that size covers a couple of standard images at best, and video costs multiples of that per request. If you were using Grok Imagine for actual video work, the free lane is gone.

An xAI employee described the paid-only policy as a "near-term" measure driven by user growth, with no timeline for reversal. Plan around it being permanent.

xAI's answer to displaced free users is a ladder of subscriptions. Here's the current lineup, per Fello AI and AI Productivity's Grok pricing page:

First, every xAI plan meters you with a rolling usage pool: third-party trackers disagree on whether it resets daily or weekly — xAI doesn't publish official numbers — but none of them report rollover. You're renting a ceiling, not buying a bank of generations. Second, SuperGrok Lite's caps are real: 480p output and 6-second clips is draft quality, fine for memes and tests, thin for anything you'd publish. The $30 SuperGrok tier is where native Grok Imagine becomes genuinely usable for creators — which makes it a $360/year commitment to a single model.

Path B: Grok Imagine inside Seedance

The alternative route is running Grok Imagine through a multi-engine platform. Seedance added Grok Imagine as an engine in mid-2026, and it sits on the same unified credit pool as Seedance 2.0 and 2.5, Sora 2, Sora 2 Pro, and Veo 3 — plus image engines (Flux, SeeDream 4, Nano Banana) and a music maker the platform scores with Suno. The site advertises 30+ engines on one meter.

The pricing is per second of output rather than per day. According to Seedance's published Grok Imagine page, 480p costs 1 credit per second and 720p costs 2 credits per second — so a 6-second 480p draft is 6 credits, a 10-second 720p clip is 20 credits, and failed renders are refunded automatically. Clips run 6 to 30 seconds, you can attach up to 7 reference images to hold a character or style steady, and all three motion modes (Fun, Normal, Spicy) are available, with Spicy limited to text-to-video.

Plan-wise, there are two billing modes and the difference matters. Seedance Basic is $19.90/month for 200 monthly credits, or $9.90/month if you commit to a year of annual billing — the ~30% discount is exactly the gap between those two numbers, and the annual plan meters 150 credits a month with the full 1,800-credit year issued upfront. Pro is $39.90/month for 500 credits, or $19.90/month billed annually for 350; higher tiers (Max, Pro Max) scale further. Source: Seedance's pricing page, checked August 4, 2026. The same page's FAQ adds a structural detail worth knowing: unused credits on a monthly subscription roll forward as long as the subscription stays active. The free tier is strictly a taste — one credit every 24 hours, with watermarked output and no commercial license. Watermark-free exports and the commercial license require a paid plan, so treat the free credit as a way to test quality, not a way to work. Does Seedance's Grok Imagine match native?

Mostly, with a few differences in both directions. Seedance's implementation supports both text-to-video and image-to-video, 480p and 720p output, 6–30 second durations, up to 7 reference images, and all three motion modes. That 30-second ceiling and 720p access on any paid plan actually exceeds what native SuperGrok Lite allows (480p, 6 seconds) — you'd need the $30 SuperGrok tier for comparable headroom natively.

Native access wins elsewhere. If your workflow lives inside X — generating directly from Grok chat, posting straight to your feed — nothing third-party replicates that loop. Native subscribers also get xAI's newest model updates on day one and Spicy mode on mobile image-to-video, where Seedance restricts Spicy to text-to-video. And at the $100–$300 tiers, xAI's allowances dwarf anything a credit-pool platform offers. Generation speed on Seedance is advertised at 5–8 minutes per clip with no queue — fine for planned work, potentially slower than native at off-peak times.

Which path should you pick?

Pay xAI directly if: you're a heavy Grok-only user, your workflow is X-native, or you want Spicy mode on image-to-video. SuperGrok at $30/month is the honest recommendation there — Lite's 480p/6-second caps frustrate anyone doing real work.

Go the Seedance route if: you're a musician or short-form creator who treats Grok Imagine as one brush in a kit. The pitch is simple: for $19.90 month-to-month — or $9.90/month if you commit to annual billing — you get Grok Imagine plus Sora 2, Veo 3, and Seedance 2.0 on one meter, with credits that roll over instead of expiring, watermark-free exports, and a commercial license on paid plans. If you're weighing which engine deserves your best prompts, our Sora 2 vs Veo 3 vs Seedance comparison covers where each one wins. You can start with Seedance Basic and upgrade only if your clip count demands it.

Do nothing if: you were a casual free user making the occasional meme. Whatever small trickle your Grok account still has costs $0 and covers genuinely light use — just know the free outputs are watermarked and not licensed for commercial release. Seedance's 1 free credit a day won't add a video, since rendering there takes paid credits.

Is Grok Imagine still free in 2026?+
