Youka does the same job and exports up to 4K. LyricMV highlights words but does not advertise vocal removal. Canva and Adobe Express time lines by hand.

A karaoke video is a lyric video with the lead vocal taken out and the words lit one at a time so a singer can follow. That differs from our AI lyric video generator guide, where the vocal stays in and line timing is enough. This page compares the browser tools that do the karaoke job, using only what each vendor publishes.

It is for singers who want a track that does not exist as karaoke, KJs who need MP3+G files, and artists who want a sing-along version of their own song. If you only want words over your own vocal, start with our step-by-step lyric video tutorial instead.

Which AI karaoke video maker should you pick?

Pick MyKaraoke Video if you want vocal removal, word fill and a published price list in one tab. Pick Youka if you need 4K export or a desktop app, and LyricMV if your song keeps its vocal and you want a vertical template fast. Skip Canva and Adobe Express for karaoke; neither removes vocals or times words automatically.

How do these tools remove vocals and time each word?

MyKaraoke Video and Youka both separate the vocal from a finished mix with AI, then align your pasted lyrics to the singing. LyricMV and SunoMV transcribe and time words but do not advertise vocal removal. Canva and Adobe Express do neither; you place each line on a page and stretch clips until they match.

MyKaraoke Video's pitch is "Upload a song and paste the lyrics. We take the vocals out and sync every word," with a sing-along video "in about two minutes" (mykaraoke.video, read October 25, 2026). If the AI lands close but not exact, you drag any word's edge in the sync editor and nudge it in 10 ms steps. Five layouts cover two-line fill with a countdown, one big centred line, a scroll, duet colours and a bilingual line.

Youka splits the job into two pages: the lyric video maker keeps the original vocal, and its Karaoke Video Maker removes it (youka.io lyric video maker, read October 25, 2026). Both share one editor for word or line timing, line breaks and a colour per singer. Youka's own advice is to paste clean lyrics with every repeated chorus written out in full.

LyricMV runs OpenAI Whisper for word-level timestamps and says transcription takes under 2 minutes in 99+ languages (lyricmv.com, read October 25, 2026). Its Deep Shadow template gives the karaoke look, but the page never mentions taking vocals out. SunoMV's guide puts the standard plainly: "word-by-word sync means each word lights up only at the moment it’s sung, instead of a whole line appearing at once" (SunoMV Team, SunoMV word-by-word guide, read October 25, 2026). It reads a Suno share link and leaves instrumental breaks blank.

What does a karaoke video cost on each tool?

MyKaraoke Video publishes the clearest numbers: $0.10 a credit pay as you go, about $1.20 to $1.60 per video by its own estimate, or $7.99 a month for 300 credits. LyricMV charges 10 credits per minute of audio and says that lands near $3 per video. Youka, SunoMV, Canva and Adobe Express keep prices on separate pages we did not source here.

MyKaraoke Video says a typical video uses about 8 to 12 credits, depending on the song's length and whether you upload it or paste a YouTube link (MyKaraoke Video FAQ, read October 25, 2026). The 30 signup credits therefore cover two or three videos. Premium at $14.99 gives 600 credits that last 60 days, about 50 to 75 videos by the vendor's count. Subscribers export free through a queue; instrumental downloads never use credits.

On LyricMV a 3-minute song is 30 credits for a full render, the 480p preview is free, and full renders take 5 to 15 minutes, per the vendor. Youka gives 15 credits to new accounts and is honest about what they are: "It is a free trial, not unlimited free exports" (Youka, read October 25, 2026). Its weakness is that the lyric page never shows a credit price, so you cannot cost a batch before signing up.

What are the export limits and watermark rules?

MyKaraoke Video exports 1080p MP4 and watermarks free exports, with a paid plan removing the mark; Youka exports MP4 up to 4K and its lyric page does not mention a watermark at all. LyricMV's free output is a 480p, 30-second preview without a watermark, and its paid 1080p renders are also clean. Canva's and Adobe Express's lyric pages say nothing about watermarks.

MyKaraoke Video's limits are about throughput: 1 concurrent export free, 2 on Standard, 5 on Premium. MP3+G files for KJ software and transparent ProRes exports are Premium only. Inputs are MP3, WAV, FLAC, AAC and MP4 music videos, with over 1,500 fonts.

LyricMV shows two upload caps on one page, 50 MB in the uploader and 500MB in its steps and FAQ; plan for the smaller. All of its templates are portrait 9:16, mostly 1080x1920, which suits Shorts and Reels and fails a widescreen YouTube karaoke channel. Youka's Desktop app for Windows and macOS lists "Free export" and offline playback.

Can Canva or Adobe Express make a karaoke video?

Not in the karaoke sense: Canva's lyric templates, 57 of them, have you type lyrics page by page, then "Adjust the clip lengths to match each line of the song"; Beat Sync in Pro aligns clips to the music, not words to the vocal. Adobe Express has you speed clips up or slow them down to sync lyrics. Neither page mentions vocal removal.

They still have a place. Canva lets you download the finished video free and sync manually with beat markers at no cost (Canva lyric video templates, read October 25, 2026). Adobe Express adds brand kits on Premium and lets you translate the lyrics inside the editor (Adobe Express lyric video maker, read October 25, 2026). For a worship slide or a classroom sing-along where line timing is enough, either works.

For a bar or a KJ rig they fail on both things that define karaoke: the vocal stays and the words do not fill in. Our lyric video, visualizer and music video comparison explains where a plain lyric video fits; a karaoke version is a fourth asset.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need stems to make an AI karaoke video?+

No. MyKaraoke Video says one finished song is enough, with no stems and nothing to install; its AI separates the vocal from the mix. If you already have an isolated vocal track, it can auto-sync the lyrics to it. If you only have an instrumental, you sync the lyrics by hand in its editor instead.

How many credits does one karaoke video use on MyKaraoke Video?+

The vendor says a typical video uses about 8 to 12 credits, depending on song length and whether you upload a file or paste a YouTube link. The 30 signup credits therefore cover two or three videos. Pay-as-you-go credits cost $0.10 each, and Standard gives 300 credits a month for $7.99.

Can I export a karaoke file for KJ software, not just an MP4?+

On MyKaraoke Video, yes, but only on Premium at $14.99 a month, which adds MP3+G export alongside transparent video. Instrumental downloads never use credits on any tier. Youka, LyricMV, Canva and Adobe Express do not mention MP3+G on the pages we read, so assume MP4 only from those.

Does Youka remove the vocal or keep it?+

Both, on different pages. Youka's lyric video maker keeps the original vocal for a traditional lyric video, while its Karaoke Video Maker removes the lead vocal for a sing-along. Either path shares the same editor, 15 free credits for new accounts, and MP4 export up to 4K from the browser.

Is LyricMV usable for karaoke?+

Partly. Its Deep Shadow template highlights words one at a time from Whisper timestamps, which gives the karaoke look, but the page never mentions removing vocals, so the singer is still in the mix. Templates are portrait 9:16, and a full render costs 10 credits per minute of audio after a free 480p preview.

What is the difference between a karaoke video and a lyric video?+

A lyric video keeps the artist's vocal and shows the words, usually a line at a time, to promote a release. A karaoke video strips the lead vocal and lights each word as it should be sung so someone else can perform it. Most tools here do the first; only MyKaraoke Video and Youka advertise the second.
