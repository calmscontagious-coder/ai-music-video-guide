AI Vocal Demos: Hear Your Lyric Sung Before You Book a Singer

By the AI Music Video GuideUpdated November 13, 202610 min read

Quick answer: A text to music model can sing your lyric back as a scratch vocal. The tool we link regenerates one section at a time and caps a track at 5 minutes (ElevenLabs docs, read November 13, 2026). The job is narrow and common. You've written a lyric and have a melody idea, and you want to hear it sung before you pay a singer or send it to a co-writer. This guide covers what the model does with a lyric, the 'try a different pre-chorus' loop, length, cost, and the license line between a demo and a release.

What does an AI vocal demo generator do with your lyric?

It turns text into a sung track. The music page says you can 'Set the genre, mood, instruments, and structure', choose vocals or just an instrumental, and 'Select any section and regenerate just that part' (ElevenLabs music page, read November 13, 2026). You supply the lyric; the model supplies a voice, a melody and an arrangement around it.

The docs describe Eleven Music as 'a Text to Music model that generates studio-grade music with natural language prompts in any style' and say 'The model understands both natural language and musical terminology' (ElevenLabs docs, read November 13, 2026). For a demo that means you can write the prompt the way you'd brief a session player.

Music v2 is described as 'Better vocals, instrumentation, and arrangement across every genre', and the docs add 'Faster and more complex vocal delivery, including fast rap and dense lyrical patterns' (ElevenLabs music page and docs, read November 13, 2026). Music v2.5 is called 'our most advanced music model' and 'supports the same workflows, including composition plans, Audio Reference and inpainting' (ElevenLabs docs, read November 13, 2026).

Two features matter for revisions. The music page says you can 'Generate songs with a consistent voice using a Vocal from your tracks or our curated Vocal Library' and 'Generate every track with a consistent sound using a Style created from your existing music' (ElevenLabs music page, read November 13, 2026). So every revision can share one voice.

The music page's FAQ lists English, Spanish, French, German and Japanese among the languages Music v2 supports (ElevenLabs music page, read November 13, 2026). What the pages do not say: anything about the pitch accuracy of the vocal, or whether it follows a melody you hum. We did not test the audio.

How do you try a different pre-chorus without redoing the whole song?

Use inpainting. The docs say you can 'select a specific section of a generated song and regenerate just that section', and the music page lists 'Inpainting and section editing' as a feature (ElevenLabs docs and music page, read November 13, 2026). The verse and chorus you like stay put; only the section you select changes.

The docs also say you can 'Edit the sound and lyrics of individual sections or the whole song' (ElevenLabs docs, read November 13, 2026). That is the demo loop: keep the chorus, rewrite two lines of the pre-chorus, regenerate, compare. The music page's plan rows name 'Inpainting and section editing' without saying which plan includes it, so check before you buy. The pages do not say whether a regenerated section always matches the tempo and key of the rest, so listen for the seam.

Audio Reference is the feel-matching lever. The docs say it accepts reference tracks of 'up to approximately 30 seconds' and 'is available with Music v2 and v2.5 on all paid plans' (ElevenLabs docs, read November 13, 2026). It is not listed for the Free plan. A rough phone recording of the groove you want goes here.

The prompt and the reference have a hard rule. The Music Terms prohibit prompting with any artist's or songwriter's real or stage name, any song, album, publisher or record label name, or 'a substantial or distinctive portion of any song's lyrics' (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026). Your own lyric is fine. A lyric that quotes another song is not.

The same terms bar Output that 'replicates or mimics the voice, likeness, or identifiable characteristics of any recording artist' where that is misleading or implies a false endorsement (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026). For a scratch vocal that is a feature: you want a placeholder voice, not an impression of the singer you plan to hire.

How long can a demo be, and what file do you get?

Between 3 seconds and 5 minutes per generation, per the docs, which also list output as 'MP3 (44.1kHz, 128-192kbps) and WAV formats' (ElevenLabs docs, read November 13, 2026). That range covers a 30-second hook sketch and a full-length demo in one tool. The music page itself does not state a track length limit.

One more line on files. The Music Terms list high-quality downloads (lossless WAV or 192 kbps mp3) as Yes on Pro and No on Free, Starter and Creator (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026), so on Starter and Creator the exhibit does not promise a lossless file.

The music page's plan rows list '2 and 4 stems' on Starter and Creator, 'Up to 6 stems' on Pro, and no stems listed on Free (ElevenLabs music page, read November 13, 2026). The page does not say which parts the stems separate into, so we can't promise an isolated vocal for a producer who wants the demo voice on a real backing track.

Two credit rules shape a demo habit. 'Your credit allowance resets at the start of each billing cycle, which begins on the day you subscribed', and 'Unused credits roll over for up to two months, up to 2x your monthly quota' (ElevenLabs pricing page, read November 13, 2026). The help center adds that on downgrade or cancellation 'unused credits are not rolled over' (ElevenLabs help center, read November 13, 2026).

The API has a pay-per-use price: Music is $0.15 per minute (ElevenLabs API pricing page, read November 13, 2026), which makes a 3-minute demo $0.45, our arithmetic at that rate. The docs say 'The Music API is available for paid subscribers' (ElevenLabs docs, read November 13, 2026), so we would not assume the per-minute price stands alone without a plan.

On ownership and clearance the terms are short. Section 2.2 says 'you retain any and all rights in and to your Input and Output', and Section 3.1 says 'you are responsible for reviewing Output for clearance before using, publishing, or distributing it' (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026). Checking is on you.

Film and TV are where the pages disagree. The docs say Eleven Music 'is cleared for nearly all commercial uses, from film and television to podcasts and social media videos', while the music page FAQ says Self-Serve plans permit commercial use 'except for film, TV, and Studio Games' (ElevenLabs docs and music page, read November 13, 2026). Exhibit A adds radio to that exclusion on all four plans in the table (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026). Where the two differ, the Music Terms are the page to check. That is our reading, not legal advice.

The terms also put platform disclosure on you: on 'any streaming platform, digital service provider, or content-hosting service' 'you are responsible for complying with that platform's policies on the disclosure of AI-generated content' (ElevenLabs Music Terms, read November 13, 2026). Do you have to label the demo when it goes on a video platform?

On YouTube, yes when the music is the point of the video. Its disclosure page lists 'Creates music that's the main focus of the video' among the realistic AI content creators must disclose (YouTube Help, read November 13, 2026). A lyric video or visualizer makes the music the focus; if the AI content is realistic, disclose.

The same page puts 'Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs' on the no-label list; a sung AI vocal is not on that list. The switch sits in YouTube Studio during upload, under 'AI use' in the Attributes section, and YouTube may also apply labels automatically (YouTube Help, read November 13, 2026).

Frequently asked questions

Can an AI music model sing my own lyrics as a demo?

The pages say yes: you set genre, mood, instruments and structure, pick vocals or an instrumental, and the model sings the lyric you supply. You can also lock one consistent voice across revisions from a vocal library or your own tracks. The pages don't describe pitch accuracy or whether the model follows a melody you hum, and we did not test the audio ourselves.

How much does a 3-minute scratch vocal cost?

The pricing page lists music at about 900 credits per minute, so a 3-minute demo is about 2,700 credits and a 30-second hook about 450, our arithmetic at that rate. On the pay-per-use API, music is $0.15 per minute, which makes the same demo $0.45 by our arithmetic. The free tier's 10k credits cover about 11 minutes of music, again our arithmetic.

Can I download the demo on the free plan?

No. The music terms' plan exhibit lists downloads as not permitted on the free tier, with attribution required when you distribute. The first paid tier, $6 a month, lists 30 minutes of downloads a month with no attribution required, which is enough for files that go to a singer or co-writer. Streaming is prohibited on both of those tiers.

Can I regenerate just the pre-chorus and keep the rest?

Yes. The docs describe inpainting as selecting a specific section of a generated song and regenerating only that section, and say you can edit the sound and lyrics of individual sections or the whole song. What the pages don't say is whether the new section always matches the tempo and key of the parts you kept, so listen for the seam before you send the file.

Can the demo vocal imitate the singer I plan to hire?

Don't prompt for that. The music terms prohibit prompts that use any artist's real or stage name, any song or label name, or a substantial portion of any song's lyrics, and they bar output that mimics a recording artist's voice where that misleads or implies endorsement. A placeholder voice from the vocal library does the demo job without that risk.

Does a demo under a lyric video need an AI label?

On the video platform whose rules we read, yes, when the AI music is realistic and the main focus of the video. The setting sits in the upload flow under an AI-use question. Cloning your own speaking voice for a voiceover is on the no-label list; a sung AI vocal is not.
