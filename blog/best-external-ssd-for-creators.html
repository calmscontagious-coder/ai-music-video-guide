The Best External SSD for Creators: Speed Tiers for Mac and PC

Short answer: match the drive to the fastest port you actually have. On a recent Mac or a PC with USB4 or Thunderbolt, a USB4 drive such as the Corsair EX400U is the one to buy for editing straight off the drive. For backups, sample libraries on an older laptop, or a mixed Mac and PC studio, a 10Gbps drive such as the Samsung T7 Shield runs at full speed everywhere. Skip 20Gbps "Gen 2x2" drives if you work on a Mac.

The speed tiers in plain words

  • 10Gbps (USB 3.2 Gen 2): about 1,000MB/s. Every current Mac and most PCs run these at full speed.
  • 20Gbps (USB 3.2 Gen 2x2): about 2,000MB/s, but only on a PC port that supports Gen 2x2. Apple silicon Macs connect these at 10Gbps (Apple Community); Wirecutter found the same on a USB4 laptop (Wirecutter). Some Intel Macs are an exception (Eclectic Light).
  • USB4 (40Gbps): about 3,000 to 4,000MB/s on USB4 or Thunderbolt ports, which most recent laptops and all Apple silicon Macs have.
  • Thunderbolt 5: up to about 6,000MB/s, and only on a Thunderbolt 5 computer.

What creators need from a drive

Video: phone 4K at default settings takes about 190MB a minute, while 4K 10-bit ProRes HDR takes about 6GB a minute; recent Pro iPhones can record ProRes straight to an external SSD (Wirecutter). A 10Gbps drive handles most 4K editing; ProRes, RAW and multicam timelines are where USB4 pays off.

Sample libraries and project files: these load thousands of small files, so random-read speed matters more than the headline number. Wirecutter's tests show USB4 drives well ahead on small files, with the SanDisk Extreme PRO USB4 the exception on small writes (Wirecutter).

Mac and PC in one studio: format the drive as exFAT to read and write it on both. Use APFS for a Mac-only drive or for Time Machine (Apple Disk Utility guide). Formatting erases the drive, so do it before you copy anything.

Prices: Wirecutter reports flash storage prices rose sharply through 2026 and some drives go in and out of stock, so check the current price before you decide between tiers (Wirecutter).

Which drive should you buy?

  • Apple silicon Mac, editing video or running sample libraries from the drive: Corsair EX400U, or the OWC Envoy Ultra on a Thunderbolt 5 Mac.
  • Mixed Mac and PC studio, or an older laptop: Samsung T7 Shield.
  • Windows desktop with a 20Gbps USB-C port: Samsung T9 or Lexar SL500.
  • Outdoor shoots and camera dumps: SanDisk Extreme PRO with USB4, with a second copy elsewhere.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 20Gbps USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 SSD faster on a Mac?+

No. Apple silicon Macs connect Gen 2x2 drives at 10Gbps, so they run at about the same speed as a cheaper 10Gbps drive. On a Mac, buy a 10Gbps drive or a USB4 or Thunderbolt drive.

What format should an external SSD use for both Mac and PC?+

exFAT. Both systems read and write it. Use APFS only for a Mac-only drive or a Time Machine backup.

Do I need USB4 or Thunderbolt for 4K video editing?+

Not for most 4K work: a 10Gbps drive handles it. USB4 or Thunderbolt helps with ProRes, RAW, multicam timelines and very large copies.

Is an external SSD a backup?+

Not on its own. It is one copy in one place. Keep a second copy on another drive and a third copy offsite or in the cloud.

Which external SSD is best for sample libraries?+

One with strong random-read speed on the port you have. Wirecutter's tests put USB4 drives such as the Corsair EX400U ahead on small files; on an older laptop a 10Gbps drive such as the Samsung T7 Shield is fine.
