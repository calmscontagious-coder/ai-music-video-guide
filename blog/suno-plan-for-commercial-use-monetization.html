Which Suno Plan Do You Actually Need to Monetize? Free vs Pro vs Premier Rights, Explained

By the AI Music Video GuideUpdated October 25, 20266 min read

Quick answer: Songs made on Suno's Free plan are licensed for personal, non-commercial use only — you can't monetize them, and upgrading later doesn't change that by default. Pro ($10/month, or $8 billed yearly) and Premier ($30/month, or $24 billed yearly) carry the identical commercial-use grant, but since Suno's September 3, 2026 terms the download is the licence: a song you generate on a paid plan and download through Suno within your monthly allowance (20 songs on Pro, 60 on Premier) can be distributed and monetized, and you keep those rights after you cancel. Premier buys more credits and Suno Studio access, not stronger rights, so for most independent artists Pro is the monetization unlock.

If I pay for one month of Suno Pro, can I monetize those songs forever?

Only the songs you downloaded. Suno's terms, effective September 3, 2026, tie commercial use to a permitted download within your plan's monthly allowance, and the rights on a song you downloaded survive cancellation (Source: Suno Terms of Service, read October 25, 2026; Suno pricing, read October 25, 2026). So a single $10 Pro month gives you up to 20 monetizable songs, not every track you generated. Songs made on the Free plan, or after you cancel, carry no commercial rights. Three details explain how this works.

1. The plan and the download together decide a song's status. A song generated on the Free plan is non-commercial. A song generated during a Pro or Premier subscription carries commercial-use rights once you download it through Suno within your monthly allowance. Suno's terms forbid commercial use of output you never downloaded through an approved channel, and recording or stream-ripping a copy doesn't count.

2. Paid-tier songs stay monetizable after you cancel. According to Suno's help center, you "retain the rights to the songs you made while subscribed, even after the subscription is canceled." Suno's current terms put it more narrowly: the commercial rights on a song you downloaded are perpetual and unaffected by cancellation or downgrade. So one month of Pro, cancel, and the songs you downloaded that month (up to 20, plus any extra downloads you bought) remain monetizable; the rest of what you generated doesn't.

3. Free-plan songs do not gain rights retroactively. The same article is explicit that subscribing does not, by default, grant retroactive commercial licensing to songs you made on the free tier. Suno notes it "may offer retroactive rights in certain cases," which reads as an exception process, not a promise. If a free-tier track turns out to be the keeper, contact Suno support about retroactive licensing before you distribute it — don't assume the upgrade covered it.

The fine print: license, copyright, and your distributor

Three details trip people up after they subscribe.

Commercial rights are not copyright. According to Suno's paid-subscription rights page, subscribers can monetize their songs, distribute to streaming platforms, and use tracks in film, TV, and games — but the same page carries a warning most roundups skip: "granting commercial use rights does not guarantee copyright protection," which is "determined by your region/country's copyright office, not by Suno." In the US, purely AI-generated material has limited copyright protection; your own lyrics, structural edits, and recorded additions are where enforceable ownership accrues. Suno's license means Suno won't come after you for monetizing — it doesn't mean you can stop someone else from copying the track.

Keep proof of when you subscribed. Because a song's monetization status depends on whether it was created during a paid period, your subscription invoices and account creation history are the evidence. Save the invoices and don't delete the Suno account that holds your generation timeline. Worth adding: Suno previewed 2026 product changes tied to its Warner Music agreement (Source: Digital Music News), so screenshot the rights page on the day you subscribe — you want a record of the terms that were live when you built your catalog.

Distributors and platforms have rules on top of Suno's. A Suno commercial license makes monetization legal from Suno's side; it doesn't obligate DistroKid, TuneCore, or Spotify to accept the track. Acceptance and disclosure policies vary widely — our breakdown of which platforms accept AI music covers the current landscape, including which distributors take AI tracks and what they ask you to declare.

Does Suno Premier include more commercial rights than Pro?

No. Pro and Premier carry the identical commercial-use grant; the pricing page lists the same rights line on both paid tiers, with no expanded-license category. Premier's extra $20 per month buys capacity and tooling — 10,000 monthly credits instead of 2,500, access to Suno Studio, and a third stem-separation type — not stronger licensing.

A persistent belief on Reddit and in comment sections is that Premier carries a "bigger" or "more commercial" license. It doesn't. Here is what the extra $20/month actually buys:

  • 4x the credits — 10,000 versus 2,500 per month.
  • Suno Studio — the multitrack generative workstation for layering, arranging, stem generation, and recording. Per the official Studio introduction, "Studio is only available with a Premier plan."
  • 3 stem-separation types versus Pro's 2.

If your only reason for picking Premier was "the license must be better," save the $20. It isn't.

Which Suno plan should you buy to monetize your music?

For most independent artists, Pro at $10 per month is the monetization unlock: roughly 500 songs of monthly generation capacity, of which 20 a month can be downloaded with commercial rights you keep after cancelling (extra downloads can be bought). Stay on Free only while nothing goes public. Choose Premier only when you need more than 2,500 credits a month or Studio's stem-level production — the license itself is identical.

  • You're exploring and nothing goes public: stay Free. Fifty credits a day is ~10 songs of practice, and you lose nothing as long as none of it gets monetized.
  • You release music in any form — streaming via a distributor, monetized YouTube, sync pitches, beat sales: Pro. About 500 songs a month is more capacity than most artists ever use, and every song made under it is cleared. Our guide to making money with AI music maps the actual revenue routes once you're licensed.
  • Burst strategy: subscribe to Pro only for the months you're generating. Batch-produce your catalog, download the keepers within your monthly allowance, cancel, and keep monetizing what you downloaded. Suno's terms keep download rights after cancellation; songs you didn't download, and songs made in the gaps between subscriptions, are non-commercial.
  • You need 500+ songs monthly, or stem-level production: Premier, for the credit pool and Studio — not for the license.
