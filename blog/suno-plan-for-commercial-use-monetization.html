Which Suno Plan Do You Actually Need to Monetize? Free vs Pro vs Premier Rights, Explained

By the AI Music Video Guide team · Updated August 11, 2026 · 6 min read

This post contains affiliate links — if you sign up through them we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you (disclosure). Researched and edited for accuracy with AI assistance.

Quick answer: Songs made on Suno's Free plan are licensed for personal, non-commercial use only — you can't monetize them, and upgrading later doesn't change that by default. Pro ($10/month) and Premier ($30/month) carry the identical commercial-use grant: any song created while you're subscribed can be distributed and monetized, and you keep those rights after you cancel. Premier buys more credits and Suno Studio access, not stronger rights, so for most independent artists Pro is the monetization unlock.

The exact question: "If I pay for one month, can I monetize forever?"

This is the question people type into a search bar at the moment the card comes out, and the answer has three parts. All three come from Suno's own help center, not from forum folklore.

1. The plan you're on when a song is created decides that song's status. A song generated on the Free plan is non-commercial. A song generated during a Pro or Premier subscription carries commercial-use rights. The status is fixed at creation time — not at download time, not at upload-to-Spotify time.

2. Paid-tier songs stay monetizable after you cancel. According to Suno's help center, you "retain the rights to the songs you made while subscribed, even after the subscription is canceled." So yes: one month of Pro, a few hundred songs generated during that month, cancel — every one of those songs remains legally monetizable indefinitely.

3. Free-plan songs do not gain rights retroactively. The same article is explicit that subscribing does not, by default, grant retroactive commercial licensing to songs you made on the free tier. Suno notes it "may offer retroactive rights in certain cases," which reads as an exception process, not a promise. If a free-tier track turns out to be the keeper, contact Suno support about retroactive licensing before you distribute it — don't assume the upgrade covered it.

The fine print: license, copyright, and your distributor

Three details trip people up after they subscribe.

Commercial rights are not copyright. According to Suno's paid-subscription rights page, subscribers can monetize their songs, distribute to streaming platforms, and use tracks in film, TV, and games — but the same page carries a warning most roundups skip: "granting commercial use rights does not guarantee copyright protection," which is "determined by your region/country's copyright office, not by Suno." In the US, purely AI-generated material has limited copyright protection; your own lyrics, structural edits, and recorded additions are where enforceable ownership accrues. Suno's license means Suno won't come after you for monetizing — it doesn't mean you can stop someone else from copying the track.

Keep proof of when you subscribed. Because a song's monetization status depends on whether it was created during a paid period, your subscription invoices and account creation history are the evidence. Save the invoices and don't delete the Suno account that holds your generation timeline. Worth adding: Suno previewed 2026 product changes tied to its Warner Music agreement (Source: Digital Music News), so screenshot the rights page on the day you subscribe — you want a record of the terms that were live when you built your catalog.

Distributors and platforms have rules on top of Suno's. A Suno commercial license makes monetization legal from Suno's side; it doesn't obligate DistroKid, TuneCore, or Spotify to accept the track. Acceptance and disclosure policies vary widely — our breakdown of which platforms accept AI music covers the current landscape, including which distributors take AI tracks and what they ask you to declare.

The Premier myth: $30 does not buy extra rights

A persistent belief on Reddit and in comment sections is that Premier carries a "bigger" or "more commercial" license. It doesn't. Suno's pricing page lists the same commercial-use rights line on both paid tiers; there is no expanded-license category. What the extra $20/month actually buys:

  • 4x the credits — 10,000 versus 2,500 per month.
  • Suno Studio — the multitrack generative workstation for layering, arranging, stem generation, and recording. Per Suno's own Studio introduction, "Studio is only available with a Premier plan."
  • 3 stem-separation types versus Pro's 2.

If your only reason for picking Premier was "the license must be better," save the $20. It isn't.

Which plan, by output volume

  • You're exploring and nothing goes public: stay Free. Fifty credits a day is ~10 songs of practice, and you lose nothing as long as none of it gets monetized.
  • You release music in any form — streaming via a distributor, monetized YouTube, sync pitches, beat sales: Pro. About 500 songs a month is more capacity than most artists ever use, and every song made under it is cleared. Our guide to making money with AI music maps the actual revenue routes once you're licensed.
  • Burst strategy: subscribe to Pro only for the months you're generating. Batch-produce your catalog, cancel, keep monetizing indefinitely. This is explicitly supported by Suno's post-cancellation policy — just remember songs made in the gaps between subscriptions fall back to non-commercial.
  • You need 500+ songs monthly, or stem-level production: Premier, for the credit pool and Studio — not for the license.

Frequently asked questions

If I pay for one month of Suno Pro, can I monetize those songs forever?+

Yes. Suno's help center states you retain the rights to songs made while subscribed even after cancellation. Every song generated during your paid month stays monetizable indefinitely. Songs made before you subscribed (on Free) or after you cancel do not get commercial rights.

Does Suno Premier include more commercial rights than Pro?+

No. Pro and Premier carry the identical commercial-use grant. Premier's extra $20/month buys 10,000 credits instead of 2,500, exclusive access to Suno Studio (the multitrack generative workstation), and a third stem-separation type — capacity and tooling, not licensing.

Can I put songs from Suno's Free plan on Spotify?+

Not for monetization. Free-plan songs are licensed for personal, non-commercial use only, and upgrading later does not retroactively license them by default — Suno says it may offer retroactive rights only in certain cases via support. To distribute and earn, the song needs to be created while you're on Pro or Premier.

Do I own the copyright to songs made with Suno Pro?+

Suno grants commercial-use rights, but its help center explicitly warns that commercial rights do not guarantee copyright protection — that's determined by your country's copyright office. In the US, purely AI-generated output has limited protection; your own lyrics, edits, and recorded contributions are what add protectable human authorship.
