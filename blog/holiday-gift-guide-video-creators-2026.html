Holiday Gift Guide for Video Creators: 10 Useful Ideas

By the AI Music Video GuideUpdated November 11, 20264 min read

Quick answer: This guide is for anyone who gives a gift to a person who makes videos or other content. The three best picks are a clip-on wireless microphone, a fast memory card or SSD, and a free afternoon of help with their editing backlog.

Most of the ideas below cost little or nothing and come from any shop. None of the links on this page earn the studio anything. Prices appear only where we read them on the seller's own page, and they may have changed.

Ten gift ideas for creators

1. A batch-editing afternoon

Free

Offer a few hours to sit beside them and do the dull jobs: naming clips, sorting footage into folders, backing up the card. Say what you will do on a card so the gift is concrete.

Suits: Anyone with a backlog of footage. Check before buying: Ask which jobs they dread most, and agree the date before you wrap it.

2. Gaffer tape, cable ties and a small pouch

A few dollars

Cables on the floor, a light that will not stay put, a lead that keeps unplugging. A roll of tape and some ties fix most set problems.

Suits: Home shooters who set up and strike a space each time. Check before buying: Ask for tape sold as gaffer tape for cables; the listing should say how it comes off a surface.

3. A lens and screen cleaning kit

A few dollars

A blower, a soft cloth and a pen-style brush keep smudges out of the frame. It is small, practical and easy to post.

Suits: Anyone who films on a phone or a camera. Check before buying: Match it to what they clean: a camera lens, a phone, a monitor.

4. A compact or flexible phone tripod

Low price

A tripod frees both hands for demos and gives steady talking-head shots. Flexible-leg models also wrap around a rail or branch.

Suits: Phone filmers who currently prop the phone on a stack of books. Check before buying: The listing should state the phone weight it holds and the clamp width; check it against their phone in a case.

5. A small rechargeable LED light

Low to mid price

Soft light on a face is the quickest upgrade for evening shoots. Battery-powered panels can sit on a shelf or clip to a stand.

Suits: People who film indoors after dark. Check before buying: Look for adjustable brightness and colour temperature, and a way to charge it over USB.

6. A clip-on wireless microphone

DJI Mic Mini kit: about $99 when checked (DJI Store, checked 2026-11-11; the page showed it out of stock)

Clear voice is the thing viewers notice first. DJI's page lists a clip and magnetic mount, omnidirectional recording, up to 400 m of stable transmission, and up to 48 hours of use with the charging case. One receiver can pair with two transmitters. Other brands make similar kits.

Suits: Anyone who records speech on the move: vlogs, interviews, tutorials. Check before buying: Stock (it showed out of stock when we checked), what plugs into their phone or camera, and whether the receiver fits with their phone case. See our microphone guide for what to listen for.

7. A fast memory card or a portable SSD

Mid price

Cards and drives are consumable gear for shooters. Faster storage matters most for high-resolution video.

Suits: Anyone shooting 4K or running short on space. Check before buying: The speed class their camera needs, the capacity, and the seller; buy from the maker or a reputable shop because fake cards exist. See memory card speed classes and external SSDs for creators.

8. DaVinci Resolve Studio licence

The free version costs nothing; Studio about $236 when checked (Blackmagic Design, checked 2026-11-11)

Blackmagic says there is a free version of DaVinci Resolve and lists Studio as the paid edition. A licence is a clean gift for an editor who has outgrown the free version.

Suits: An editor who already uses Resolve and has hit a limit. Check before buying: Compare the free and Studio feature lists on Blackmagic's page first, and confirm they use Resolve. The price may have changed since we checked.

9. An hour with a specialist

Varies

A colour grade review, an audio clean-up lesson or an editing session with someone whose work they admire. It teaches more than another gadget.

Suits: Creators who want to improve, not just collect gear. Check before buying: Book the person first, and ask how long the voucher stays valid.

10. Closed-back headphones for editing

Mid price

Closed-back headphones keep outside noise out while cutting dialogue and music. Comfort over a long session counts more than a spec sheet.

Suits: Editors who work in a shared room. Check before buying: Whether they already own a pair they like, the cable type, and a return window. Our headphone guide lists what to check.

What not to buy

Skip a camera or a lens unless they asked for that exact model: shooters have strong views about bodies and lens mounts. Skip a software subscription that bills their card without asking; give a licence or a voucher they can choose to use. Skip unbranded bundles of lights and mics with no named maker and no return window.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good gift for someone who makes videos?

A clip-on wireless microphone, a fast memory card or SSD, or a free afternoon of help with editing backlog. Pick the one that removes a problem they already complain about.

What can I give a creator for under $20?

Gaffer tape and cable ties, a lens and screen cleaning kit, or a small notebook for shot lists. Free options include a batch-editing afternoon or a night of watching their latest edit and giving honest notes.

Should I buy a camera for a creator?

Only if they named the exact model. Camera bodies and lens mounts are personal choices, and a wrong one is hard to return.

Is DaVinci Resolve free?

Blackmagic Design says there is a free version of DaVinci Resolve. Studio is a separate paid edition, listed at about $236 when we checked on 2026-11-11. Check the page for the current price.
