Veo 3 Daily Limit on Google AI Pro: It's 1,000 Flow Credits Now

By the AI Music Video Guide teamUpdated October 25, 202612 min read

Quick answer: Google AI Pro has no Veo 3 daily limit anymore. As of October 21, 2026, the $19.99 plan includes 1,000 Google Flow credits a month plus 50 free credits a day. Veo 3.1 costs 100 credits per Quality clip, 20 per Fast clip, 10 per Lite clip. That buys 10 Quality clips a month, and nothing rolls over.

Google stopped counting Veo videos per day in May 2026 and started counting credits, so we rebuilt this page around the credit math.

Every number below comes from a page we opened on October 21, 2026, or from a dated news report we name in the text.

Is there still a Veo 3 daily limit on Google AI Pro?

No. The three-videos-a-day cap Google set when Veo 3 reached AI Pro worldwide (TechCrunch, July 3, 2025) is gone. Google replaced it at I/O in May 2026 with two meters: monthly Flow credits for Google Flow, and a compute allowance in the Gemini app that refreshes every 5 hours until you hit a weekly ceiling.

The only daily number left is the 50 free Flow credits every account gets, and that's a free sample, not a Veo 3 daily limit. The Decoder's May 19, 2026 report on the restructure described the change: daily prompt limits were dropped for a "compute-used" model where a video prompt eats more of your allowance than a text prompt. Google's own limits page doesn't publish video counts at all. It rates each plan as a multiple of a standard limit (Pro is 4x) and warns that media generation "will consume more of your usage."

That opacity has a cost. A week after the change, Android Authority (May 26, 2026) reported a Google AI Pro subscriber whose single failed video prompt consumed the entire five-hour allowance in minutes, and Google said it was looking into it. For anything beyond a clip or two, Flow is the only lane where you can do the arithmetic in advance.

One more change matters for Veo specifically. Google's help page for video in the Gemini app now says videos there are made with Gemini Omni, the conversational video model Google introduced at I/O 2026, not with Veo. Veo 3.1 lives in Flow and in the Gemini API. If you want Veo, you want Flow credits.

How many Veo 3.1 videos do 1,000 Flow credits buy?

Ten Quality clips, 50 Fast clips or 100 Lite clips a month, before you count the 50 free daily credits. Google's Flow credits page, read October 21, 2026, prices Veo 3.1 Quality at 100 credits for everyone, Fast at 20 and Lite at 10 for non-Ultra subscribers, and halves Fast and Lite to 10 and 5 on Ultra.

Two lines on that page catch people out. First, the cost is per generation, not per request, and Google's own example is a single Flow request that produces two videos. Leave the output count at two and every Quality prompt costs 200 credits, which turns your ten clips into five. Second, Quality only renders 8-second clips. If you want a 4-second insert at the top tier, you still pay for 8 seconds.

The daily 50 credits work differently from the monthly grant. Your first generation of the day triggers the refresh, unused daily credits vanish at the next refresh, and non-subscribers may be blocked from video entirely during peak hours, which Google puts at roughly 2 PM to 5 PM UTC. Subscribers skip the peak-hour block.

What do Google's AI plans cost in October 2026, and what does each buy in Veo?

On Google's US plans page, read October 21, 2026: AI Plus is $4.99 a month with 200 Flow credits, AI Pro is $19.99 with 1,000, and AI Ultra is $99.99 with 10,000 or $199.99 with 25,000. The $19.99 Pro plan works out to 2 cents a credit, so a Quality clip costs about $2.00 and a Fast clip about $0.40 if you use every credit.

Ultra costs five times Pro and gives ten times the credits, and because Fast and Lite cost half as much on Ultra, the Fast-clip count goes up twenty-fold. Rollover is the line that doesn't change with price. Google's Flow page says unused monthly credits don't carry into the next month, and unused daily credits don't carry into the next day. A quiet month banks nothing for release week. Pro and Ultra subscribers can buy extra AI credits, and Google's purchase page describes one-time top-ups and an auto-reload option, but it doesn't list a per-credit price.

Why does one music video burn through 1,000 credits?

Because a three-minute song needs 10 to 15 shots and each shot takes two or three generations to land, call it 12 shots and 36 takes. At Quality that's 3,600 credits, more than three months of Pro's monthly grant. At Fast it's 720 credits and fits in a month, but your finals ship at the draft tier.

If you've done any AI music video storyboarding, the retry rate won't surprise you. Prompts get revised, camera angles change, hands melt, lip-sync drifts. Every one of those takes costs the same credits as the keeper. The daily 50 helps if you're disciplined: generate every day for 30 days and you get 1,500 credits on top of the 1,000 the plan grants. They can't be saved up, and a Quality clip costs 100, so even if Flow lets you combine them with your monthly credits, they lift Pro to about 20 Quality clips a month at most. Miss a day and that day's 50 is gone.

The shape of the problem is the real issue, not the number. Music video work happens in bursts: a release week where you need 40 generations, then three weeks where you need none. A monthly allowance that expires is the wrong shape for that, whatever the tier. Our Veo 3.1 music video tutorial walks through the shot plan we use to keep the count down, but no shot plan turns 36 takes into 10.

How do you stretch Flow credits on Google AI Pro?

Draft cheap, finish expensive, and set outputs to one. Block shots on Gemini Omni Flash at 360p (6 credits for 8 seconds) or Veo 3.1 Lite (10 credits), lock composition on Fast (20), then spend Quality (100) only on the shots that carry the song. Done strictly, 1,000 credits covers 30 Lite drafts (300), 10 Fast passes (200) and 5 Quality finals (500).

  • Turn the output count down. Flow's default request can generate two videos. One Quality prompt at that default costs 200 credits. Set it to one before you render anything at the top tier.
  • Draft on Omni Flash or Lite. Google's API docs (updated June 30, 2026) now recommend Gemini Omni Flash as the default video model and reserve Veo 3.1 for scene extension and last-frame control. In Flow, Omni Flash at 360p is the cheapest way to test a prompt, and Veo Lite at 10 credits is the cheapest way to test it in the Veo look.
  • Use the daily 50 on purpose. A quick Lite draft each morning puts up to 1,500 credits a month to work that would otherwise expire. Nothing is added on a day you skip.
  • Upscale last, and only keepers. 1080p upscaling is free for subscribers, so there's no reason to pay a higher tier for resolution alone. 4K is Ultra-only at 50 credits.
  • Know your reset clocks. Monthly credits refresh at the start of your billing cycle, not the calendar month. Schedule the heavy day right after a reset.

These tactics cut waste. They don't change the ceiling. Ten Quality clips is ten Quality clips, and the month ends whether you used them or not.

Is Seedance a better deal than Google AI Pro for Veo music videos?

On rollover, yes. On Veo version, no, and we can't prove it's cheaper per Veo clip. Seedance, an independent pooled-credit platform at seeddance.video (its footer says it isn't affiliated with the model makers), sells Pro at $19.90 a month on a yearly plan for 350 credits, rated at up to 35 renders across all its engines, with unused credits rolling forward while you stay subscribed, but its engine menu says "Veo 3," not Veo 3.1, and it publishes no per-render Veo price, while Google's $19.99 buys 50 Veo 3.1 Fast clips.

Here's what its pricing page showed on October 21, 2026. Basic is $9.90 a month for 150 credits and up to 15 video renders. Pro is $19.90 for 350 credits and 35 renders. Max is $34.90 for 750 credits and 75 renders, and Pro Max is $59.90 for 1,500 credits and 150 renders.

All four are the annual rate the page displays by default (it labels that a 30 percent saving, so monthly billing costs more), and annual plans issue the full year's credits on day one: 4,200 on Pro. Every paid plan includes a commercial license and watermark-free exports. The free tier is one credit every 24 hours, which won't render a video.

Three honest caveats. The page carried a "price increase coming soon" banner when we read it, so the $19.90 figure may not hold. The footer still lists Sora 2 among its engines, though OpenAI's help center says the Sora API shut down on September 24, 2026, so the engine list changes without notice. And we couldn't open a Veo model page on the site today (the link returned a 404), so we can't confirm which Veo build sits behind the "Veo 3" label.

What you get that Google doesn't sell is a Music to Video tool that takes a whole song, MP3, WAV or M4A up to 10 minutes, and builds a video to its length. Veo can't take audio in any tier, and Google's Gemini Omni Flash accepts audio as an input but renders 10-second clips at most. It isn't cheap: the tool's page, read October 21, 2026, charges 2 credits a second at 480p and 4 at 720p, so a three-minute song at 720p costs 720 credits, about two months of the Pro grant, though failed generations are refunded. For a musician, that whole-song tool is often a bigger reason to be there than Veo is. Our AI video generator pricing comparison puts these plans next to Runway, Kling and the rest.

Who should stay on Google AI Pro, and who should leave?

Stay if you make a clip or two a week, if you'd pay for the bundle anyway (5 TB of storage, YouTube Premium Lite, Gemini in Gmail and Docs), or if you want Veo 3.1 Quality with native audio for a few hero shots a month. At $2.00 per Quality clip, Pro beats the Gemini API's $3.20 for an 8-second Standard clip. Leave if you storyboard whole videos in bursts.

Frequently asked questions

How many Veo videos a day do you get on Google AI Pro?+

There's no daily video count anymore. Since May 2026, Google AI Pro gets 1,000 Google Flow credits a month plus 50 free credits a day, and Veo 3.1 costs 100 credits per Quality clip, 20 per Fast clip and 10 per Lite clip. That's ten Quality clips a month from the subscription, or about 20 at most with the daily credits, which can't be saved up.

Do Google Flow credits roll over?+

No. Google's Flow credits help page, read October 21, 2026, says unused monthly credits don't carry into the next month and unused daily credits don't carry into the next day. Monthly credits refresh at the start of your billing cycle, not on the first of the month. Purchased AI credits are the exception; Google says those may expire on their own schedule instead.

Do failed or ugly Veo generations still use credits?+

Ugly ones do: Flow charges per generation whether you keep the clip or not, and a request that produces two videos charges twice. Failed ones are less clear-cut. In Google's Gemini community forum, a volunteer Product Expert wrote that credits for failed jobs are sometimes refunded automatically, though not always instantly, and pointed users to Flow's credit activity log and its Report a problem tool (support.google.com/gemini/thread/382593567, October 2025, read October 25, 2026). With two or three takes per usable shot, your real Quality capacity on AI Pro is closer to three or four finished shots a month than ten. Set outputs to one and draft on Lite or Omni Flash before you spend Quality credits.

Does the Gemini app use Veo 3.1 for video?+

Not anymore, according to Google's help page for video generation in Gemini Apps, which says videos there are made with Gemini Omni, Google's conversational video model from I/O 2026. Veo 3.1 is available in Google Flow and through the Gemini API. If you want Veo's look, scene extension or first-and-last-frame control, use Flow credits or pay per second on the API.

Can I buy more Flow credits without upgrading to Ultra?+

Yes, if you're on AI Pro or Ultra. Google's purchase page for AI credits describes one-time top-ups and an auto-reload option that apply to Flow and Antigravity, available in supported regions except Japan. The page we read on October 21, 2026 doesn't publish a per-credit price, so budget for a top-up only once you've seen the figure at checkout.

Is the Veo on Seedance the same as Google's Veo 3.1?+

Probably not. Seedance's engine list, read October 21, 2026, says "Veo 3," and we couldn't open a model page to confirm which build that is or what one render costs. You get Veo through one pooled credit balance that rolls over, which Google doesn't offer, but Veo 3.1 features such as scene extension and the Lite, Fast and Quality tiers are only guaranteed in Flow or the API.
