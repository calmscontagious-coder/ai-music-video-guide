Short answer: for drones, action cameras and phones, buy a U3, V30, A2 microSD such as the Samsung PRO Plus. For a mirrorless camera with a UHS-II slot, a V60 card such as the Lexar Professional 1800x covers most stills and 4K; step up to V90 only for 8K or very high-bitrate video. A Nintendo Switch 2 needs a microSD Express card; nothing else works for its games.

The speed classes, explained

A card carries several overlapping marks. The video marks (V30, V60, V90) are the ones to buy by, because they promise a minimum sustained write speed in MB/s, which is what video recording needs (SD Association). The big "up to" number on the front is a read speed, usually measured with the maker's own reader.

Sources: SD Association speed classes; Kingston's speed class guide for the A-ratings and bus limits. UHS-I cards rated above 104MB/s reach it with proprietary modes and a matching reader, as SanDisk and Samsung both footnote.

Match the card to the device

  • Drones: check your drone's recommended-card list. DJI publishes one per model; for the Mini 4 Pro it lists V30 and V90 cards (DJI).
  • Mirrorless cameras: use the camera manual's minimum for each video mode. A UHS-II card only runs at UHS-II speed in a UHS-II slot (Wirecutter).
  • Phones and tablets: look for A2 as well as V30, because apps and photo libraries are random reads and writes.
  • Nintendo Switch 2: microSD Express only. The original Switch takes ordinary microSD (Nintendo support).
  • Older devices: a slot marked SDHC takes cards up to 32GB, not SDXC (Wirecutter).

How to avoid fake cards

  • Buy from the brand's own store page or a retailer selling and shipping the card itself, not an unknown marketplace seller (Wirecutter).
  • Test every new card before you trust it: a full-capacity test such as H2testw writes the whole card and reads it back, so a fake reports its real size. It erases the card, so run it first (r/photography).
  • Tom's Hardware's database of more than 200 tested microSD cards includes fakes and endurance failures if you want to check a brand (Tom's Hardware).

Frequently asked questions

What does V30 mean on a memory card?+

V30 guarantees a minimum sustained write speed of 30MB/s, enough for 4K video in most phones, drones and action cameras. V60 and V90 guarantee 60MB/s and 90MB/s and need a UHS-II card.

Does the Nintendo Switch 2 work with a regular microSD card?+

Not for games. Nintendo says the Switch 2 is only compatible with microSD Express cards for game storage. The original Switch uses regular microSD cards.

Is a UHS-II card faster in a UHS-I camera?+

No. It works, but at UHS-I speed. Buy UHS-II only if your camera, or at least your card reader, has a UHS-II slot.

Why does my card not reach the speed on the label?+

The large number is usually a read speed measured with the maker's own reader. Write speeds are lower, and many UHS-I cards above 104MB/s need a matching reader to reach their rating.

How do I check if a memory card is fake?+

Run a full-capacity test such as H2testw on a new card before you use it. It writes the whole card and reads it back, so a fake shows its real size. It erases the card.
