What does "all-in-one" actually mean in AI subscriptions?

An all-in-one AI subscription takes one of two shapes. Shared-credit-pool suites — Seedance, ImagineArt, OVOV — let one credit currency buy video, music, images, or voice interchangeably. Bundled-access platforms — Krater, 1min.AI — put many models behind one login but center on chat and price media generation steeply. Only the shared-pool design reliably replaces a stack of separate tools.

Shared credit pool. The practical benefit is flexibility: a quiet music month becomes a heavy video month without waste, because unspent capacity isn't locked to a single modality.

Bundled access. Genuinely useful when chat models are your real workload — but do the math on video costs before assuming savings, since a single render can run 50 to several hundred credits.

Credit policy matters just as much as price: some platforms roll unused credits forward, others delete them monthly. That single detail can double or halve your effective cost.

The five contenders

Seedance — deepest engine bench for music-first creators

Seedance (note the double-d domain, seeddance.video — single-d lookalike sites are unrelated clones) puts an unusually long engine list on one credit pool. Verified on the platform in August 2026: video generation via Seedance 2.0 and 2.5, Sora 2, Sora 2 Pro, Veo 3, Kling, and Wan, with Grok Imagine a recent addition to the lineup; image tools running Flux Kontext, Flux Krea, SeeDream 4, and Nano Banana; music composed with the Suno engine; plus text-to-speech, text-to-dialogue, and speech-to-text. Its @-reference grammar lets one prompt combine up to 12 assets (9 images, 3 clips, 3 audio files), which is how you keep an artist's face and a song's audio consistent across a whole video campaign.

Pricing, verified on the Seeddance pricing page: Basic $9.90/month, Pro $19.90, Max $34.90, Pro Max $59.90 when billed annually (150, 350, 750, and 1,500 monthly credits respectively, with the full year's credits issued upfront). The plan cards print the per-credit rate — $0.066 on Basic, falling to $0.040 on Pro Max. Month-to-month billing is also offered and runs 20% to 50% more per credit depending on the plan; the annual toggle carries the page's "Save 30%" badge. Every paid plan includes watermark-free exports and a commercial license covering every output; the pricing FAQ confirms unused credits roll forward while the subscription stays active. Krater — strongest for chat, thinner on music

According to Krater's own pricing guide, Pro is $20/month for 1,500 credits, scaling to $119/month for 10,000; the live pricing page lists annual billing for Pro at $200/year — about $16.67/month — and that is the floor: there is no cheaper entry tier. You get 350+ chat models (GPT, Claude, Gemini), image generation via Flux and Seedream, video via "Veo, Kling, and Sora-class models," and voice cloning. Caveat for musicians: music appears in Krater's marketing copy but is not detailed in its pricing documentation, and video runs 50 to several hundred credits per generation — a full music video's worth of renders can exhaust the Pro allocation.

ImagineArt — biggest raw credit pools

Verified on ImagineArt's live pricing page in August 2026, during a limited-time "50% off" sale: Basic $9/month for 3,000 monthly credits, Standard $20 for 8,000, Ultimate $34 for 16,000, and Creator $175 for 100,000 — all billed yearly, marked down from list prices of $13, $30, $55, and $350. One account spans image, video (its plan comparison names Seedance 2.0, Kling 2.6, Google Veo 3.1, and Wan 2.6), and audio, including AI song and music generation plus voice clones. Two catches: those are promotional prices with a countdown clock attached, and the pricing FAQ states credits "reset at the start of each billing cycle" and "do not carry over" — light months are simply lost money.

1min.AI — cheapest entry, hardest math

According to Capterra's listing, 1min.AI's Pro plan starts around $5/month (Business is $8), with frequent lifetime deals. But the credit system is opaque — the listing documents no rollover policy, reviewers flag the lack of transparency, and a single image generation can consume over 10,000 credits — which makes cross-platform comparison genuinely difficult, and heavy video work drains balances fast. Fine for experiments; audit carefully before making it your production hub.

OVOV — never-expiring credits

Verified on OVOV's pricing page: Basic $9.90/month for 190 credits, Pro $29.90 for 650, Max $79.90 for 1,860 — all four modalities (image, video, music, voice) on one pool, no watermark, commercial license, and credits that never expire. At the entry tiers OVOV is actually the cheaper platform per credit — $0.052 versus Seedance Basic's $0.066 — though a credit buys different amounts of output on each platform, so per-credit price alone doesn't settle the comparison. Seedance's rate drops below OVOV's entry price at its higher tiers ($0.047 on Max, $0.040 on Pro Max), and its engine bench is broader; OVOV's no-expiry policy is the standout for irregular creators.

The workload test: one month of indie-artist output

Take a realistic release month: 1 song, 1 music video (assume ~8 video renders including retakes), 4 social clips, 1 cover image, and 1 voiceover for promos. Twelve video renders, one image, one song, one voice pass.

On the DIY stack that's $57 minimum — and Runway's 625 credits cover 104 seconds of Gen-4 Turbo footage per its own pricing page, so retake-heavy months push you toward top-ups. On Seedance, the plan cards price a video render at roughly 10 credits and an image at roughly 1: twelve renders ≈ 120 credits, leaving ample room for the song, voiceover, and cover inside Pro's 350 monthly credits at $19.90 on annual billing — and a lean month even fits Basic's 150 credits at $9.90 (annual-billing rates; billed monthly, Basic is $19.90 for 200 credits and Pro is $39.90 for 500). ImagineArt's Standard tier carries the largest raw pool here (8,000 credits), though per-model costs vary and anything unused vanishes at the cycle reset. Krater's Pro could run tight if renders land at the upper credit range, and OVOV's 190-credit Basic likely forces the $29.90 Pro for video-heavy months.

When does a dedicated AI tool still beat an all-in-one suite?

A dedicated tool wins when one modality dominates your workload. Producing stems inside Suno Studio requires Suno Premier at $30/month; generating hundreds of exploratory clips each month can justify Runway's Unlimited plan; and professional voice cloning remains strongest on ElevenLabs Creator at $22/month. An all-in-one suite pays off only when moderate output spreads across several modalities.

Honest limits of every suite, Seedance included:

Suno Studio. Seedance's Music Maker generates with the Suno engine, but Suno's stem-level DAW, MIDI export, and multi-track editing exist only on Suno Premier at $30/month. If you produce inside Studio rather than just generating songs, keep Premier. Our AI music generator roundup covers where each tool's ceiling sits.

Runway's unlimited iteration. If you generate hundreds of exploratory clips monthly, Runway's Unlimited plan changes the economics — we ran that math in our Runway Unlimited analysis.

Verdict

If your month spreads across a song, a video, clips, artwork, and a voiceover — the standard indie release cycle — consolidation wins clearly: $9.90 to $19.90 a month on Seedance at annual-billing rates ($19.90 to $39.90 billed monthly) versus $57+ stacked, with one credit pool absorbing whichever modality dominates that month. Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest all-in-one AI subscription covering video, music, and voice?+

1min.AI has the lowest entry price at roughly $5/month, but its credit system is opaque — rollover is undocumented and a single image generation can consume over 10,000 credits — so real costs are hard to predict. Among true shared-credit-pool suites, Seedance Basic and OVOV Basic both start at $9.90/month; Seedance includes 150 monthly credits on annual billing with rollover, while OVOV includes 190 credits that never expire.

Does Seedance really use Suno for music generation?+

Yes. The reseller's homepage states the platform lets you "compose music with Suno," and every paid plan carries a commercial license covering every output, music included. What you don't get is Suno Studio — the stem-level DAW with MIDI export — which is exclusive to Suno's own $30/month Premier plan.

Do credits roll over on all-in-one AI platforms?+

Policies differ sharply. Seedance rolls unused credits forward while your subscription is active; OVOV credits never expire; Krater credits reset monthly; ImagineArt's own pricing FAQ says credits reset at the start of each billing cycle with no carry-over; and 1min.AI's credit policy is opaque — rollover is not documented on its pricing listing. For irregular creative schedules, rollover policy matters as much as headline price.

Is an all-in-one platform always cheaper than separate subscriptions?+

No. Heavy specialist use still favors dedicated tools: producing inside Suno Studio requires Suno Premier, hundreds of exploratory renders monthly can justify Runway Unlimited, and professional voice cloning is still strongest on a dedicated AI voice generator's Creator plan. Consolidation wins when moderate output is spread across several modalities — the typical indie release month.

Does the Seedance free tier include a commercial license?+

No. The free tier grants 1 credit per 24 hours, outputs are watermarked, and there is no commercial license. Watermark-free MP4/PNG exports and a commercial license covering every output are included on all paid plans, which start at $9.90/month billed annually; month-to-month billing starts at $19.90 and costs 20% to 50% more per credit depending on the plan.
