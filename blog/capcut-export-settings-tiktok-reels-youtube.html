Best CapCut Export Settings for Reels, TikTok and YouTube

By the AI Music Video GuideUpdated November 10, 20264 min read

Quick answer: For vertical video, export 1080 x 1920 at 30 fps in H.264; CapCut's own Reels guide suggests 4,000 to 6,000 Kbps at 1080p. For YouTube, keep the frame rate you recorded in and use YouTube's recommended 8 Mbps for 1080p at 30 fps, or 12 Mbps at 60 fps.

What export settings should I use for each platform?

Match the platform, not the maximum. The figures below are published by the platforms or by CapCut.

Instagram Reels1080 x 1920 (9:16)30 fps4,000 to 6,000 KbpsCapCut Reels guide

Instagram Help sets the floor for Reels: an aspect ratio between 1.91:1 and 9:16, at least 30 fps, and at least 720 pixels of resolution. CapCut's Reels guide recommends H.264 video with AAC audio.

For ordinary TikTok posts we found no bitrate figure published by the platform. Its ad specification lists a minimum of 516 kbps, which is a floor and not a target. The same vertical 1080 x 1920, 30 fps file you make for Reels is the sensible starting point there.

YouTube Help asks for H.264 video, AAC-LC or Opus audio, and the same frame rate the footage was recorded in. Its bitrates above are for standard dynamic range uploads.

Where are the export settings in CapCut?

They are part of the Export step on every version. CapCut's export help page describes each:

  • Phone app: look at the top right of the editor. Beside Export is a resolution label such as 1080P. Tap it to choose 720P, 1080P, and 2K or 4K where your phone supports them.
  • Desktop app: click Export at the top right. The panel lets you set resolution, frame rate up to 60 fps depending on the source, and bitrate.
  • Web editor: click Export and choose the resolution. Higher options appear only when the project and the browser support them.

Can I export 4K from CapCut?

Yes, with conditions. The desktop app has full 2K (2560 x 1440) and 4K (3840 x 2160) export. The phone app offers them only on devices that can handle it. The web editor offers 4K when the browser supports hardware-accelerated encoding and the project is set up for it.

Two limits are worth knowing. On the phone, CapCut says you cannot export 4K if every clip in the project is 1080p or lower, and the web editor offers a resolution only when the project holds media of at least that size. In its notes on the web editor, CapCut adds that free accounts may meet watermarks or bitrate limits on 4K exports, while Pro subscribers get unrestricted 4K. If 4K delivery is part of your work, check CapCut Pro.

For desktop, CapCut lists macOS Monterey 12.0 or later, or Windows 10 64-bit or later.

Why does my CapCut export look blurry?

First check whether it really is. CapCut's help page on blurry video says the editor preview is deliberately lower quality to keep editing smooth, and the exported file keeps the resolution of the source. Play the exported file on another screen before changing anything.

If the file itself is soft, CapCut points to these causes:

  • The first clip set the project size. On the phone app, the timeline takes the resolution of the first clip you import and does not upscale later. Start with your highest-resolution clip.
  • The export resolution is lower than the source. Set it to match, 1080p or 4K.
  • The bitrate is low. On the phone, set bitrate to High or Highest.
  • You zoomed in. Digital zoom or crop beyond 100 percent pixelates footage.
  • A web template locked the size. Starting from a template in the web editor may fix the project at 1080p.

Why does my exported video stutter?

CapCut's help page on lagging exports blames settings the device cannot keep up with. On a computer, 4K or 60 fps exports can exceed what the machine can process; update the graphics driver, turn on hardware acceleration, close other programs, and lower the resolution, frame rate or bitrate.

On a phone, free up storage, restart, update the app and export at 1080p or lower. In the web editor, use Chrome or Edge, close other tabs and try 1080p instead of 4K.

What is a safe default if I post everywhere?

Export one vertical master at 1080 x 1920, 30 fps, with the bitrate set high, and one horizontal master at 1920 x 1080 for YouTube. Those two files cover the short-video apps and a standard YouTube upload without re-editing.

Check the vertical file for a leftover ending clip before posting; our guide to exporting without a watermark shows where it hides. For turning one song into many cuts, see one song, twenty videos.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best resolution to export from CapCut?

Match where the video is going. For vertical short video, 1080 x 1920 at 30 fps is the standard CapCut's own Reels guide recommends. For YouTube, export at the resolution you shot in, commonly 1920 x 1080. Exporting above the resolution of your source clips adds file size without adding detail.

What bitrate should I use in CapCut for Instagram Reels?

CapCut's Reels guide suggests 4,000 to 6,000 Kbps for a 1080p Reel, with H.264 video and AAC audio at 30 fps. Instagram's own help page sets minimums only: at least 30 fps and at least 720 pixels of resolution, in an aspect ratio between 1.91:1 and 9:16.

Can CapCut export in 4K for free?

CapCut's help page, in its notes on the web editor, says free accounts may meet watermarks or bitrate limits on 4K exports, and that Pro subscribers get unrestricted 4K. You also need source clips of at least 4K, and a device that supports it. The desktop app has the fullest 2K and 4K support.

Why is my CapCut video blurry after export?

Often it is only the preview, which CapCut lowers on purpose. If the exported file is soft, start the project with your highest-resolution clip, export at the source resolution, raise the bitrate setting, and avoid zooming past 100 percent. A web template can also lock a project at 1080p.

Should I export at 30 or 60 fps from CapCut?

Use the frame rate you recorded in. YouTube Help asks for uploads in the same frame rate as the recording, and CapCut's Reels guide treats 30 fps as the standard for vertical video. Exports at 60 fps need more bitrate and more processing power, and CapCut notes that 4K or 60 fps exports can be more than a computer can process.
