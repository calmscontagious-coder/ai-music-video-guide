CapCut on Desktop vs Phone vs Web: Which One to Use

Quick answer: Use the phone app for fast vertical edits and the widest template library, the desktop app for long projects and full 2K and 4K export control, and the web editor for quick starts, iPad editing and managing a shared workspace. One Pro subscription covers all three; the Standard plan covers the phone app only.

Which version of CapCut should I use?

Pick by the job. CapCut's own comparison puts it this way: the PC version is strongest when a project needs heavier revision and deliberate finishing, the web version suits quick starts and lighter tasks, and the phone version fits fast edits that begin with footage you just shot.

Does one subscription work on all three?

With Pro, yes. CapCut's help page on cross-platform use says Pro members get full benefits in the phone app on iOS and Android, the desktop app on PC and Mac, and the web editor. You can start a project on one device and continue on another. Pro also carries 1 TB of cloud storage and 1,200 credits a month for AI features, and those follow the account, not the device.

Standard is narrower. CapCut says it is sold in certain regions and that its benefits apply in the phone app only. Paid features on a computer need Pro.

One practical rule from CapCut: sign in with the same account, by the same method, everywhere. Its help centre warns that each login method creates a separate account, even with the same email address, so a Pro plan bought under one login will not show up under another.

Why does Pro cost more on desktop?

CapCut says the difference is deliberate. Its pricing help page explains that the desktop version is built for longer sessions, higher-resolution projects and more complex workflows, with different membership benefits and higher maintenance costs. You may subscribe wherever you prefer.

Compare the two prices before paying. Our guide to CapCut plans and prices shows where each price is displayed, or you can open the CapCut plans page directly. New subscribers in supported regions can use a 7-day trial to test Pro on the device they plan to pay on.

What is the phone app best at?

Templates and speed. CapCut's help page on missing templates calls the phone app the primary place where new templates and effects are released. Desktop gets a curated subset, sometimes weeks or months later, and the web editor shows a general, limited set.

The phone is also where the Standard plan lives, and where Auto Cut and auto captions sit beside the footage you just shot. If your video starts as phone footage and ends as a vertical post, you may never need another device. Our phone-only music video workflow is built on that idea.

What is the desktop app best at?

Control and long timelines. CapCut's export help page gives desktop full 2K (2560 x 1440) and 4K (3840 x 2160) export, with frame rate up to 60 fps and an adjustable bitrate. It recommends desktop for 4K projects for the most control and the fastest rendering.

Requirements are modest: macOS Monterey 12.0 or later, or Windows 10 64-bit or later, with enough processor, graphics power and free disk space for the footage you are cutting.

What desktop cannot do: buy a Teams plan, invite collaborators or change workspace settings. CapCut sends those tasks to the web.

What is the web editor for?

Three things. It runs in a browser, with nothing to install. It is where shared workspaces are managed: member invitations, roles and brand kit settings all live in CapCut Online. And CapCut calls it the most accurate view of your subscription status.

Its limits are real. Auto Cut is not available on the web, the template set is the smallest, and 4K export depends on your browser; CapCut names Chrome as the most reliable.

Can I use CapCut on an iPad?

Through the browser, yes. CapCut's iPad help page says its dedicated iPad app is in beta development and is not in the U.S. App Store. In the meantime it recommends CapCut Online:

  1. Open Safari or Chrome on the iPad.
  2. Go to capcut.com.
  3. Sign in, or create an account.
  4. Start editing.

Frequently asked questions

Is CapCut better on PC or on a phone?

It depends on the job. CapCut's own comparison says the PC version is strongest for heavier revision and finishing, and the phone app fits fast edits that start from footage you just shot. Desktop has full 2K and 4K export control; the phone app gets the newest templates first.

Can I use my CapCut Pro subscription on both phone and computer?

Yes. CapCut says Pro benefits apply in the phone app, the desktop app and the web editor when you sign in with the same account. The Standard plan is the exception: its benefits apply in the phone app only, so paid features on a computer need Pro.

Is there a CapCut app for iPad?

CapCut's help page says a dedicated iPad app is in beta development and is not in the U.S. App Store. It recommends using CapCut Online in Safari or Chrome on the iPad instead: go to capcut.com, sign in, and edit in the browser.

Why are some CapCut templates missing on my computer?

Templates are released on the phone app first. CapCut says the desktop app shows a curated subset of the library and the web editor a smaller general set. If you need a specific template, apply it on your phone, then open the synced project on desktop to keep editing.

What computer do I need to run CapCut on desktop?

CapCut lists macOS Monterey 12.0 or later, or Windows 10 64-bit or later. For 2K and 4K exports the computer also needs enough processor and graphics power and free disk space. If exports stutter, CapCut suggests updating the graphics driver and lowering resolution, frame rate or bitrate.
