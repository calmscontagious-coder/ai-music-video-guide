Short answer: for most musicians, the gift that helps right away is a pair of closed-back studio headphones such as the Sony MDR-7506. For a vocalist or podcaster in an ordinary bedroom, a dynamic mic such as the Samson Q2U beats a costlier condenser. For someone who records every week, the Shure MV7+ or a quiet Mac mini is the big upgrade.

How to pick for the musician you know

  • Ask what they record. Vocals, voice and podcasts point to a mic; beats and mixing point to headphones or a computer.
  • Think about the room, not only the gear. A dynamic mic suits an echoey bedroom; a condenser suits a quiet, treated room.
  • Check what it connects to. XLR mics need an audio interface and a cable; USB mics plug straight in.
  • Check the delivery date on the product page before you order for a holiday date.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good gift for a musician who records at home?

Closed-back studio headphones such as the Sony MDR-7506 or Audio-Technica ATH-M50x. They help with every recording and mix, whatever else is in the studio.

Which microphone should I gift for a bedroom studio?

A dynamic mic such as the Samson Q2U or Shure MV7+, because it picks up less room echo than a condenser. A condenser such as the AT2020 suits a quiet, treated room.

Does an XLR microphone need anything else?

Yes. It needs an audio interface, such as the Focusrite Scarlett Solo, and an XLR cable. A USB mic plugs straight into a computer.
