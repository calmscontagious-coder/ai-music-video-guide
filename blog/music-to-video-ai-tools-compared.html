Music-to-Video AI Tools Compared (2026): Upload a Song, Get a Finished Video

By the AI Music Video Guide team · Updated August 4, 2026 · 8 min read

This post contains affiliate links — if you sign up through them we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you (disclosure). Researched and edited for accuracy with AI assistance.

Quick answer: If you want to upload a finished song and get a complete, beat-synced video back with zero prompting, BeatViz is the cheapest dedicated option at roughly $3.30–$5.95 per finished 3-minute video depending on tier, and Seedance's Music to Video tool is the most capable one — fully generated footage, lyric-accurate lip sync, and output up to 10 minutes, priced at 2–4 credits per second of audio. Freebeat and Revid are built around short-form clips, and Pictory is a stock-footage assembler aimed at spoken audio rather than music.

Who this roundup is for

You have a finished master — a Suno export, a bounce from your DAW, a mixed single — and no video skills. You do not want to storyboard scenes, write camera prompts, or stitch ten-second clips together. You want the platform to listen to the track and build the video. That makes this a different category from prompt-driven video generators: here the song is the only required input, and the tool does the directing.

It is a specific product category, and it works the same way everywhere: the tool runs audio analysis (beat detection, tempo, section changes, and in the better tools, vocal isolation for lip sync), then generates or selects visuals, then assembles everything so cuts land on the rhythm. The quality differences come from two things: how deep the audio analysis goes, and whether the visuals are generated from scratch or pulled from stock and template libraries.

If you already know you want shot-by-shot control, this is not your article — the directed workflow linked at the end is your lane. This piece is for one-click auto-generation, including an honest look at where it dead-ends.

The five tools, reviewed

Seedance Music to Video — deepest auto-generation, longest output

Seedance's Music to Video is the closest thing here to a full production pipeline in one upload. You feed it an MP3, WAV, or M4A from 5 seconds up to 10 minutes (50 MB cap), optionally add up to 3 reference photos so the same performer appears in every scene, optionally type a short scene description, and pick 9:16, 16:9, 1:1, or 4:5. The engine analyzes the vocal itself, so mouth movement follows the actual sung words, and scene changes lock to the beat. According to the tool's own documentation, renders are billed at 2 credits per second of audio for standard resolution or 4 credits per second for HD, failed generations are refunded automatically, and processing takes roughly 3–6 minutes per minute of audio.

The economics are the honest catch. The free tier grants +1 credit every 24 hours, keeps a watermark, and carries no commercial license — you cannot meaningfully test a full song without paying. Paid plans run $9.90 (Basic, 150 credits/month) to $59.90 (Pro Max, 1,500 credits/month), all watermark-free with a commercial license, and unused credits roll over. The differentiator is that those same credits also drive the platform's video engines — Seedance 2.0, Sora 2, Sora 2 Pro, Veo 3, Kling, Wan, Grok Imagine — plus image tools and a Suno-powered music maker, so one pool covers your whole pipeline. Freebeat — the everything bundle

Freebeat pairs beat-synced video generation with a stack of licensed music and video engines (Suno V5, Seedance 2.0, PixVerse, Veo among them). Current live pricing: Basic $4.99/week (1,990 credits, promo from $6.99), Pro $26.99/month (10,000 credits), Ultimate $39.99/month (19,000), Creator $199/month (95,000). The free tier is real but tight: according to NemoVideo's review, which quotes Freebeat's own Terms of Service, it gives 500 non-expiring credits, videos capped at 30 seconds, 720p output with a watermark, and non-commercial use only; paid plans lift the cap to around 6 minutes. Per-video cost varies widely because each engine bills credits differently. We dig into the company itself in is Freebeat legit.

Revid — short-form promo machine

Revid's music video generator takes an uploaded MP3/WAV or — usefully — a pasted Spotify or Suno link, detects rhythm and structure, and builds a beat-synced vertical video with lyric captions. You choose stock footage, AI-generated clips, or animated AI images as the visual bed, then refine in its editor. Source: Revid's tool page. Pricing sits at $39/month (Hobby), $39/month for Growth (listed at $99 with a locked-in discount, 2,000 credits/month), and $199/month for Ultra with 12,000 credits. The catch: Revid is squarely a TikTok/Reels/Shorts tool. It is excellent for 30-second promo clips of a song, and the wrong tool for a full-length video.

BeatViz — cheapest full-song specialist

BeatViz is the budget pick for full-length videos, and it has grown from a pure music-video tool into a bundle that also includes AI song generation. Its live pricing page lists three paid tiers on annual billing: Basic at $18.13/month (list $25.90) with 2,059 credits — enough for about 9 minutes of music video per month — Pro at $34.90 (6,600 credits, about 29 minutes), and Creator at $90.90 (18,216 credits, about 82 minutes); monthly billing runs higher. Every paid tier includes 1080p export, no watermark, and commercial use rights (source: BeatViz pricing). That works out to roughly $1.10–$1.98 per finished minute — about $3.30–$5.95 for a 3-minute video, cheaper the higher the tier, and the lowest real number in this roundup. The free plan is a demo: 20 credits, a 15-second audio cap, and watermarked 720p exports, confirmed by both the pricing page and AI Tools' listing. Visual range is style-template driven, so expect polished visualizer-adjacent output rather than cinematic scenes.

Pictory — right tool, wrong genre

Pictory markets an Audio to Video workflow that, in its own words, transforms podcasts, voiceovers, and music into videos with visuals, captions, and animations — it transcribes the audio and matches stock footage to it. That is genuinely useful for spoken content, but there is no beat-locked scene generation and no generative footage. Plans run $29/month Starter ($25 annual, 200 export minutes/month), $59 Professional ($35 annual, 600 minutes), and $199 Team ($119 annual, 1,800 minutes), with a 14-day free trial. If your track has lyrics you want visualized on rhythm, Pictory will feel like a slideshow.

Where one-click auto-generation falls short

Be clear-eyed about the ceiling. Auto-generation reads the audio, not your intent, and it fails predictably in three places. Narrative fails first: if your song tells a story, no audio analyzer knows the verse about leaving home should show a car and the bridge should show a kitchen — you get mood, not plot. Character consistency is second: Seedance's reference photos hold one performer across scenes, but nothing here maintains a cast, wardrobe changes, or a location that recurs with meaning. Lyric-specific imagery is third: lip sync tracks the words, but visuals only loosely follow them — a lyric that name-checks a specific image usually will not get it.

When any of those three matter, graduate to the directed workflow: generate scenes shot-by-shot with written prompts, keep your references locked, and cut to the beat yourself. Verdict by use case

Fastest: Revid, if short-form is the goal — paste a Suno or Spotify link and a captioned vertical clip comes back in minutes. Cheapest per finished video: BeatViz, at about $3.30–$5.95 for three 1080p watermark-free minutes with commercial rights — cheapest on its higher tiers. Best-looking full-length output: Seedance Music to Video — it is the only tool here generating original footage with vocal-driven lip sync at up to 10 minutes. Most controllable: none of them, honestly; that is what the manual workflow is for.

If you expect to make more than one video, the credit-pool argument favors Seedance: the same balance that renders your music video also covers Sora 2, Veo 3, and Kling renders, image generation for cover art, and its built-in Suno music engine. Plans start at $9.90/month with commercial licensing on every paid tier — you can start with a Basic plan and run one full-song test before committing further. If the output reads as a visualizer to you, drop to BeatViz for volume work; if it reads close-but-not-quite, the directed workflow is your next step, not a sixth tool.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best music to video AI tool in 2026?+

It depends on the job. BeatViz is the cheapest for full-song videos (about $3.30-$5.95 per finished 3-minute video at 1080p with commercial rights, depending on tier). Seedance's Music to Video produces the most capable output — fully generated footage with vocal-driven lip sync, up to 10 minutes long. Revid is best for short-form TikTok/Reels promo clips, Freebeat bundles music and video engines under one subscription, and Pictory only suits spoken audio like podcasts.

Can I make an AI music video for free?+

Technically yes, but not one you can publish commercially. Every free tier in this comparison watermarks its output: Freebeat's free tier caps videos at 30 seconds in 720p and is non-commercial per its Terms of Service, BeatViz's free plan gives just 20 credits and caps audio at 15 seconds, and Seedance's free tier grants just 1 credit per 24 hours with no commercial license. Watermark-free output with commercial rights requires a paid tier on all five platforms.

How much does a 3-minute AI music video cost?+

On BeatViz, roughly $3.30-$5.95 depending on tier — annual plans work out to about $1.10-$1.98 per finished minute (Basic at $18.13/month covers about 9 minutes of video per month; Creator at $90.90 covers about 82). On Seedance's Music to Video, a 3-minute song costs 360 credits at the standard rate or 720 credits at HD — roughly $14-$24 or $29-$48 respectively at annual-billing credit rates. Subscription tools like Revid ($39/month) and Pictory (from $25/month annual) bill monthly rather than per video.

How long can an AI-generated music video be?+

Seedance's Music to Video renders up to 10 minutes (600 seconds), matching your audio length exactly, and accepts MP3, WAV, or M4A files up to 50MB. Freebeat's paid plans reach about 6 minutes. BeatViz allocates monthly video minutes by tier (about 9 to 82 minutes), so full songs are possible. Revid focuses on short-form clips for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts, and Pictory limits you by total export minutes per month rather than per video.

When should I use a manual workflow instead of one-click music-to-video generation?+

When narrative, character consistency, or lyric-specific scenes matter. Auto-generation reads your audio, not your intent — it delivers mood and beat-synced cuts, not plot. If your video needs a story arc, a recurring cast or location, or visuals that match specific lyrics, direct each scene yourself with written prompts and cut to the beat manually.
