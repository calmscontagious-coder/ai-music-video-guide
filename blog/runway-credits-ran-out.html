Runway Credits Ran Out Mid-Project? Your Real Options, With Honest Math

By the AI Music Video GuideUpdated July 31, 20266 min read

Quick answer: When your Runway credits run out mid-project, you have three real options: buy top-up credits at $0.01 per credit ($10 minimum), upgrade to the next plan tier, or route the rest of your shot list to a cheaper per-generation platform and keep Runway for the shots only Runway does well. There is no unlimited fallback anymore — Runway retired the old Unlimited plan and its Explore mode, and the current top self-serve tier, Max ($76/month billed annually), is still fully credit-metered. The honest math: finishing a 3-minute music video entirely on Gen-4.5 burns roughly 6,500–13,500 credits (about $65–135 in top-ups), while a hybrid workflow — Runway for hero shots and Act-Two, a cheaper platform like Seedance (plans from $9.90/month) for B-roll volume — usually costs less than half that.

You are mid-project, the credit counter hit zero, and the paste-another-prompt muscle memory just slammed into a paywall. Before you reflex-buy a top-up pack, it is worth ten minutes to run the numbers, because the cheapest path depends on what kind of shots you still need.

How much do Runway credits actually cost right now?

As of July 2026, Runway's self-serve plans are: Free (125 one-time credits), Standard at $12/month billed annually ($15 month-to-month) with 625 credits/month, Pro at $28/month annually ($35 monthly) with 2,250 credits/month, and Max at $76/month annually ($95 monthly) with 9,500 credits/month plus one month of credit rollover (source).

Two things matter most when you have run dry. First, extra credits are sold as top-ups at $0.01 per credit, with a 1,000-credit ($10) minimum purchase (source), available on Standard and above — and unlike your monthly allowance, top-up credits do not expire at the end of the cycle (source). Second, the escape hatch a lot of long-time users remember is gone: the old Unlimited plan with Explore mode was retired in 2026, replaced by the purely credit-based Max tier (source, corroborated here). Runway's current pricing page lists no unlimited or relaxed-mode option on any plan (source). If your budget plan was "upgrade to Unlimited and grind," that plan no longer exists.

Here is the burn rate that emptied your balance, converted to top-up dollars per raw minute of generated footage. As of July 2026, Runway's pricing page lists Gen-4.5 at 60 credits per 5-second generation (12 credits/second), while independent breakdowns put full-quality Gen-4.5 output as high as 25 credits/second depending on version and settings — so budget for the range (source, source).

What does a finished 3-minute music video cost on Runway?

Raw minutes are not finished minutes. In practice you generate several takes per shot and keep the best one — a 3:1 selection ratio is a realistic floor for music video work. So a 3-minute finished video needs roughly 9 minutes of raw generation:

  • All Gen-4.5: ~540 seconds × 12–25 credits = 6,480–13,500 credits. That is $65–$135 in top-ups, or most-to-all of a Max plan's 9,500 monthly credits.
  • All Gen-4 Turbo: ~2,700 credits. A Pro plan (2,250/month) plus a $10 top-up covers it — you would use about 450 of the 1,000 top-up credits, and the rest bank, since purchased credits do not expire (source). But Turbo is the draft-quality tier, not what you want your single riding on.
  • Realistic mix (hero shots on Gen-4.5, connective tissue on Turbo): typically 3,500–6,000 credits, i.e. Pro plus $15–40 in top-ups, every video.

If those numbers feel high, they are — Runway is priced as a pro filmmaking tool, not a volume B-roll machine. We break down full-project budgets across platforms in our AI music video cost guide and the wider pricing comparison.

Should you top up, upgrade, or switch?

  1. Top up ($10+) if you are genuinely near the finish line — a few hero shots short. Top-ups do not expire monthly, so nothing is wasted.
  2. Upgrade to Pro or Max if you make videos every month and consistently blow through your allowance. Max at $76/month annually with rollover is honest value for heavy Runway-native workflows — it just should not be your B-roll budget.
  3. Split the workload if most of what you still need is coverage: performance angles, location shots, texture cuts. That volume does not need Runway's premium models, and paying Gen-4.5 rates for it is how credits vanish.

When is staying on Runway the right call?

Honestly: often, for part of the video. Act-Two's performance capture — driving a generated character with your own filmed performance — has no direct equivalent on the budget platforms, and at 5 credits/second it is actually one of Runway's cheapest tools (source). Aleph-style targeted video editing is likewise a Runway strength. If your remaining shot list is "the two close-ups where my character actually sells the chorus," buy the $10 top-up and stay put. Cancelling Runway to save money and then losing your hero-shot pipeline is a false economy — we tell people the same thing in reverse about Seedance in our legitimacy review.

What about Kling or Veo instead?

Kling 3.0 can extend a single clip to around three minutes total, which suits long continuous takes, and Veo 3.1 reaches roughly 148 seconds through chained 7-second extensions — we compare the approaches in our guide to longer AI videos and go deep on Kling in Kling vs Seedance for music videos. Running out of credits is annoying, but it is also useful information: it means your workflow is routing volume work through a premium-priced tool. Fix the routing and the wall stops appearing.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my Runway credits run out so fast?+

Runway's premium models burn credits quickly: Gen-4.5 costs roughly 12-25 credits per second of video depending on version and settings, so a single 10-second generation can cost 120-250 credits. The Standard plan's 625 monthly credits can disappear in under a minute of Gen-4.5 footage. Add multiple takes per shot (a 3:1 generate-to-keep ratio is normal) and even the Pro plan's 2,250 credits covers only a few finished minutes.

Does Runway still have an Unlimited plan?+

No. As of July 2026, Runway has retired the old Unlimited plan and its Explore mode. The current top self-serve tier is Max at $76/month billed annually ($95 month-to-month), which includes 9,500 credits per month with one month of rollover — but it is still fully credit-metered, with no unlimited or relaxed generation mode.

Is it cheaper to buy Runway top-up credits or upgrade my plan?+

Per credit, top-ups are actually slightly cheaper: 2,250 credits costs $22.50 in top-ups versus $28/month for the Pro plan billed annually. The case for upgrading is convenience and features, not price — a higher tier renews automatically every month, unlocks that tier's plan features, and saves you repeated manual purchases. Rule of thumb: buy top-ups for a one-off project crunch; upgrade a tier if you overshoot your allowance every month and want it handled automatically.

What is the cheapest way to finish a music video when Runway credits run out?+

Split the workload. Keep Runway (a $10 top-up or your existing plan) for hero shots and Act-Two performance capture, and generate B-roll and coverage on a cheaper per-generation platform. Seedance 2.0, for example, starts at $9.90/month, generates up to 15-second clips, and accepts your music track as a reference input. Finishing all footage on Runway Gen-4.5 can cost $65-135 per 3-minute video; a hybrid setup typically runs under half that.

Do Runway credits roll over to the next month?+

Mostly no. Standard and Pro plan credits reset each month and unused credits are lost. The Max plan is the exception — it allows unused credits to roll over for one month. Purchased top-up credits are different: they do not expire on a monthly cycle, which makes them safer to buy than an upgrade if your usage is occasional.
