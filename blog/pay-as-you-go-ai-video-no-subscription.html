Quick answer: If you make videos in occasional bursts instead of every week, an AI video generator with no subscription — Karya, JAI Portal, or MaxVideoAI — usually beats a rolling monthly plan, because their credits don't expire between projects. The exception is the month you actually produce a full video: once you need roughly 15 or more premium-engine clips in a single month, a cheap subscription with rollover credits (Seedance Basic at $9.90 for 150 credits) undercuts pay-as-you-go per-clip rates, and you can cancel when the release cycle ends.

The quarterly-release math: why subscription fatigue is rational

Say you release one music video per quarter. A $30/month video tool costs $360 a year for four bursts of actual work — that's $90 per video, and you paid for roughly eight idle months. Most subscription platforms make this worse by expiring unused monthly credits, so the idle months aren't even banking anything. Resenting that billing model isn't cheapness; it's arithmetic. What does "pay as you go" actually mean in AI video?

Pay-as-you-go AI video means you pay only for the clips you generate — no recurring monthly charge. Two billing models get sold under the label: one-time credit packs that you top up (Karya, JAI Portal) and metered per-clip pricing quoted live before each generation (MaxVideoAI). Three clauses decide whether either deal is good: credit expiry, commercial-license terms, and the failed-generation refund policy.

Credit packs. You buy a one-time bundle of credits, spend them across engines at different rates, and top up when you run out. Karya and JAI Portal work this way. The critical fine print is whether the pack expires.

Metered generation. The platform quotes a live dollar price per clip — based on engine, duration, and resolution — before you press generate. MaxVideoAI works this way on top of a credit balance. The critical fine print is the per-engine price spread, which can be enormous.

The hidden costs: retry burn, engine spreads, resolution surcharges

Retry burn. Auto-refunds only cover technical failures. A render that completes successfully but looks wrong — mangled hands, drifting faces, a camera move you hate — still burns credits. For planning, budget 2–3 generations per keeper clip; that multiplier applies identically to PAYG and subscriptions, but on PAYG you feel it in dollars per press. (Platforms differ on what counts as "failed" — see what happened when we audited Kling's failed-generation credit policy.)

Per-engine price spreads. On MaxVideoAI, the same 10 seconds of 1080p video costs $0.52 on LTX 2.3 Fast and $3.79 on Seedance 2.0 — a 7x spread on one platform. On Karya the spread is wider still: 3 credits to 630 credits depending on engine and mode. PAYG headline prices are always quoted from the cheapest engine; the engines musicians actually want for polished visuals sit at the expensive end.

Resolution surcharges. MaxVideoAI's LTX 2.3 Fast jumps from $0.52 at 1080p to $2.08 at 4K for the same 10-second clip — a 4x multiplier for pixels alone. If you're producing for YouTube rather than TikTok, price your project at the resolution you'll actually deliver.

When does a cheap subscription beat pay-as-you-go?

A cheap subscription overtakes pay-as-you-go at roughly 15 premium-engine clips per month. Seedance's Basic plan is $9.90/month for 150 credits — about 15 video renders, $0.66 per clip — with watermark-free exports and a commercial license included, and unused monthly credits roll forward while the plan stays active (Source: Seedance pricing page). Metered premium rates of $1.46–$3.79 per clip pass that total fast.

That one Basic credit pool is shared across Seedance 2.0, Sora 2, Veo 3, Kling, and Wan — the same premium engines the PAYG platforms meter individually. Here's the honest math those platforms don't show you:

Verdict by release cadence — and the hybrid play

1–2 videos per year, budget engines fine: JAI Portal or Karya. Non-expiring packs, a few dollars per project, and Karya's 200 free signup credits may cover your first tests entirely.

Quarterly releases with premium engines: the hybrid play. Run PAYG (MaxVideoAI for its pre-generation price quotes) during idle months for tests and single clips, then buy one subscription month for each release burst and cancel afterward. Seedance makes this practical: cancellation is a self-serve button in the order menu — we documented the exact steps in our guide to canceling a Seedance subscription — and unused credits stay live through the end of the billing period. One Basic month at $9.90 during a release cycle costs less than most PAYG platforms charge for four premium clips.

Monthly or faster output: a subscription with rollover credits simply wins; PAYG per-clip premiums compound every month you create.

One caution regardless of route: don't judge any of these platforms by their free tiers. Seedance's free tier, for instance, grants just one credit per 24 hours, outputs are watermarked, and there's no commercial license — watermark-free exports and commercial rights start at the paid tiers. The billing model you choose matters far more than the free credits you're greeted with.

Frequently asked questions

Do pay-as-you-go AI video credits really never expire?+

It depends on the platform, so audit the claim before buying. Karya states on both its pricing page and blog that credits never expire, and JAI Portal's pricing FAQ says credits are "yours forever" — both claims hold as published. MaxVideoAI publishes no expiry window on its pricing page, so confirm in-app before loading a large balance. Seedance's one-time packs carry roughly a ten-year validity window, and its subscription credits roll over month-to-month while the plan stays active.

Is there a truly free AI video generator with no subscription?+

You can test for free, but not produce for free. Karya gives 200 free credits on signup with no card, JAI Portal gives 10, and Seedance's free tier grants one credit per 24 hours with watermarked output and no commercial license. Free credits are enough to evaluate engines — a releasable, watermark-free video with commercial rights requires paid credits on every platform we tested.

When does a subscription beat pay-as-you-go for AI video?+

At roughly 15 premium-engine clips per month. Metered PAYG rates for engines like Kling 3 Pro ($1.46/10s) or Seedance 2.0 ($3.79/10s on MaxVideoAI) overtake Seedance's Basic plan ($9.90 for about 15 renders) fast. A full 3-minute music video — about 23 final clips plus retries — costs $102–$265 on metered premium PAYG versus $34.90 for one Seedance Max month.

Do failed AI video generations refund my credits?+

MaxVideoAI and JAI Portal both refund failed generations automatically; Karya doesn't publish a policy. The bigger cost is renders that succeed technically but look wrong — those burn credits everywhere. Budget 2–3 generations per usable clip when pricing any project.

What's the cheapest way to make one AI music video without a subscription?+

Buy a one-time month of a cheap plan instead of metered clips. One Seedance Basic or Max month covers a full video burst at $0.47–$0.66 per render with a commercial license, and you can cancel immediately after — unused credits stay live until the billing period ends. Pure PAYG only wins if a budget engine like LTX 2.3 Fast or Wan meets your quality bar.
