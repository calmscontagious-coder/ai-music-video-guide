Sora 2 Alternatives for Music Videos After the Shutdown (2026)

By the AI Music Video Guide team · Updated October 21, 2026 · 12 min read

This post contains affiliate links — if you sign up through them we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you (disclosure). Researched and edited for accuracy with AI assistance.

Quick answer: Sora 2 is gone. The app closed April 26, 2026; the API ended September 24, 2026. For music videos, Veo 3.1 is the closest match for native audio, Kling 4.0 wins on lip-sync and 30-second takes, Wan 3.0 is the cheapest 30-second clip with sound, and a Seedance bundle from $9.90 a month billed annually covers several engines at once.

If you built your music video pipeline on Sora 2, both deadlines have now passed. The question is no longer when to move. It is which sora 2 alternative for music videos replaces the specific thing Sora did for you, at a price you can keep paying per release. This is the migration map by job, with each price dated to its source.

What happened to Sora 2, and is any of it still running?

Nothing is still running. OpenAI closed the Sora web and mobile apps on April 26, 2026, then switched off the Sora API, including the sora-2 and sora-2-pro endpoints, on September 24, 2026 (OpenAI Help Center, checked October 21, 2026). Any third-party app that routed to Sora 2 lost the engine the same day.

Two housekeeping points before you move on. First, OpenAI's help page still points to an export tool for clips you made in Sora, and says it will email users if a final export window opens. Pull any old renders you want now. Second, prepaid ChatGPT or Sora credits can still be spent on Codex, so the money is not lost even though the video model is.

One warning for anyone shopping bundles. The multi-engine platform we use still listed "Sora 2" and "Sora 2 Pro" on its homepage on October 21, 2026. Those are legacy entries; the model cannot run without OpenAI's API, so check the live model picker before you pay.

Which Sora 2 strength are you actually replacing?

Sora 2 earned its place in music video work for four reasons: synced native audio generated with the picture, believable physics, accurate lip-sync, and the Cameo feature that put your own face and voice into a scene. A good migration matches each of those to a surviving engine instead of hunting for one "Sora killer."

Cameo has no clean replacement. Kling 4.0 accepts a voice reference and up to 10 reference images, and Seedance 2.5 takes up to 30 images, so you can rebuild a consistent likeness. Neither is the one-click "put me in the scene" button Sora shipped. Budget an afternoon of reference wrangling for that job.

Is Veo 3.1 still the closest match to Sora 2?

Yes, for the audio-plus-realism combination that made Sora useful to musicians. Veo 3.1 generates dialogue, sound effects and ambient beds natively, in the same pass as the picture (Google DeepMind). Its Standard tier is the priciest per-second rate among the Gemini, Kling and Alibaba APIs; at 720p, Fast and Lite cost a quarter or less of that.

On the Gemini API, Veo 3.1 Standard with audio costs $0.40 per second at 720p or 1080p and $0.60 per second at 4K. Veo 3.1 Fast is $0.10 per second at 720p, $0.12 at 1080p and $0.30 at 4K. Veo 3.1 Lite is $0.05 at 720p and $0.08 at 1080p with no 4K option (Google Gemini API pricing, checked October 21, 2026). A 10-second hero shot at Standard quality is $4.00 before you count the takes you throw away.

The subscription route is Google Flow. Google's plan page lists 1,000 Flow credits a month on Google AI Pro, 10,000 on Ultra 5x and 25,000 on Ultra 20x (Google One, checked October 21, 2026). Google's page does not print dollar prices in every region; a tracker we checked listed AI Pro at $19.99 a month and Ultra at $249.99 as of August 2, 2026, with a Veo 3.1 Quality clip costing 100 credits (CostGoat).

That works out to roughly ten full-quality clips a month on Pro, which is one music video's worth of hero shots, not a whole edit. We ran a full head-to-head in Sora 2 vs Veo 3 vs Seedance if you want the shot-by-shot results.

What does Kling 4.0 change for music videos?

Kling 4.0 became available to everyone on October 15, 2026, and it doubles the single-pass length from 15 to 30 seconds while adding two-channel stereo audio and better lip-sync (Kling AI release notes). For one unbroken performance shot where the model sings or speaks a line it generates, it is built for exactly that job. It will not mime to your finished vocal: its audio input is a voice reference only, so lip-syncing to your own track still needs a separate step.

The spec sheet matters here. Kling 4.0 takes up to 10 keyframe images, so you can pin the shot you want at 0, 6, 12 and 20 seconds and let the model travel between them. It accepts up to 15 reference assets in one generation, including 5 videos with a 30-second combined limit and a voice reference.

Output is 720p, 1080p or 4K, with 21:9 framing for a widescreen look, and speech comes out in nine languages, English, Chinese and Hindi among them, with regional accents (Kling AI, September 30, 2026). A lighter Kling 4.0 Flash variant tops out at 720p and 20 seconds for fast drafts.

Two honest limits. Kling has not published how many credits a Kling 4.0 clip burns, and its API was still marked "coming soon" on October 19, 2026 (The Decoder, October 19, 2026). The Decoder's side-by-side also found Kling 4.0 holds its own against Wan 3.0 and Seedance 2.5 without pulling ahead on shot logic or natural-sounding dialogue.

For budgeting, Kling's plans showed Standard at $8.80 a month for 660 credits and Pro at $32.56 for 3,000 credits after a discounted first month, and the Kling 3.0 API bills $0.126 per second at 720p with audio and $0.168 at 1080p (Wireflow, October 19, 2026). Run one short test and note the credits before you plan a batch. Our Kling vs Seedance for music videos test covers where each one breaks.

Is Wan 3.0 the cheap 30-second option?

Yes. Alibaba's Wan 3.0 generates clips up to 30 seconds at 30 fps with audio switched on by default, and the Standard tier costs $0.05 per second at 480p, $0.10 at 720p and $0.20 at 1080p (The Decoder, August 24, 2026). A full 30-second 1080p clip with sound lands at $6.00, the same money that buys 15 seconds of Veo 3.1 Standard.

The Decoder noted the Standard rate carried a 30 percent discount at the time, so confirm it before you budget. The faster Prime tier runs $0.068, $0.14 and $0.28 per second at the same three resolutions, and fal's standard playground rates matched those numbers on September 30, 2026 (fal). Wan 3.0 also takes a mixed bag of references in one request: up to 10 images, 5 videos and 5 audio clips, plus PDFs and web pages if you want it to storyboard from a document. For a lyric video built from your cover art and a reference reel, that is a real workflow.

The caveats are real too. Alibaba's own API reference still labels Wan 3.0 as "in preview" as of October 11, 2026, and the first-frame and first-and-last-frame modes cannot accept audio at the same time; audio references only work in reference-to-video mode and cap at 15 seconds combined (Alibaba Cloud Model Studio). Standard-tier renders also take several minutes, and the docs recommend polling every 15 seconds. If you need Wan 3.0 without an Alibaba Cloud account, Runway added it to tool mode and workflows on August 24, 2026, and the Seedance bundle lists it as an engine.

Where does Seedance fit for someone leaving Sora?

Seedance is the engine family that now sits closest to what Sora 2 promised: picture and a stereo mix in one forward pass, with lip-synced dialogue, ambience and score. Seedance 2.5 shipped on July 31, 2026 with 30-second single-pass generation, multi-round extensions, and up to 30 images, 10 video clips and 10 audio clips as references (ByteDance Seed). It also added timestamp-level editing of audio and video, which is the feature that saves you a re-render when one bar is wrong.

For the "sync visuals to my finished master" job, Seedance's audio upload does the heavy lifting. Tag an MP3 in the prompt and the model beat-locks cuts and camera moves to it, or leaves the audio alone and composes a bed when you do not supply one. Reference audio on Seedance 2.5 there caps at 30 seconds combined, so for a whole song the bundle points you to its separate Music to Video tool, which takes a track up to 10 minutes at 2 credits a second at 480p or 4 at 720p (vendor figures, checked October 21, 2026; we have not tested it yet).

On cost, be precise. The Seedance bundle we use grants one free credit every 24 hours after sign-in, and its terms tie commercial rights to paid plans. The bundle is Seeddance, an independent multi-model platform; its own footer says it is not affiliated with any third-party AI model vendor, ByteDance included.

Paid plans, billed annually, start at Basic for $9.90 a month with 150 credits (about 15 video renders), then Pro at $19.90 for 350 credits, Max at $34.90 for 750 and Pro Max at $59.90 for 1,500. Every paid tier includes watermark-free MP4 and PNG exports and a commercial licence covering outputs (checked October 21, 2026).

Two honest notes. Seedance 2 there renders 4 to 15 seconds per clip; Seedance 2.5 reaches 30 seconds but bills 12 credits a second at 480p, 23 at 720p and 48 at 1080p, so Basic's 150 monthly credits buy about 6 seconds of 720p, and one 30-second 720p take (690 credits) fits a single month's allowance only from the $34.90 Max plan up.

The homepage also still lists Sora 2 even though the model is dead. Basic's 15 or so renders are Seedance 2 renders: enough for one careful video, not a content calendar.

What about Runway, and the "sync to my finished song" job?

Runway is a strong editor and a weak Sora replacement, for one reason: its own Gen-4.5 model renders picture, and sound still arrives as separate tracks from its SFX, voice and music tools rather than inside the video pass (Runway changelog, October 15, 2026). If your workflow is "generate a performance, then score it in a DAW," that is fine. If you wanted the model to hear the scene, it is a step backward.

Pricing is credit-based: Gen-4.5 costs 12 credits per second, and the Standard plan at $12 a month billed annually gives 625 credits, about 52 seconds of Gen-4.5. Pro is $28 for 2,250 credits and Max is $76 for 9,500 (Runway pricing, checked October 21, 2026). Runway's real value for musicians is that it now hosts Seedance 2.5, Wan 3.0 and Kling 3.0 inside one timeline, with ProRes export, so you can generate elsewhere and finish here.

If you have a mastered track and only need cuts locked to it, a dedicated beat-sync tool is still the shortest path. General video models are better at generating sound than at obeying an existing master. We keep an updated test of those tools in beat-sync AI video editing.

Which Sora 2 alternative for music videos should you pick?

Pick by job: Veo 3.1 for model-generated sound and realism, Kling 4.0 for long performance takes with generated lip-sync, Wan 3.0 for the cheapest 30 seconds with sound. A Seedance plan from $9.90 a month is the hedge if you want several engines on one bill, as long as you budget Seedance 2.5's per-second credit cost. Its audio upload is the closest surviving thing to a Sora workflow.

Whatever you choose, run the same 10-second chorus through two engines before you commit a month of credits. Prices on this page are dated for a reason: three of these models changed price or version between July and October 2026, and the next round will not announce itself.
