How to Sell AI Music Video Services and Actually Get Paid

By the AI Music Video Guide team · Updated November 3, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer: Sell AI music video services by pricing against what buyers already see. Fiverr listings for AI music videos typically ran from $43 to $162, averaging $93 (Fiverr, read October 25, 2026). Charge for deliverables and revisions, confirm the client holds rights to the song, and disclose AI use.

Selling AI music videos is a service business first and a technology story second. The tool makes the clips, but clients pay for a finished video, a clear scope and clean rights to the song. This guide covers what the public market pays, how we'd structure a quote, what to check on rights, and whether the videos can earn money on their own. Every figure comes from a source we read on October 25, 2026, and where our notes were thin, we say so.

What do clients pay for an AI music video?

On Fiverr, AI generated music videos typically cost $43 to $162, with an average of $93 (Fiverr, read October 25, 2026). That is a public reference point, not a rule. Fiverr says price moves with seller experience, video complexity and extras like editing or revisions, so your own rate should move with them too.

What the Fiverr range does and doesn't tell you

The $43 to $162 spread is wide because the work varies. Fiverr's category page ties price to seller experience, video complexity and deliverables such as editing and revisions (Fiverr, read October 25, 2026). We'd expect a simple visual for one song to sit toward the low end and a complex, heavily edited video with revisions to sit higher, though the page doesn't break prices down that way.

We wouldn't copy the $93 average as your price. It blends beginners and veterans, and it knows nothing about your tool costs or your hours. Use it as a sanity check instead. A quote far above the range needs a visible reason, such as more complex visuals or a larger set of deliverables. A quote under the bottom of the range probably means you are underpricing the revision work.

How should you structure what you charge?

Charge by deliverable, not by effort. Fiverr's category page lists experience, complexity and add-ons like editing or revisions as price drivers (Fiverr, read October 25, 2026), so we'd build tiers around those: a base video, a more complex version, and paid revisions. Write the revision limit into the quote before work starts.

A three-tier structure that matches the price drivers

  • Base: one video built from the client's song, with a fixed number of revision rounds written into the quote.
  • Extended: more complex visuals or added editing. Fiverr names complexity and editing as price factors, so this is where the higher number belongs.
  • Add-ons: extra revisions, alternate cuts or additional deliverables, each priced separately so scope creep has a price.

Tiers also make the sales conversation easier. Instead of defending one number, you let the client choose how much complexity they want to pay for. If you sell AI music video services full time, this is also how you protect your margin when a client asks for a fifth round of changes.

Cost your tools before you quote

Your price has to cover generation, failed attempts and your own time. Our notes carry no published generator rates, so we won't state any here. Check current pricing and the commercial terms yourself before you publish a price list.

That is a creator's claim, so confirm it on the provider's own page. Our piece on Suno plans for commercial use covers the plan question in more detail.

What rights do you need before you take a paid job?

You need the right to use the music, and the platform's rules matter too. Dynamoi lists proper rights to music and original, non-impersonating content as YouTube requirements (Dynamoi, read October 25, 2026). If the song came from Suno, one tutorial says commercial use requires a paid subscription (YouTube, read October 25, 2026).

The risks are not abstract. Songbay, a service that helps people sell and protect AI songs, lists the problems AI-assisted tracks can run into (Songbay, read October 25, 2026):

  • Content ID disputes on YouTube, TikTok or Spotify
  • Music distributor rejections due to similarity claims
  • Sync and licensing clearance requests
  • Protecting human-written lyrics used in AI songs
  • Platform monetisation disputes
  • Future changes in AI copyright law

Songbay sells services in this area, so read that list as a prompt for questions, not as proof of how often each problem happens. It is still a useful checklist for a client intake form.

What we'd put in the contract

  • The client confirms they own or have licensed the song, including any AI-generated parts and the plan used to make them.
  • AI use is disclosed, and the video does not impersonate a real person or artist.
  • Ownership of the finished video is stated in plain words. Our article on who owns an AI music video lays out the questions to ask.
  • The number of revision rounds, the delivery formats and the payment schedule are listed.

If the job involves a cover song, read whether AI cover songs are legal first. None of this is legal advice, and a client with a complicated rights situation should talk to a lawyer before you start.

Can you also monetize the videos you make?

Yes, on YouTube, if the videos meet its policies. Soundraw says YouTube allows AI music videos to earn money when they follow monetization guidelines, with AI use disclosed and original content (Soundraw, read October 25, 2026). Dynamoi puts the Partner Program threshold at 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours (Dynamoi, read October 25, 2026).

Ad revenue is slow money compared with a client fee. Dynamoi says music CPMs typically run $1 to $5 per 1,000 views (Dynamoi, read October 25, 2026), and that is before you reach the subscriber and watch-hour thresholds. For most people selling videos to clients, ad income is a bonus on a channel they own, not a replacement for invoices.

The same Dynamoi page helps explain why many music clients have tight budgets. It puts Spotify at $0.004 per stream, so 25,000 streams for $100 a month, and Apple Music at $0.008 per stream, or 12,500 streams for the same $100 (Dynamoi, read October 25, 2026). It also lists Bandcamp at $8.20 per album sold at $10. An independent artist earning at those rates may well compare your quote to the Fiverr range we covered above.

Soundraw lists offering music creation services as one way to earn from AI music, alongside monetizing on YouTube (Soundraw, read October 25, 2026). Video services sit naturally next to that. If you want the YouTube side in depth, our guide to monetizing AI music videos on YouTube goes through the steps.

One more point for client work: agree in writing who owns the channel and its revenue. If you deliver a video to a client's channel, the ad income is theirs unless your contract says otherwise.

Where should you list the service and how do you deliver it?

Fiverr already lists AI generated music videos as its own category (Fiverr, read October 25, 2026), so you are not inventing a market. You can list there or sell direct. Either way, deliver a short sample, a written scope and the rights paperwork, because those separate a professional from someone who just pressed a button.

A marketplace gives you buyers but also gives you direct price comparison against every other seller, which is why the range above matters. Selling direct gives you control over pricing and the client relationship, but you carry the work of finding people. We'd start on a marketplace to learn what buyers ask for, then move repeat clients to direct work.

A delivery checklist

  • Send a short sample or a storyboard before full production. Our guide to storyboarding an AI music video shows one way to do it.
  • Get written confirmation of the song's rights before you generate anything for the final cut.
  • State the revision count and what counts as a new request rather than a revision.
  • Agree the file formats and platforms the video is meant for.
  • Keep your prompts, settings and tool receipts, so you can answer questions if a platform flags the video later.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I charge for an AI music video?+

Start from the public range. Fiverr's AI generated music video category typically runs from $43 to $162, averaging $93 (Fiverr, read October 25, 2026). Then adjust for your experience, how complex the video is, and extras like editing and revisions. Test your quote against your own tool costs and time before publishing a price.

Do I need a paid plan to sell videos made with Suno songs?+

One tutorial we read says you need a Suno subscription to sell or commercially use the music, and that the lowest tier is $10 a month (YouTube, read October 25, 2026). That is a creator's claim, not Suno's terms. Check the provider's current plan page before you accept a paid job.

Can I earn ad money from the videos I make for clients?+

Only if the channel owner qualifies. YouTube monetization needs Partner Program eligibility, which Dynamoi lists as 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours, plus original content and proper music rights (Dynamoi, read October 25, 2026). Agree in writing who owns the channel revenue before you start.

Should I disclose that the video is AI generated?+

We would. Soundraw advises disclosing AI use on YouTube and publishing original, high-value content (Soundraw, read October 25, 2026). Telling clients up front can also head off disputes later, and Songbay lists platform monetisation disputes among the problems AI songs can hit (Songbay, read October 25, 2026).
