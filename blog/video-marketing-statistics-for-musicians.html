Video Marketing Statistics for Musicians: 2026 Figures With Sources

By the AI Music Video Guide team · Updated November 2, 2026 · 7 min read

This post contains affiliate links — if you sign up through them we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you (disclosure). Researched and edited for accuracy with AI assistance.

Quick answer: The 2026 figures worth a musician's attention are Wyzowl's 82% of video marketers reporting good ROI (down from 93%), its 51% naming 30 to 60 seconds the best length, Statista's 48.2% of internet users watching music videos weekly, and Wistia's finding that almost 40% of companies spent under $5,000 on video in a year. None of these surveys is music-specific.

Marketing statistics get copied from blog to blog until the year, the sample and the wording fall off. We went back to the publishing pages, pulled the figures an independent artist can act on, and kept each tied to its source, year and sample. Two cautions apply throughout: these are general surveys of businesses and internet users, not of musicians or fans, and several come from vendors that sell video production, hosting or websites.

Which 2026 video marketing statistics can a musician trust?

Trust the figures that name a sample and a year. Wyzowl's State of Video Marketing 2026 surveyed 266 marketers and consumers in late 2025, which is small but documented; Wistia polled more than 900 professionals; Statista's audience data runs to Q4 2024. Aggregator pages that repeat these numbers often still carry the previous year's figure.

Digital Applied, a marketing agency that re-read 108 figures at their publishers' pages in October 2026, puts it plainly: "many of the video statistics in circulation are old, misdated or impossible to trace" (Digital Applied, updated October 21, 2026). Its checks table traces the often-quoted "93% of marketers say video gives a positive ROI" to Wyzowl's 2025 edition. The 2026 edition says 82%.

Vendor status matters too. Wyzowl produces explainer videos, Wistia hosts video, Wix sells websites, Loopex Digital and Digital Applied are agencies, Market.us sells reports and Cramer is a production company. None of them will ask whether you need video at all.

What do Wyzowl's 2026 numbers say about video ROI?

Wyzowl's 2026 edition reports that 91% of businesses use video for marketing, 82% of video marketers say it gives a good ROI, and 92% plan to spend the same or more on video in 2026. The ROI figure fell 11 points from 93% in the 2025 edition, its lowest reading since 2018 (Wyzowl, 2026 edition, read October 25, 2026).

The same survey shows how marketers judge success. Views are the most common measure (67%), then engagement (63%) and leads and clicks (52%); only 32% track bottom-line sales. For a musician the equivalent trap is counting views on a video and never checking saves, follows or ticket sales.

On cost, respondents split three ways: 38% say video has become more expensive, 32% see no change and 30% say it is cheaper. Among businesses that do not use video, "too expensive" and "don't feel it's needed" tie as the top reason at 24% each.

Do people actually watch music videos online?

Yes, more than any other kind of video. Statista data cited by Wix found 48.2% of internet users worldwide watched music videos online every week in Q4 2024, more than 13 points ahead of comedy and meme clips at 35%. DataReportal's Digital 2026 report puts online video reach at 94.6% of online adults over the past 30 days (Wix, read October 25, 2026).

That is the one statistic here that speaks directly to musicians, and it measures audience behavior rather than marketer opinion. It cuts both ways: the category is crowded because it is popular. Live streams (27.7%) and tutorials (25.8%) trail far behind in the same table.

In Wyzowl's 2026 survey YouTube is both the most used channel (82%) and the one most often rated effective (69%); TikTok is used by 40% and rated effective by 29%. Those are business marketers rating their own campaigns, not a measure of music discovery.

Short-form or long-form: what do the surveys say about length?

Short wins on every survey here. In Wyzowl's 2026 edition 51% of video marketers say 30 to 60 seconds works best, 71% pick somewhere between 30 seconds and two minutes, and only 4% choose three minutes or more. HubSpot's 2026 State of Marketing has 48.6% naming short-form the format with the biggest return against 28.6% for long-form, as reported by Digital Applied.

Wistia's 2026 report, cited by Wix, finds videos under a minute average a 52% engagement rate, meaning viewers watch about half the runtime. Loopex Digital repeats a claim that short-form earns 2.5 times the engagement of long-form, but it credits a secondary blog rather than a study, so we leave that multiplier out of the table (Loopex Digital, updated May 13, 2026).

A three-minute music video sits outside the length every marketer survey rates best. The full video is the asset you cut into the 30 to 60 second pieces these surveys reward, which is the method in our guide to turning one song into twenty videos.

How much do marketers spend per video, and what does that imply for a music video?

Less than production-company blogs suggest. Wistia's 2026 survey found almost 40% of companies spent under $5,000 on video production in the previous year, and a Statista study cited by Market.us has 25% of marketers spending $1,000 to $5,000 on an average video, with 4% above $20,000. Both describe business budgets, not music video quotes.

Market.us also states that 42% of video marketers spend $0 to $500 on an average video and that companies allocate between $100 and $1,000 for production, but the page gives no survey name, year or sample for either figure, so treat both as unverified (Market.us, undated, read October 25, 2026). Wyzowl's 46% of video marketers putting a third or less of their marketing budget into video is better documented.

Cost perception is where sources disagree. Wyzowl's 2026 edition reports 38% rising, 32% unchanged and 30% falling. Loopex Digital and Market.us both quote 49% rising, 29% falling and 21% unchanged, an older set that Loopex prints beside the 2026 numbers on the same page. We use the 2026 edition.

Cramer, a corporate production company, publishes no dollar figures. Its budgeting guide lists drivers instead: crew day rates, equipment and location, shoot days, editing and color, music licensing, talent fees and rush timelines, with a simple shoot wrapping in two to three weeks including editing and cinematic projects taking six to eight weeks or longer (Cramer, undated, read October 25, 2026). Where does AI video fit in these statistics?

AI is the fastest-moving number in the set, not the cheapest. Wyzowl's 2026 edition finds 63% of video marketers have used AI to create or edit video, up from 51% a year earlier, the largest single-year rise of any measure in its report. Yet more of those same marketers say video got more expensive (38%) than cheaper (30%).

Digital Applied checked the widely shared claim that AI cut production costs by 40%, from $4,200 to $2,500 a minute, and found no source for it. AI changes who can afford a video, not what a crew charges, and a generated video is a different product: it cannot film you performing.

What should an independent musician do with these numbers?

Budget for short clips first, one long cut second, and measure something other than views. The surveys reward 30 to 60 second video, show marketers spending modest sums per video, and show ROI confidence slipping where teams count views alone. A release plan built on that evidence looks different from one built on a production company's price list.

Frequently asked questions

Are these video marketing statistics about musicians?+

No. Every survey here polls businesses or general internet users, not artists or fans. Wyzowl's 2026 edition surveyed 266 marketers and consumers, Wistia polled more than 900 professionals, and Statista's weekly viewing data covers internet users worldwide. The one music-specific figure is Statista's 48.2% weekly music video audience. Read the rest as context for budgeting, not as proof about music.

Why do some pages say 93% of marketers get good ROI and others say 82%?+

Both numbers come from Wyzowl, from different editions. The 2025 edition reported 93%, an all-time high for that series; the 2026 edition, built on late-2025 fieldwork, reports 82%. Aggregator pages such as Loopex Digital's report updated in May 2026 still carry the older figure. When a statistic has no year attached, assume it is a previous edition until you check the publisher's page.

What video length do marketers rate best in 2026?+

Thirty to sixty seconds, chosen by 51% of video marketers in Wyzowl's 2026 survey, with 71% picking somewhere between 30 seconds and two minutes. Only 4% rate three minutes or more as most effective. That favors teasers and clips cut from a music video as the asset you push on social platforms, with the full cut living on YouTube.

How much do most companies spend on video production in a year?+

Wistia's 2026 State of Video survey, cited by Wix and Digital Applied, found almost 40% of companies spent under $5,000 producing video in the previous year and just over 30% spent more; the rest did not track it separately. A separate Statista study cited by Market.us has 25% of marketers spending $1,000 to $5,000 on an average video. Neither figure is specific to music videos.

Has AI made video cheaper, according to the surveys?+

Not for most marketing teams. In Wyzowl's 2026 edition 63% of video marketers have used AI tools, up from 51%, yet 38% say video has become more expensive against 30% who say cheaper. Digital Applied could find no source for the claim that AI cut costs 40% per minute. AI lowers the entry price for a musician working alone; it has not cut what crews charge.
