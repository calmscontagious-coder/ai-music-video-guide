Is CapCut a good fit for a small business?

For short videos made in-house, yes. The free plan lists basic editing tools and free templates. The phone app keeps the whole job on the device you already film with. And when more people get involved, paid plans add shared storage and a brand kit.

It suits the business that wants to show real work: the plumber's finished bathroom, the bakery's morning trays, the salon's before and after, the landscaper's Friday walk-through. It is not a strategy by itself. A video still needs a reason for someone nearby to call, book or visit.

What should the first four weeks look like?

1Set up and learn the editorOne account, one test clip exported
2Video one: who you are and wherePosted, with captions
3Video two: one job or one productPosted, with a clear next step

Week 1. Install the app and create one account for the business. Decide now which sign-in method you will use and stick to it: CapCut's help centre warns that each login method creates a separate account, which becomes a problem the day you pay for a plan. Then cut one throwaway clip to learn trim, text and export.

Weeks 2 to 4. One video a week is enough to start. Film on the phone, in daylight, holding the phone upright. Say the business name and the town in the first sentence. End each video with one next step: call, book online, or visit, with the detail on screen.

How do I keep the videos safe to use as ads?

By watching what you take from CapCut's library. Its Materials License Agreement allows promotional use only for items marked for commercial use, and a single unmarked sticker, font or effect turns the whole video into personal-use only.

Three habits cover most of it:

  • Start from CapCut's templates for commercial use, or from a blank project with your own footage.
  • Use your own audio: your voice, the sound of the shop, or music you hold the rights to. CapCut's general music library is for personal videos.
  • If you work in health, pharmacy or supplements, keep library items out of promotional videos altogether. The agreement bars its materials from ads in those fields.

The full rules are in our guide to CapCut commercial use.

Should I add captions and a voiceover?

Captions, yes, on anything with speech. They carry the message for anyone watching with the sound off. Generate them, then correct the spelling of your business name, street and services by hand. Our auto captions guide has the steps and the fixes.

A voiceover is optional. The owner's own voice is usually the better choice for a local business, because customers will hear the same voice when they call. If nobody wants to be recorded, CapCut's text to speech can read a script instead.

What export settings should I use?

For vertical video, CapCut's Reels guide recommends 1080 x 1920 at 30 fps. In the phone app, tap the resolution label beside Export and choose 1080P. Watch the exported file once outside the app before you post it, and delete any branded ending clip a template may have added.

More detail is in CapCut export settings and exporting without a watermark.

When does a business need Pro or Teams?

Not in month one, in most cases. Pay when a specific limit is costing you time.

  • Pro makes sense when you rely on premium templates, edit on a computer, or want the storage. CapCut's pricing page lists 1 TB of storage and 1,200 monthly AI credits with Pro, and its help centre says Pro works on phone, desktop and web.
  • Teams makes sense when a third person joins. A new shared space is free for 2 people; beyond that CapCut asks for Teams, which gives every member Pro features, 1,000 GB of shared space, brand templates and a brand kit.

You can compare CapCut plans when you get there, and our plans and prices guide explains why the price differs by device.

What if there is no time to do this?

Then be honest about it early. Three videos that never get posted help nobody. Either block one hour a week for the plan above, or hand the online side to someone whose job it is.

Frequently asked questions

Is CapCut free for small business use?

The editor has a free plan that covers basic editing tools and free templates, and a business may use it. What matters for business use is the licence on library items: only assets marked for commercial use may appear in promotional videos. Your own footage and your own audio avoid the question.

What videos should a small business make first?

Start with three: who you are and where you are, one finished job or one product, and an answer to a question customers ask often. One a week is a realistic pace. Say the business name and town early, add captions, and end with a single next step such as call, book or visit.

Can a small business use CapCut music in its videos?

Not the general music library, which CapCut licenses for personal, non-commercial videos. Its Commercial Sounds library is for business use, but only for videos shown within 3 places: CapCut, TikTok and TikTok for Business. Elsewhere, use your own voice, natural sound, or music you hold the rights to.

Does a small business need CapCut Pro?

Usually not at the start. The free plan is enough to cut, caption and export a short vertical video. Pro becomes worth paying for when you depend on premium templates, edit on a computer, or need the 1 TB of storage. Teams is for three or more people sharing one workspace.

What is CapCut Teams?

Teams is CapCut's plan for groups, priced per seat. CapCut says each member gets Pro features, and the group gets 1,000 GB of shared space, brand templates and a brand kit for colours, logos and fonts. A new shared space is free for 2 people; adding more requires Teams.
