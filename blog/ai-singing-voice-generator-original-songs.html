AI Singing Voice Generators for Original Songs: Vocals, Licensing, Cost

By the AI Music Video GuideUpdated November 10, 202610 min read

Quick answer: For sung vocals on a song you wrote, choose between a full-song generator with built-in vocals, a voice conversion tool sold by the minute, or a voice cloner whose guide does not mention singing. Licensing is tiered at ElevenLabs and Suno, Kits AI states no terms, and realistic AI music that is a YouTube video's main focus must be disclosed.

The phrase "AI singing voice generator" covers at least three different products. We read the pricing and help pages for ElevenLabs, Suno and Kits AI on November 2, 2026, plus YouTube's disclosure rules, to sort out which type fits a songwriter, what each tier lets you do with the result, and what the pages leave unsaid.

Which type of tool actually sings your original song?

Full-song generators sing. Eleven Music and Suno produce a finished track with vocals from a prompt, and Suno's pricing page also lists creating with your own voice and adding vocals. Kits AI sells voice conversion minutes and lists generative vocals. Voice cloning is the third type, and the ElevenLabs cloning guide does not mention singing.

Full-song generators with built-in vocals

Eleven Music is the full-song option from ElevenLabs. Its product page says you can "Generate songs with a consistent voice using a Vocal from your tracks or our curated Vocal Library", and describes Music v2 as offering "Better vocals, instrumentation, and arrangement across every genre" and "Long-form composition" (Eleven Music page, read November 2, 2026).

The same page carries an "Introducing Music v2.5" notice, so treat version names as current to that page date only. For a songwriter, the consistent-voice feature is the part that matters: one vocal identity across several songs instead of a new singer each time. Voice conversion sold by the minute

Kits AI sells voice conversion minutes, not songs. Its pricing page counts "conversion minutes per month" and "download minutes per month" separately, includes a voice designer, a voice blender and "Kits generative vocals" even on the free tier, and lists a Choir tool from Starter (Kits AI pricing page, read November 2, 2026). The page does not describe the workflow, so check its help pages before assuming you can feed it a sung take.

Voice cloning

Voice cloning is the third product type. The ElevenLabs voice cloning guide describes two methods and sets rules for consent, and it does not address singing. Instant Voice Cloning asks for 1 to 2 minutes of good audio and Professional Voice Cloning for 30 to 180 minutes (ElevenLabs voice cloning guide, read November 2, 2026).

The guide says you can only create a Professional Voice Clone of your own voice, and that even with their consent you cannot clone someone else's voice. It does not state the same rule for Instant Voice Cloning, so read the vendor's terms before cloning anyone. Our piece on whether AI cover songs are legal covers the wider question.

What does each licensing tier let you do with the vocals?

Commercial rights are tier-gated at ElevenLabs and Suno. Eleven Music is personal use only on Free, commercial without streaming on Starter, and commercial but not enterprise on Creator and Pro. Suno lists commercial rights from Pro upward and points to its terms of service for limits. Kits AI does not state commercial terms on its pricing page.

Licensing by plan at the generator we link

The Eleven Music page says "The tracks you generate are cleared for broad commercial use. Commercial rights vary by subscription tier." Its pricing table reads Free "Personal use only", Starter "Commercial, no streaming", and Creator and Pro "Commercial, not enterprise" (Eleven Music page, read November 2, 2026).

The FAQ adds a carve-out: "On Self-Serve plans, online and offline commercial use is permitted, except for film, TV, and Studio Games." The ElevenLabs pricing page lists "Music" under Free, and Starter is the first tier with a Commercial License and "Music commercial use" (ElevenLabs pricing page, read November 2, 2026). On the Music page itself, Free is shown as browse only.

If the consistent-voice workflow is the one you want, pick the ElevenLabs plan that matches how you will release the song. The page does not define "no streaming", but the label on Starter suggests that tier is not meant for a streaming release, so check the Music terms before choosing.

Suno licensing by plan

Suno's pricing page is blunt: Free has no song downloads and no commercial rights, while Pro and Premier both list commercial rights (Suno pricing page, read November 2, 2026). The page also points to Suno's terms of service for limitations on commercial use. We cover the release side in our guide to the Suno plan you need for commercial use. One line to notice: "Credits included in subscriptions do not carry over from day to day or month to month."

Kits AI licensing: not on the pricing page

The Kits AI pricing page says nothing about commercial terms, royalty-free or licensed artist voices, or training minutes (Kits AI pricing page, read November 2, 2026). We are not saying the terms are bad, only that we could not read them where a buyer would look. Do not assume a converted vocal is cleared for release until you find the document that says so. Do you have to disclose AI vocals on YouTube?

Yes, when the AI music is the main focus of the video. YouTube's rule covers realistic AI-generated or altered content, and its list includes "Creates music that's the main focus of the video". For AI music the label may appear in the video player itself rather than only in the expanded description (YouTube Help, read November 2, 2026).

The YouTube Help page on altered or synthetic content draws the line at realism and prominence. Disclosure is required when content "Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do" or "Creates music that's the main focus of the video", among other cases. It is not required for non-realistic AI content, for "primarily aesthetic" edits such as beauty filters, or for "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs" (YouTube Help, read November 2, 2026).

Cloning your own voice for voice-overs or dubs is on the page's exempt list. The page does not address a song sung with a cloned voice, and it links a separate help article for music creators, so check that one before publishing. You disclose by selecting "Yes" under "AI use" in YouTube Studio, which adds a label; YouTube may also apply labels itself. The page says disclosure alone does not affect audience reach or monetization eligibility. For how other services treat AI tracks, see which platforms accept AI music.

If you turn the finished song into a video, the reseller we link above is one place to generate the clips, and the disclosure rule applies to the music either way.

What do the pricing pages not tell you?

None of the three pages states a maximum track length. Suno does not say how many songs a credit buys. Kits AI omits commercial terms and annual prices. ElevenLabs leaves the "Music per month" unit unlabelled. Every one of these gaps is a question you should settle before you pay, not after.

  • Track length caps. The Eleven Music page states no maximum. The only length clues are featured tracks running from 2 minutes 18 seconds to 3 minutes 12 seconds (Eleven Music page, read November 2, 2026), and that is an observation, not a limit. The Suno pricing page gives upload limits but no maximum for a generated song; the Kits AI page gives no length figure.
  • The "Music per month" unit. The Eleven Music page lists 11, 33, 134 and 667 with no unit (Eleven Music page, read November 2, 2026). The credits-per-minute rate implies minutes; the page does not say.
  • Singing from a clone. The ElevenLabs cloning guide does not mention singing. The Eleven Music page says a consistent voice can come "from your tracks", but how that relates to a Professional Voice Clone, available "on our Creator plan or above" per the cloning guide, is not spelled out (ElevenLabs voice cloning guide, read November 2, 2026).
  • Model dates. Suno's page gives none; treat every version name as "as of November 2, 2026".

For a finished sung track with one recurring voice, ElevenLabs states the most licence detail per plan of the three pages we read. Suno's first paid tier ($8/month) costs more than ElevenLabs Starter ($6/mo) but lists commercial rights without a "no streaming" label; the cheapest ElevenLabs tier without that label is Creator at $22/mo.

Suno says less about what a credit buys. Kits AI fits if you want voice conversion by the minute, once you have found its commercial terms. Our walkthrough on how to make an AI song covers the steps all three share.

Frequently asked questions

Can an AI voice generator sing lyrics I wrote?

Yes, if it is a full-song generator with built-in vocals. A full-song tool writes and performs the track from a prompt, and one we read lets you keep a consistent voice across songs; another lists creating with your own voice. A voice conversion tool sells conversion minutes and lists generative vocals, but its pricing page does not describe the workflow. A voice cloner's guide does not mention singing.

Which plan do I need to release the song commercially?

It depends on the vendor's tier. The AI voice generator we link allows personal use only on its free plan, commercial use without streaming on its first paid tier, and commercial but not enterprise use on its next two tiers, with film, TV and Studio Games excluded on self-serve plans. The other full-song generator lists commercial rights from its first paid tier, subject to its terms of service.

Do I have to tell YouTube the vocals are AI?

If the AI music is the main focus of the video, yes. YouTube's help page lists that case among the content creators must disclose, and for AI music the label may appear in the video player. You disclose by choosing "Yes" under "AI use" in YouTube Studio. The page says disclosure alone does not affect reach or monetization eligibility (YouTube Help, read November 2, 2026).

Can I clone a famous singer's voice for my song?

The voice cloning guide we read says you can only create a professional voice clone of your own voice, and that even with consent you cannot clone someone else's. It does not state that rule for instant cloning, so read the vendor's terms before cloning anyone, and the guide does not mention singing. If you want a recognisable style, use a generated vocal library voice or your own performance.

How long can the generated songs be?

None of the three pricing pages we read states a maximum track length. The one clue we found was featured tracks running from 2 minutes 18 seconds to 3 minutes 12 seconds on the full-song generator's page, read November 2, 2026, which is an observation rather than a limit. Test a long song on a free tier before paying for a plan on that basis.

Do unused credits roll over to the next month?

Not at the full-song generator whose pricing page addresses it. Its pricing page states that credits included in subscriptions do not carry over from day to day or month to month, and its free tier allots credits per day rather than per month. The other pages we read do not state a carry-over policy, so assume a quiet month is a lost month unless the vendor says otherwise.
