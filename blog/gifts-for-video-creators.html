Short answer: for someone who films on a phone, the gift they will use every week is a wireless clip-on mic such as the DJI Mic Mini, which TechRadar calls the tiny wireless mic to beat for phone-first creators. Add a fast memory card or a rugged SSD for a camera shooter. For an editor, a calibrated 4K monitor is the big upgrade.

How to pick for the creator you know

  • Phone or camera? Phone shooters get the most from a mic, a gimbal and a light; camera shooters from cards and a fast drive.
  • Check the card slot. Drones and action cameras take microSD; most mirrorless cameras take full-size SD.
  • Check the ports on their computer before buying a drive or monitor: USB-C, Thunderbolt or HDMI.
  • Check the delivery date on the product page before you order for a holiday date.

Solid gifts

The upgrades viewers notice first: clean sound, steady footage and fast storage.

Clean voice on a phone or camera

DJI Mic Mini (2 mics, 1 receiver, charging case)

A tiny wireless clip-on mic. TechRadar gave it an Editor's Choice for being tiny, reliable and usable with phones and cameras, and Digital Camera World called it an instant class leader. The complaints: the receiver sticks out on a phone, there is no easy way to monitor levels, and there is no backup safety track (TechRadar; Digital Camera World). DJI rates the transmitter at about 11.5 hours with noise cancelling off (DJI specs).

Skip it if: they need a backup recording on every take; DJI's bigger Mic 2 has one.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best gift for someone who films on a phone?

A wireless clip-on mic such as the DJI Mic Mini. Reviewers single it out for phone-first creators, and it works with cameras too.

Which memory card should I gift a video creator?

Check the slot first. Drones and action cameras take microSD, where the SanDisk Extreme is a common pick; most mirrorless cameras take full-size SD, where a V90 card suits high-bitrate 4K and 8K.

Is a portable SSD a good gift for a video editor?

Yes, if it matches their ports. A 10Gbps drive such as the Samsung T7 Shield runs at full speed on any Mac or PC; editors on USB4 or Thunderbolt machines can use a faster drive.
