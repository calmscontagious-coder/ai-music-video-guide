What does the Seedance content policy actually block?

The content policy at seedance.ai, read October 25, 2026, names four zero-tolerance behaviors: content that violates laws or regulations, infringes the rights of others, endangers public safety, or is fraudulent or misleading. It then lists five prohibited categories, and it applies to uploads, prompts, generated clips, shared clips and interactions with other users.

The five categories are worth knowing by name, because a blocked render rarely tells you which one it hit. "Dangerous or Illegal Activities" covers content that violates laws or IP rights. "Security Threats" includes "bypassing of security filters", so the filter-dodging tricks that circulate on video sites are a violation. "Harmful Content" covers hate speech, harassment, extremism, terrorism and self-harm.

"False Information" bans fraud, "deceptive impersonation" and "concealing AI origin to deceive". "Sensitive Material" carries zero tolerance for child sexual abuse material, non-consensual intimate images and explicit sexual content.

Section 5 matters for artists. Exceptions "may be made for educational, documentary, scientific research, or artistic expression purposes where the public benefit clearly outweighs potential harm", confirmed by email. One caution: the page calls itself "Seedance AI Community", names no company, and its menu lists Kling, Google Veo, Wan and other third-party models. Treat it as one operator's rules, not ByteDance's.

Will Seedance block real people's faces and celebrity names?

Expect a celebrity name or a famous character in a prompt to fail. GlobalGPT's guide, last updated April 7, 2026, says Seedance 2.0 introduced safety updates in February 2026 that block real human faces and copyrighted characters, and that a detected celebrity name or famous movie character makes the task fail. That is a reseller's description, not ByteDance's own text.

CNBC reported on February 16, 2026 that viral Seedance 2.0 videos appeared to show "copyrighted characters and celebrity likenesses", and a ByteDance spokesperson told CNBC: "We are taking steps to strengthen current safeguards as we work to prevent the unauthorized use of intellectual property and likeness by users." Likeness is named alongside IP, so faces are a target of the filters.

GlobalGPT's three prompt rules are the practical version. Avoid real names. Describe features instead, its example being "a tall man with short brown hair wearing an action suit". Skip copyrighted IP, asking for "a hero in a dark bat-themed armor" rather than the character by name. Weigh the source: the guide exists to sell GlobalGPT's own subscription and opens by pitching Seedance Lite, which it describes as producing 5 seconds of 1080p video, so treat it as a reseller's reading of the filters.

For a band this cuts two ways. Your own face is legally yours, and the seedance.ai policy only requires that you "have the necessary rights to all materials you upload". The filter cannot tell your face from a stranger's, so a real band photo can be refused on sight. Our guide to making a music video without showing your face covers avatar routes that pass.

What happened with Hollywood, and can you prompt copyrighted characters?

No, and the February 2026 dispute is why: the Motion Picture Association, whose members CNBC lists as Netflix, Paramount Skydance, Sony, Universal, Warner Bros Discovery and Disney, demanded ByteDance stop what it called "infringing activity", and Disney and Paramount Skydance sent cease-and-desist letters. ByteDance said it would strengthen safeguards, and a studio character in a prompt is now the fastest route to a blocked render.

The CNBC report of February 16, 2026 quotes MPA chairman Charles Rivkin: "In a single day, the Chinese AI service Seedance 2.0 has engaged in unauthorized use of U.S. copyrighted works on a massive scale." Disney's letter, as described by Axios and relayed by CNBC, alleged ByteDance had effectively pre-packaged the tool with a pirated library of copyrighted characters.

For a musician the category covers more than a cameo. A lyric video that recreates a famous animated film, a cover with a recognizable character in it, or a mascot that is plainly a studio character all sit under the seedance.ai "Dangerous or Illegal Activities" heading, which names IP rights. Describing the mood of a film is different from reproducing its characters. The music side is in our guide to whether AI cover songs are legal.

What happens when a render is flagged?

On seedance.ai, the policy describes real-time scanning of images and text before and after generation, human review of flagged content, and penalties that run from content removal to temporary or permanent suspension, reporting to authorities and legal action. Appeals go by email with a description of the content and the violation type. Credit handling on a block is not in the policy.

Because "bypassing of security filters" is a prohibited category, a workaround that gets a blocked prompt through puts the account at risk, not only the clip. And the privacy policy on ByteDance's Seed site, effective May 20, 2024, says personal information may be disclosed to "law enforcement, government authorities, and private parties" to enforce terms or deter "fraudulent, harmful, unauthorized, unethical or illegal activity".

How do musicians write prompts, album art and band clips that pass?

Describe, do not name. Build visuals from features, wardrobe, lighting and era instead of a star or a franchise, keep real faces to people who have agreed to appear, and leave explicit content out. Those three habits cover the celebrity, character and sensitive-material rules in every policy we read.

For a music video, translate the reference. "A 1970s soul singer under a single amber spotlight, sequined jacket, close on the hands" gives the model what a famous name would, without the name. To keep one performer across scenes, our guide to character consistency in AI video shows how to lock a described face rather than a borrowed one.

For album art, the risk is a recognizable character or celebrity in the frame, plus the "concealing AI origin to deceive" line if the art is passed off as a photograph. Your logo, typography and an invented figure are safe ground; our album cover art prompt guide starts there. For band photos, everyone in the image should have agreed to be animated, and the filter may still refuse a real face. A hard narrative scene may fit the seedance.ai artistic-expression exception, but that is granted by email in advance, not inferred from the prompt.

Before your next render, open the policy of the site on your receipt, rewrite any prompt that names a person or a franchise into a description, and test the concept at the shortest clip length your plan allows.

Frequently asked questions

Does Seedance allow my own face in a music video?+

Legally your face is yours, and the seedance.ai policy only asks that you hold rights to what you upload. The filters cannot verify that, and GlobalGPT's April 2026 guide says real human faces are blocked after the February 2026 safety update. Expect refusals on real photos and plan a stylized or described performer instead.

Why did my Seedance prompt fail without a clear reason?+

The most common triggers in the sources we read are a real person's name, a well-known film or game character, or sexual content. GlobalGPT's guide says a detected celebrity name or famous movie character makes the task fail. Rewrite the prompt as a physical description with wardrobe, lighting and era, then try again at a short length.

Is seedance.ai operated by ByteDance?+

The page does not say so. Its content policy refers to itself as Seedance AI Community, names no company, and its menu lists Kling, Google Veo, Wan and other third-party models alongside Seedance. ByteDance's own Seed site is at seed.bytedance.com, and its policy there names a different legal entity. Treat seedance.ai as an independent operator.

Can I make a lyric video in the style of a famous animated film?+

Style words such as painterly, hand-drawn or saturated pastel are not what the policies target. Reproducing the film's characters is. CNBC reported in February 2026 that studios accused Seedance 2.0 of unauthorized use of their characters, and ByteDance promised stronger safeguards. Describe the look, invent your own characters, and avoid franchise names.

Do blocked generations cost credits on Seedance sites?+

None of the four policies we read answers that. The seedance.ai content policy covers removal, suspension and appeals but not credits, and the Seedance25 privacy policy describes its filters without mentioning billing. Check the pricing or refund page of the site that charges you, and keep a screenshot of your balance before testing a risky prompt.

What did ByteDance promise after the Hollywood complaints?+

In a statement to CNBC on February 16, 2026, a spokesperson said ByteDance respects intellectual property rights, had heard the concerns about Seedance 2.0, and was taking steps to strengthen current safeguards against unauthorized use of intellectual property and likeness. CNBC did not report a timeline or technical detail, and the statement followed cease-and-desist letters from Disney and Paramount Skydance.
