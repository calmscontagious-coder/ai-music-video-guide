What do clients actually pay for an AI music video in 2026?

On Fiverr, AI music videos typically sell for $43 to $162 with an average of $93 across more than 2,500 listings (Fiverr category page, checked October 21, 2026). Off the marketplace, two 2026 rate reports put a beginner's short AI video at $150 to $400, mid-level work at $400 to $1,200, and specialist work at $1,200 to $3,000 or more. The gap between those two worlds is the whole story of pricing this service.

Direct clients think differently. Fueler's rate survey (May 28, 2026) lists beginner AI video at $150 to $400, mid-level at $400 to $1,200, and specialist work at $1,200 to $3,000 per video. Playcut's 2026 rate report lands in the same bands, $150 to $400, $400 to $1,000, and $1,000 to $3,500 or more for a 30 second deliverable.

Hourly work is the third lane. GigRadar's analysis of Upwork video editing (May 28, 2026) shows $20 to $40 an hour for beginners, $45 to $75 for mid-tier editors, $80 to $150 for experts, and $2,500 to $5,000 a month for an agency retainer covering eight long-form videos plus shorts.

Why do marketplace prices sit so far below direct-client rates?

Because a marketplace sells a file and a direct client buys an outcome. Playcut's 2026 report says it plainly: bidding platforms like Fiverr clear well below the direct-client ladder. Add the $39.99 full-song AI video (up to three minutes, 1080p, no watermark) that Kinetix listed on May 30, 2026, and the floor for "just make me a video" is set by a productized service, not by you. Kinetix notes that every paid tier includes human review.

A client who can get a watermark-free 1080p video for the price of a pizza will pay $600 only for what the software can't do: a concept tied to the song's structure, a lead who looks the same in the last chorus as in the first verse, cuts on the downbeat, and a human who answers the revision email.

What does one AI music video cost you to make?

Tool cost per song is small next to your hours: a 3-minute song rendered at 720p in Seeddance's Music to Video mode costs 180 × 4 = 720 credits, about $41 at the Pro plan's $0.057 per credit (720 × $0.057 = $41.04, rechecked October 25, 2026), and a 4-minute song plus a test excerpt is 960 + 60 = 1,020 credits, about $58. The real cost is the hours of direction, testing and revisions, and that time, not credits, sets your floor.

Seeddance (note the double-d domain, seeddance.video) is an independent platform that runs ByteDance's Seedance models alongside others; it is not ByteDance. Dreamina is ByteDance's own app. The Seeddance plan page, checked October 21, 2026, lists four tiers billed annually: Basic at $9.90 a month for 150 credits a month, Pro at $19.90 for 350, Max at $34.90 for 750, and Pro Max at $59.90 for 1,500.

Annual plans issue the whole year's credits up front, so Pro lands 4,200 on day one. Every paid plan includes a commercial license and watermark-free export, unused credits roll over while you stay subscribed, and monthly billing costs 30 percent more. The advertised 15 to 150 video renders a month are short clips at about 10 credits each, not songs.

Full songs are priced by the second. Music to Video bills 2 credits per second at 480p and 4 at 720p (Seeddance Music to Video page, checked October 21 and October 25, 2026). The page does not name the model behind the tool. A 3-minute song at 720p is 180 × 4 = 720 credits and a 4-minute song is 240 × 4 = 960, both more than two months of Pro's 350-credit allotment. A 30 second test at 480p adds 30 × 2 = 60. Building the same 3 minutes shot by shot on Seedance 2.5 costs far more: the Seedance 2.5 page bills 23 credits a second at 720p, so 180 × 23 = 4,140 credits, about $236 at $0.057, before retries.

Dreamina's September 23 to October 9, 2026 campaign listed Seedance 2.5 at $0.035 per generated second at 720p (90 percent off a first month) and $0.047 (40 percent off a first Super annual year), per its pricing page dated September 28, 2026. Both offers have ended. The page's earlier standing rate, Advanced billed annually in an August 10, 2026 snapshot, was $0.097 per second, or $2.91 for 30 seconds of 720p.

Our arithmetic from fal's published rates puts a finished minute nearer $35 to $55 once you count the takes you throw away; the math is in our Seedance credit cost breakdown.

How should you package and price the service?

The cleanest way to charge for AI music videos is three tiers that map onto the sourced bands: a visualizer or lyric video at the marketplace price, a directed full-song video in the $400 to $1,200 mid band, and a release package at $1,200 or more that adds cutdowns, usage rights, and a planning call. Put the middle tier where you want most clients to land.

Tier one: the visualizer

Audio-reactive loops, animated cover art, or a lyric video. Price it at the Fiverr band, $75 to $160, because that is what buyers compare it to. One or two hours, a short loop or a 480p render (360 credits for a 3-minute song), one revision round. Its job is to get a client into your inbox and a piece into your portfolio, not to pay rent.

Tier two: the directed music video

A full-song video with a written concept, a locked visual style, a consistent lead, camera moves tied to song sections, and two revision rounds. Price it inside the $400 to $1,200 band from both 2026 reports. Our math on a $600 job: about 1,020 credits for a 4-minute 720p render plus a 30 second 480p test, roughly $58 at Pro's per-credit rate. If direction, testing, editing and revisions take five hours, the effective rate lands near $108 an hour ($600 minus $58, divided by five), still inside GigRadar's $80 to $150 expert band for Upwork editors.

The first few jobs take longer than five hours. Price them at the band anyway; dropping to $150 to match your early speed trains the client to expect $150 forever.

Tier three: the release package

Everything in tier two, plus three vertical cutdowns, a captioned lyric version, a 15 second teaser with a spoken intro, and a contract line granting usage rights for paid promotion. Price it in the specialist band, $1,200 to $3,000. The extra deliverables take two to three hours in an editor; the rights line takes one sentence. Which add-ons raise the invoice without raising the render bill?

Rush delivery, usage rights, and derivative cuts. Fiverr's 2026 cost guide puts rush at 25 to 50 percent over base and 24-hour turnaround at 50 to 100 percent, and Fueler reports the same 25 to 50 percent norm. Rights and cutdowns cost you minutes and cost the client real money, because that is where the value sits.

  • Rush. Quote it as a percentage, not a favor. Your production time barely changes; the client pays for urgency, and both reports call that fair.
  • Usage rights. An artist posting to their own channels is one price; a brand running the video as a paid ad is another. Playcut frames the gap as a $57 gig versus a $600 licensed ad for the same 30 seconds. Write the rights into the quote.
  • Vertical cutdowns. Three 9:16 cuts from the master, each with its own hook in the first two seconds. In Descript that is trim, caption, export; Hobbyist at $16 a month billed annually ($24 monthly) includes 10 media hours and 1080p export, and Creator at $24 ($35 monthly) adds 4K and 30 media hours (Descript pricing, checked October 21, 2026).
  • Captioned lyric version. Never ask the video model to render lyrics; generated type warps within seconds. Add them in the editor.
  • Spoken intro or teaser narration. A 15 second teaser with a voice line sells the release on social. When is Seedance the right tool for paid work, and when isn't it?

Seedance is the right tool when the client wants a stylized or performance video driven by their own audio and the job sits in the mid-direct band, where a $41 to $58 render is a small line on a $400 to $1,200 invoice. It is the wrong tool for a full-length 720p render on a $93 marketplace gig, for convincing footage of a real, named performer, or for legible on-screen text.

What works for client jobs: Music to Video, whose page does not name the model it runs, renders the whole song in one pass, from 5 seconds to 10 minutes, with up to three reference portraits to keep one performer on screen and lip sync driven by the vocal track. Every paid plan on Seeddance adds a commercial license, watermark-free exports and credits that roll over.

Volume is the catch. Max's 750 monthly credits cover one 3-minute 720p song, and Pro's annual plan issues 4,200 credits up front, about five 3-minute 720p songs a year. Client work at volume means Max or Pro Max.

What doesn't work, and worth telling clients up front: the lead drifts unless the reference portraits are clear and front-facing; fast vocal lines can miss lip sync, so plan cutaways over them; on-screen text warps. Full-song renders burn credits fast, so Basic and Pro monthly allotments don't cover one song at 720p.

Clip-based endpoints work differently. Seedance 2.0 on fal stops at 15 seconds per generation (checked October 21, 2026), so a song built there is many clips stitched in an editor. Free-tier queues are slow, fine for testing and useless on a deadline.

If the client wants real footage of a real person, don't fake it. A one-person videographer at $500 to $1,500, per our 2026 tier guide, is the honest call for a band in a room. Sell AI video where a drawn or dreamed world serves the song better than a camera could.

How do you write a quote that holds?

Quote a fixed price per deliverable, name the included revision rounds, and state the usage rights in one line. Anchor the number to the market band for the tier, not to your render cost. Then list rush and extra rights as separate lines so the client can see what moves the price.

A tier two quote we would send: directed AI music video for one song up to four minutes, in an agreed illustrated style, concept approved in writing before rendering, $600. Includes two revision rounds and usage on the artist's own channels. Vertical cutdowns $90 each. Paid-ad usage rights quoted separately. Delivery in seven days; 48-hour rush adds 50 percent, the Fiverr guide's band, so the client gets a choice instead of a surprise.

Three habits keep that quote profitable when you charge for AI music videos by deliverable: approve the concept in writing before you spend a credit, probe at 480p before any full render, and charge for the third revision round. Each one stops a cost you would otherwise absorb yourself.

Finally, say the video is AI-made, in the quote and in the delivery note. It protects the client, who uploads under their own name, and it protects the price: you are selling direction and taste, and both are worth the mid band whether the frames came from a camera or a model.

Frequently asked questions

How much should a beginner charge for an AI music video?+

Start at the direct-client beginner band, $150 to $400 per video, as reported by Fueler in May 2026 and Playcut's 2026 rate report. On Fiverr the $93 average will anchor buyers, so use a $75 to $160 visualizer tier there for portfolio and leads, and move paying clients to the $400 to $1,200 directed tier as soon as you have three finished examples.

Is it fair to charge $600 for a video that cost about $60 in credits?+

Yes. Clients buy the outcome, not your render bill, and the 2026 rate reports we cite make the same point: a specialist and a beginner can use identical software and charge $3,000 and $150 for it. Your price covers the concept, the testing, the edit, the revisions and the responsibility. Tool cost is a line item, not the value.

Do I have to tell clients the video is AI-generated?+

Say it in the quote and again in the delivery note. The client uploads the video under their own name, so they carry the platform's labeling decision and they deserve to make it informed. Clients who find out later feel tricked and don't come back. Saying it up front also protects your price, because you are openly selling direction rather than a camera crew.

Can I sell AI music videos on a monthly retainer?+

Yes, for artists who release every month or small labels with a roster. Playcut's 2026 report puts entry retainers at $1,500 to $4,000 a month, and GigRadar found a $2,500 to $5,000 agency band on Upwork in May 2026 for eight long-form videos plus shorts. Scope the retainer to a fixed number of deliverables per month, never to unlimited requests.

What tools do I need before I take the first paid job?+

One video generator and one editor. On October 21, 2026, the reseller's Basic plan was $9.90 a month billed annually, but its 150 monthly credits cover only about 37 seconds of 720p Music to Video, so take a first paid full-song job on Pro's annual pool or on Max. A free editor handles captions. Add an AI voice generator's Starter plan ($6) for a spoken teaser and Descript Hobbyist ($16 annual) for heavier cutdown work.

How many revision rounds should the price include?+

Two. Include them in the fixed price, charge for the third, and get the concept approved in writing before you render. Most revision pain comes from rendering a full song on an untested prompt, so probe a 30 second excerpt at 480p first and fix the description there. That keeps revisions about taste, which is quick, instead of about broken renders, which isn't.
