Where is Seedance 2.5 live right now?

On October 21, 2026, Seedance 2.5 runs on five kinds of surface: ByteDance's own apps in China (Jimeng AI and Doubao Pro), Dreamina internationally including the US, the BytePlus ModelArk API for developers outside the US, API resellers such as Replicate and Atlas Cloud, and consumer tools such as Runway and Seeddance. A faster Flash variant is still listed by Dreamina as coming soon.

ByteDance's Seed team shipped the model on July 31, 2026, with Jimeng and Doubao first and API access promised through BytePlus. Here is where each route stands today, with the price basis each one publishes.

Can you use Seedance 2.5 in the United States?

Yes. As of October 21, 2026, a US musician has three routes: a Dreamina subscription, Atlas Cloud as an authorized US reseller, or a general API marketplace such as Replicate. The official BytePlus API still does not sell to US buyers, so a US developer goes through a reseller, and a US creator who wants a browser goes to Dreamina.

For most readers, Seedance 2.5 access means picking one of these three. If you want to click rather than code, Dreamina is the only official consumer app with 2.5, and the October offer makes the first month close to free. If you want per-second billing with no subscription and a US company on the invoice, Atlas Cloud sells the same model from $0.134 per second (about $0.30 per second at 720p, by its own $9 per 30-second quote), in a browser or over an API. If you already build with APIs, Replicate carries the model at the BytePlus launch rate. Our older guide to accessing Seedance from the US covered the workaround era; the Dreamina route makes most of it unnecessary, though Seeddance, the third-party platform in that guide, gives one free credit every 24 hours with no card, which is not enough for a single Seedance 2.5 clip (the shortest 480p job costs 48 credits).

What does Seedance 2.5 cost in October 2026?

Between roughly $0.007 and $1.52 per second of 720p video, depending on where you buy and the month: Dreamina's October first month is almost free, while its $15 renewal works out to about $0.35 a second by our arithmetic. At 720p, BytePlus and Replicate charge about $0.23 per second, Atlas Cloud about $0.30, and Runway 30 credits a second. Seeddance, the platform behind our affiliate link, is the priciest at about $0.92 to $1.52 per 720p second on annual plans.

Start with Dreamina. Its pricing page dated October 21, 2026 lists Seedance 2.5 at 37 credits per generated second at 720p, with or without a reference. Ten seconds use 370 credits and 30 seconds use 1,110. The $0.29 first month of US Basic includes 1,575 credits, so the whole month buys about 42 seconds of Seedance 2.5: one 30-second clip and one 12-second retry. Dreamina's own effective figure for that month is $0.0068 per second. The campaign runs October 21 to 30, 2026, and excludes anyone who took September's 90 percent offer.

Then read the renewal line. Basic renews at $15 a month for the same 1,575 credits. If the 37-credit charge holds, a 30-second clip at renewal costs about $10.57, which is roughly $0.35 per second. That is our arithmetic, not Dreamina's figure, and it is higher than every API route, though still below Seeddance. Annual plans change the math again: Basic is about $101 for the first year and renews at $167.99, per Dreamina's October FAQ. Two older Dreamina rates still circulate, $0.097 per second for annual subscribers from the August 3, 2026 release and $0.035 from the September offer; both are out of date.

The API routes are simpler. BytePlus launched Seedance 2.5 at $10.70 per million video tokens, which CellCog converts to about $0.1028 per second at 480p and $0.2312 at 720p; Replicate matches those rates. Atlas Cloud, the US reseller, quotes from $0.134 per second and about $9 for a 30-second 720p clip with sound, which works out to about $0.30 per second at 720p, the same rate CellCog found in Atlas's own calculator. Replicate works differently and charges $0.9676 per output second at 720p whenever a video is attached, about four times its plain rate (CellCog, October 6, 2026), so leave the camera-move clip out of the pack if you run on Replicate.

Seeddance (seeddance.video), a separate site that hosts several video models and the platform behind our affiliate link, issues one free credit every 24 hours to signed-in users (checked October 21, 2026), with watermarked output on the free tier. Paid plans on annual billing run $9.90 a month for 150 credits, $19.90 for 350 credits, $34.90 for 750, and $59.90 for 1,500, all with watermark-free exports and a commercial license. For Seedance 2.5 it charges 12 credits per second at 480p, 23 at 720p and 48 at 1080p (its Seedance 2.5 page, October 21, 2026), and it counts reference-video seconds at the same rate. A $9.90 Basic month on annual billing (150 credits) buys about six and a half seconds of 720p 2.5, and a 30-second 720p clip costs 690 credits, about $27.60 to $45.54 depending on the plan at annual-billing rates (up to about $68.66 on Basic billed monthly). What do 30-second clips and 50 references change for a music video?

They halve the seams and move character consistency from prompt luck to a reference pack. A 3:30 track needs seven 30-second clips instead of fourteen 15-second ones, and one generation can hold a full 16-bar verse at 128 BPM. The 50-asset brief lets the song itself drive the cut, because up to 10 audio clips go in with the pictures.

Start with the seam math, because a music video lives on its cuts. Seedance 2.0 topped out around 15 seconds per generation, so a 210-second track needed at least 14 clips and left 13 joins where a face, an outfit or the light could drift. At 30 seconds the count halves.

At 128 BPM in 4/4 a bar lasts 1.875 seconds, so 30 seconds is exactly 16 bars. One generation can now hold a slow push-in across an entire hook or a one-take performance verse, shots the 15-second ceiling could not physically contain. ByteDance's announcement also describes multi-round extension, which appends further 30-second passes, and Dreamina's launch release lists a Long Video Mode of up to three minutes, now a 180-second beta on the model page. We have not tested it. You will still cut on beats; the cuts just become choices instead of forced joins.

Seedance 2.5 accepts 30 images, 10 video clips and 10 audio clips in one brief, and on the API you address them in the prompt as [Image1], [Video1] and [Audio1], per Replicate's model page. For an artist that means one pack: several angles of the performer or an illustrated stand-in, wardrobe shots, a lighting frame, a camera-move clip, and the section of the song itself as the pacing reference. Dialogue written in quotation marks gets matching lip movement and voice, and audio can be switched off when you plan to lay the master over the picture. What does Seedance 2.5 still do badly?

It refuses photos of real people, its official resolution stops at 720p, and the cheap price is a first-month price. For a musician the face rule is the one that bites: on Dreamina and through the standard API you cannot hand it a photo of yourself and expect a performance video back.

The face rule dates to February 2026, when Disney and other studios sent ByteDance cease-and-desist letters within days of the Seedance 2.0 launch, and ByteDance responded by blocking uploads of real people. East.im's October 18, 2026 review reports the same block on 2.5 through the API, where a normal portrait fails before generation with the error InputImageSensitiveContentDetected.PrivacyInformation. What still works: text that describes a performer, illustrated or stylized characters, objects, places and products. If your video needs your own face, plan a stylized stand-in or a different tool. The one official exception is outside the US: BytePlus says vetted ModelArk clients can verify and authorize a real person's likeness, their own included, and it offers a library of over 10,000 virtual humans (BytePlus, August 6, 2026). Seeddance's 2.5 page says real-person reference images are supported; we have not tested that claim, so run a short 480p test before you plan a shoot around it.

Resolution is the second trap. BytePlus documents 480p and 720p for 2.5, at 24 frames per second per CellCog. Dreamina's model page markets "clean 4K", Atlas Cloud sells native 1080p and Seeddance lists 1080p, so the number you see depends on where you render, and the higher tiers on resellers are provider-side additions rather than documented model output. Dreamina also adds a visible AI label, an invisible watermark and C2PA Content Credentials to everything it renders, per East.im, which is fine for a lyric video and a problem for a clean master.

Should you wait for Seedance 2.5 or make the video now?

Make it now, and draft cheap. On Dreamina a second of Seedance 2.5 costs 37 credits against 17 for Seedance 2.0 Pro and 8 for Fast, so block out the video on the cheap tier, then spend 2.5 credits only on the shots that need 30 unbroken seconds.

The workflow we use has not changed with the version number. Beat-map the track, storyboard the shots, build the reference pack, and draft every shot on the cheapest engine you can reach. On Dreamina that is Seedance 2.0 Fast at 8 credits a second. Seeddance's 2.5 page lists 4 to 30 seconds and 1080p, but at 23 credits per 720p second it is a finishing tool, not a drafting one. Then move the two or three shots that earn a one-take, the chorus push-in or the performance verse, to Seedance 2.5 on whichever route fits your country. For a US reader in October 2026 that is a $0.29 Dreamina month for about 42 seconds of finished 2.5 video, or Atlas Cloud at about $9 a clip with no plan attached.

The only question left is which shots deserve the expensive seconds.
