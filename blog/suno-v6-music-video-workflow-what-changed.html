Suno v6 and Your Music Video Workflow: What Actually Changed

By the AI Music Video Guide team · Updated October 21, 2026 · 12 min read

This post contains affiliate links — if you sign up through them we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you (disclosure). Researched and edited for accuracy with AI assistance.

Quick answer: Suno v6 changes how you make the song, not how you make the video. v6 accepts video as an input reference but outputs audio only, so the video step still lives in a tool like Seedance. What did change: every older Suno model was retired on September 9, 2026, and downloads are now capped.

We rebuilt our own song-to-video routine around Suno v6 in the weeks after launch. This is what moved, what didn't, and what it costs in October 2026. Every price and date below comes from a source we opened this week, linked in the text or listed at the end.

What did Suno change on September 9, 2026?

On September 9, 2026, Suno released v6 and retired every model before it. v6 is three models: v6 and v6-wild for Pro and Premier subscribers, and v6-mini for everyone, including free accounts. Songs you made on older models stay in your library, but you can't generate new takes on those models anymore.

Suno's launch post, written by its CEO, pitches v6 at people who know what they want and v6-wild at exploration, with less predictable output. The v6 FAQ on Suno's help centre puts the retirement in the past tense: "All models prior to v6 have been retired."

The useful details sit in that FAQ rather than the launch post:

  • Credit cost didn't change. One generation still makes two songs for 10 credits, the same as v5.5.
  • Max Mode is an optional toggle that spends more credits per generation. Suno recommends it for songs over two minutes, covers that need to stay close to the source, and keeping vocals consistent across a whole track.
  • Custom models you built on v5.5 were upgraded to run on v6 automatically. Songs made with the old custom model are unaffected.
  • A Variety slider rewrites your style tags unless you set it to 0.

The last point is easy to miss. If your style prompt is precise, drop Variety to 0 before you spend credits.

Does Suno v6 make the video too?

No, Suno v6 accepts video as an input, not as an output. Neither the September 9 announcement nor the v6 FAQ mentions generating video. The one video-shaped feature points the other way: you can feed in a clip, an image or a voice memo and get a song back.

In practice you can score footage you already shot, or hand v6 a mood board and a journal entry and let it write to that. It does not mean Suno will hand you a finished music video. What Suno itself can and can't do with video is covered in Can Suno make music videos?

So the pipeline is the same shape it was under v5.5: song in Suno, visuals in a video generator, assembly in an editor. What changed is the economics around the download, which is the next section.

Why do the new download limits matter more than the model?

Because a music video needs the downloaded audio file, and downloads are now rationed. Since September 3, 2026, Free accounts opened before that date get up to 7 lifetime trial downloads for personal use, newer Free accounts only get occasional trial downloads, Pro gets 20 a month and Premier gets 60 a month. Commercial rights attach to songs you download on a paid plan.

Suno announced the caps in a downloads policy post dated August 10, 2026, effective September 3. Four details from that post shape the video workflow:

  • Limits apply to every download from September 3 onward, including songs created before that date.
  • Premier subscribers working inside Suno Studio have no download limit for that workflow.
  • Trial downloads on the Free plan are for personal use only. Commercial rights apply to songs downloaded on paid plans.
  • Extra downloads can be purchased past the cap. Suno's own post doesn't list a price; the launch-week roundup we cite below reports $2.99 per extra download, a figure we could not confirm on any Suno page.

The practical effect: pick your take before you export. The old habit of downloading eight variants and deciding in the editor now costs eight of your twenty. For a daily-upload channel, twenty won't last, and Premier's Studio exemption becomes the real reason to pay $24 a month.

Why are people angry, and does it change your plan?

The anger is mostly about retired models and rationed downloads, not the new sound. Within twelve hours of launch, the most upvoted post on r/SunoAI was a cancellation notice that reached 351 points and 495 comments, according to a launch-week roundup updated September 25, 2026.

Gearnews reported in September 2026 that complaints about Suno v6's flat mixes and muffled vocals piled up on the subreddit, with one widely shared post calling the update unusable. The same roundup notes that even critics concede v6 sounds cleaner than v5.5, that pop, rock and folk users are broadly fine, and that metal and drum and bass users are not. Several users report that v6-wild carries more of the older models' character.

Our read after a few weeks: some of this is taste, and some is a real change in what the model was trained on. We now generate the final take on v6 and the first ideas on v6-wild, and that split works for us. If you downloaded songs made on the old models, back those files up: they are the only copies of that sound you'll get.

On September 18, 2026, Sony Music and Universal Music Group filed a 45-page complaint against Suno in federal court in Massachusetts, reported by Variety the same day. The labels name 60,202 recordings and argue that training v6 on the outputs of earlier Suno models carries the earlier infringement forward. Suno called the claims flawed on the facts and the law and said v6 was trained on licensed partner content plus community creations and preference signals. A RouteNote explainer from September 2026 puts the theoretical statutory exposure at up to $150,000 per work if infringement is found to be willful. Nothing has been ruled, and the original June 2024 case is still running. It's a risk to weigh, not a verdict.

What does v6 add that helps a music video?

Two features: section edits and single-lyric swaps you ask for in plain language. Suno has let Pro and Premier users replace a section since October 10, 2024, so the ability isn't new; v6 turns it into a one-sentence instruction instead of a manual selection.

Before v6, Replace Section (Suno release note, October 10, 2024) had you highlight a 10 to 30 second region, rewrite its lyrics and pick one of two new takes. v6's FAQ describes the same job as an instruction: change one section while preserving the rest, or change one word or line while leaving everything else intact. That makes a late fix, like a mispronounced name after the visuals are done, faster to try. Neither Suno page promises the timing holds to the frame, so line the edited section up against your cut before you export.

Two more items worth knowing for video work:

  • Mashups from multiple sources in one request let you pull vocals from one song and drums from another. Useful for a remix cut of a video you've already released.
  • Image and video inputs let you brief the song from the storyboard instead of the other way round. Drop the storyboard frames in, ask for the song that fits them, then shoot to that song.

The catch on both is cost. The FAQ says many image and video inputs raise the credit price of a generation, and Max Mode costs more again. Budget a 10-credit generation as the floor, not the norm, when you're feeding it visuals.

How do we turn a Suno v6 song into a video right now?

Generate on v6 with Max Mode for anything over two minutes, download once, then build the visuals in Seedance using the downloaded audio as a reference. Seedance 2.0 makes clips up to 15 seconds; Seedance 2.5 reaches 30 seconds per generation and can extend a clip twice.

The routine we use today:

  1. Write the brief first: tempo, structure, who sings, what the video will show. Generate two takes on v6 and one on v6-wild. Set Variety to 0 if the style prompt is specific.
  2. Fix weak sections with a plain-language edit rather than a fresh generation. Swap single lyrics the same way.
  3. Download once. That's one of your 20 on Pro. Store the file somewhere you won't lose it.
  4. Open Seedance and load a slice of the track as reference audio. ByteDance's February 12, 2026 launch post says Seedance 2.0 takes up to 9 images, 3 video clips and 3 audio clips per generation, plus text, and outputs multi-shot video with sound up to 15 seconds.
  5. Generate shot by shot in 15-second slices, keeping one image set for character consistency. Cut to the beat in your editor.

Where Seedance is strong: multi-shot output in one pass, synchronized sound, and holding a character across shots when you reuse the same reference images. Where it's weak, and we'd rather you hear it from us: it doesn't always follow the reference audio, and sung lip-sync is hit or miss, especially on fast lines. Our fixes are in Seedance not following reference audio? 3 fixes that work. A three-minute song is also at least twelve shots on 2.0, which is twelve chances for a continuity slip. Seedance 2.5 halves that count, at a higher per-second price.

Can you still release a v6 song through DistroKid?

Yes, for now. DistroKid's help centre says it accepts music made with AI tools if you own 100 percent of the rights, don't mimic anyone's voice or identity, don't mass-upload generic content to game algorithms, and don't infringe. For a Suno song that means a permitted download on a paid plan; free-plan trial downloads carry no commercial rights.

DistroKid also asks at upload whether any part of the track was AI-generated. Its AI credits article lists the options: the lyrics, the music, all of the audio, or part of the audio. If you pick all of the audio, it asks whether the artist name is a human or an AI persona, because streaming services need that to display the credit.

Two cautions. DistroKid notes that streaming services can still reject or remove a release that doesn't meet their guidelines, so passing DistroKid's form isn't the same as passing Spotify's.

DistroKid is also in a fight of its own. Universal sued it in Delaware federal court on September 15, 2026, alleging it floods streaming services with AI-generated slop (Los Angeles Times), and DistroKid confirmed to Billboard on October 12, 2026 that it had removed a very small number of recordings in response. Its written AI policy hasn't changed as of October 21, 2026. If it does, our guide to which platforms accept AI music is where we'll record it.

Should you leave Suno over v6?

Not on the sound alone. Leave if your release plan depended on a retired model's voice, or if backing your catalog with a company facing two label lawsuits is more risk than you want. Stay if Suno v6 or v6-wild gives you a take you'd put your name on.

The decision rule we'd use:

  • If your genre is pop, rock, folk or ambient, test v6 for a week before deciding. Most reports we read in that lane are fine.
  • If you're in metal or drum and bass, try v6-wild first and keep expectations low.
  • If a persona from v4.5 or v5.5 was your artist's whole identity, that voice is gone. Decide whether you're rebuilding it or retiring it.
  • If you release more than twenty songs a month, Premier's Studio download exemption is the plan, not Pro.

Frequently asked questions

Does Suno v6 cost more credits than v5.5 did?+

No. Suno's v6 FAQ says a generation still produces two songs for 10 credits, the same as before. Two things raise the price: feeding the prompt many images or video clips, and turning on Max Mode, which Suno recommends for songs over two minutes and for covers that must stay close to the source.

Can I still use a song I made on Suno v4.5 or v5?+

Yes. Songs made on retired models stay in your library and you can still play, share, remaster and cover them, and download them against your monthly cap. What you can't do is generate a new take on the old model. Any new iteration runs on v6, so the downloaded file is the only copy of that older sound you'll have.

Does Suno v6 generate music videos?+

No. v6 can take a video, an image or a voice memo as an input and write a song to match, but it outputs audio only. Neither the launch post nor the v6 FAQ mentions video output. You still need a separate video generator such as Seedance and an editor to cut the shots to the track.

How many downloads do I get on Suno Pro after September 3, 2026?+

Twenty per month on Pro, sixty per month on Premier, and up to seven lifetime trial downloads on Free accounts opened before that date, while newer Free accounts only get occasional trials. The caps apply to every download from September 3, 2026 onward, including older songs. Premier subscribers working inside Suno Studio aren't limited, and extra downloads can be bought once you pass your allowance.

Is Seedance the only video tool that works with a Suno song?+

No. Any generator that accepts an audio reference or lets you cut to a track will work, and plenty of people pair Suno with Kling, Veo or Runway. We use Seedance because its multi-shot output and synchronized sound suit music videos, but it doesn't always follow reference audio and sung lip-sync is inconsistent.

Will DistroKid reject a Suno v6 song?+

Not for being AI-made. DistroKid accepts AI music if you own all the rights, don't impersonate anyone, don't spam uploads and don't infringe. You'll need a permitted download from a paid Suno plan, and you'll fill in DistroKid's AI credits form at upload. DistroKid and streaming services can still remove releases; DistroKid pulled a small number after Universal sued it in September 2026.
