Hailuo AI's Credit Problems: 3-Day Expiry, Failed Generations, and Where Burned Users Go

By the AI Music Video Guide team · Updated August 11, 2026 · 7 min read

This post contains affiliate links — if you sign up through them we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you (disclosure). Researched and edited for accuracy with AI assistance.

Quick answer: Hailuo AI's video model is genuinely good — it has ranked No. 1 for physics simulation on WorldModelBench and renders clips in 30–90 seconds — but the consumer platform around it holds a 1.4/5 on Trustpilot, with complaints clustering on signup credits that expire in 3 days, credits consumed by failed or off-prompt generations, and billing that continues after cancellation attempts. Users who picked Hailuo for motion quality mostly migrate to Kling, Seedance 2.0, or Wan, all of which run on Seedance's single credit pool with no short-fuse expiry.

The split verdict: love the model, distrust the platform

Hailuo AI is the consumer video product of MiniMax, the Shanghai AI company whose Hailuo 02 model went viral in mid-2025 for physics so convincing that "cat diving" clips flooded TikTok. The model deserves its reputation. According to Amrytt's hands-on June 2026 review, Hailuo generates faster than anything in its category (30–90 seconds per clip), ranked No. 1 for physics simulation on WorldModelBench, and leads for character and anime animation — while scoring just 2.2/5 for support and carrying a bottom-tier Trustpilot rating driven by "billing & refund failures."

That split is the entire story. On Trustpilot, Hailuo's profile sits at 1.4 out of 5 across roughly 90 reviews at the time of writing (August 2026), and the complaints read like a checklist of billing pain: frustration with the subscription process, unexpected charges, difficulty obtaining refunds, and a credits system reviewers describe as stacked against them. Recurring reports include duplicate charges landing on accounts that showed no active subscription and cancellation attempts that stretch into months of unanswered support tickets. For independent musicians budgeting $10–25 a month for video, this is exactly the failure mode that hurts most: the tool works, the billing doesn't.

The complaint audit: where your credits actually go

Every complaint category is verifiable against Hailuo's own published terms.

Signup credits expire in 3 days. Hailuo's official payment policy states that welcome credits for accounts registered after June 18, 2025 "will expire three days after being granted." Source: Hailuo AI Video Subscription Service Terms. That's roughly 200 credits on a 72-hour fuse — if you sign up on a busy Tuesday, your trial is gone before the weekend you planned to use it.

Subscription credits zero out monthly. The same policy states membership credits "are valid for one month" and "unused credits do not roll over to the next month." Only one-time purchased credit packs (sold at $1 per 70 credits) get a long runway — valid through December 31 of the second calendar year after purchase.

Failed generations: the paper policy and the lived reality diverge. On paper, Hailuo's terms say that when "a video generation failure" occurs or content fails review, "the system shall automatically refund the credits consumed." In practice, Amrytt's tested review lists "credits drain fast; failed generations still consume them" as a core con, and Trustpilot reviewers repeatedly describe the sharper version of the problem: a render that completes but ignores your prompt is a successful generation as far as billing is concerned, so the credits are gone either way. Content moderation adds another drain — reviewers describe runs of content-violation rejections on prompts other platforms render without issue. This isn't unique to Hailuo — we documented the same pattern on Kling in our failed-generation credits breakdown — but Hailuo's monthly expiry means you can't even bank credits while you learn what the moderation layer tolerates.

Billing continues after cancellation attempts. The policy itself is strict: cancellation "takes effect only at the start of the following billing cycle" and "no refunds shall be issued for payments already made." Trustpilot's complaint pattern goes beyond the policy — reviewers describe charges landing after written cancellation confirmations, trials that billed early, and refund requests routed into support forms that never resolve.

Current pricing, for context: According to CostBench's pricing tracker (last verified July 1, 2026), Hailuo runs five plans — Free, Standard at $7.99/month (1,000 credits), Pro at $24.99 (4,500), Master at $63.99 (10,000), and Max at $199.99 (20,000) — and contracts auto-renew. Treat those as promotional checkout rates rather than list prices: Hailuo's own subscription terms (the payment policy linked above) list Standard at $14.99 and Pro at $54.99 for the same credit allotments, so check the number on the checkout page before subscribing — whatever rate you sign at is the one that auto-renews.

If you're staying: how to protect yourself

The model is good enough that staying is a defensible choice. Protect yourself on four fronts:

  • Pay with a virtual card. Given the documented pattern of post-cancellation charges, use a virtual card you can freeze, not your main debit card. Never store a card for a service you're only trialing.
  • Time your signup. The 3-day welcome-credit fuse means you should register only when you have a clear 72-hour window to actually generate. Prepare prompts and reference images before you create the account.
  • Spend down before renewal. Subscription credits vanish at cycle end. Calendar a reminder two days before your billing date and burn the balance on b-roll or experiments. If you buy extra credits, prefer the one-time packs — their multi-year validity beats the monthly zero-out.
  • Reduce failure burn. Keep prompts concrete and physical rather than abstract, avoid anything moderation might read as a celebrity or trademarked character, test new prompt styles at the shortest clip length before committing credits to longer renders, and favor image-to-video with a clean reference frame — it fails less than pure text-to-video.

Cancel and refund walkthrough

If you subscribed on the web: log in at hailuoai.video, open your profile icon, go to account settings, find the subscription/billing section, and cancel — the steps are confirmed in Subscribed.fyi's cancellation guide. If you subscribed through the iOS or Android app, cancel in your Apple or Google subscription settings instead — the website cannot cancel an app-store subscription.

Then do three things the burned reviewers wish they had done: screenshot the cancellation confirmation with a visible date, check your statement on the next two billing dates, and if a charge lands anyway, dispute it with your card issuer immediately using those screenshots — waiting on the support-form loop is the pattern that turns one bad month into three. Under Hailuo's terms you should not expect a refund for the current cycle; the realistic goal is stopping future charges.
